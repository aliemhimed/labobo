Dosage Compensation, X-Inactivation and Barr Bodies

Why females switch off one X chromosome in every cell, how to count Barr bodies, why some genes escape and still matter in Turner and Klinefelter syndromes, the Lyon hypothesis and mosaicism, and the molecular switch (XIST, TSIX, XITE).

Labobo topic: MG Wk 6: X-Inactivation & Barr Bodies

In 60 seconds

  • Dosage compensation: one X is inactivated in every female somatic cell so both sexes express one active X.
  • Happens around the blastocyst stage; choice is random but then permanent in all daughter cells (Lyon, 1961).
  • Barr bodies = number of X chromosomes − 1 (XX → 1, XY → 0, 45,X → 0, XXY → 1, XXX → 2).
  • Barr body = condensed heterochromatin (histone deacetylation, promoter DNA methylation) at the nuclear membrane; found by Barr and Bertram, 1949.
  • About 15% of X genes escape inactivation and 10% vary → explains symptoms in Turner, Klinefelter and triple X.
  • Females are mosaics: calico/tortoiseshell cats, patchy colour vision in carriers.
  • XIST lncRNA coats the X to be silenced (recruits PRC1/PRC2); TSIX (antisense) blocks XIST on the active X; XITE boosts TSIX.

Dosage compensation

  • The X carries hundreds of essential genes unrelated to sex (metabolism, development, cell function), needed in both sexes.
  • If both Xs worked in females, every X gene would be expressed at double dose compared with males, upsetting the balance with autosomes and causing developmental problems.
  • Dosage compensation equalises X gene products: one X is inactivated in every female somatic cell.
  • This X-chromosome inactivation (XCI), also called Lyonization, happens early, around the blastocyst stage. The inactive X condenses into the Barr body.

Barr bodies

  • Discovered in 1949 by Canadian researchers Murray L. Barr and Ewart G. Bertram: a small dark body at the edge of the nucleus in nerve cells of female cats, not males.
  • Later found in many female tissues, including human buccal (cheek) epithelial cells. Also called the sex chromatin body.
  • A Barr body is an inactivated X: highly condensed, transcriptionally silent, usually pressed against the inner nuclear membrane.

How it is kept silent

  • Histone deacetylation → chromatin compacts.
  • DNA methylation of promoters → transcription is permanently suppressed.

The Barr body is the visible evidence of dosage compensation; it is facultative heterochromatin (heterochromatin that is silent in one cell type or chromosome copy but not the other).

Counting Barr bodies

Number of Barr bodies = total number of X chromosomes − 1. Only one X per cell stays active, whatever the karyotype.

KaryotypeConditionBarr bodies
46,XXNormal female1
46,XYNormal male0
45,XTurner syndrome0

47,XXXTriple X2

Counting Barr bodies helps in cytogenetic sex determination and in diagnosing sex chromosome abnormalities.

Genes that escape inactivation

  • Not every gene on the inactive X is switched off: about 15% stay active and about 10% show variable inactivation.
  • Most escape genes are in the pseudoautosomal regions (PARs) at the tips of the X.

This explains why an abnormal number of X chromosomes still causes symptoms even though extra Xs are inactivated:

ConditionWhy symptoms appear
Turner (45,X)Missing active copies of escape genes → growth and ovarian defects
Klinefelter (47,XXY), triple X (47,XXX)Extra active copies of escape genes → mild cognitive or physical abnormalities

The Lyon hypothesis and mosaicism

Proposed in 1961 by British geneticist Mary F. Lyon, from mouse coat patterns and human X-linked traits:

  1. One X is inactivated early in development (around the blastocyst stage).
  2. Which X (maternal or paternal) is chosen at random in each cell.
  3. Once chosen, the same X stays inactive in all descendant cells (a clonal pattern).

So every female is a mosaic: some cells express the maternal X, others the paternal X.

Evidence

  • Mouse coat colour: a heterozygous female XᴮXᴼ (black/orange alleles on X). Cells that silence Xᴮ show orange; cells that silence Xᴼ show black. Clones of cells form patches → a variegated, mottled coat, as in calico and tortoiseshell cats.
  • Males have one X, so only one colour: no patches.
  • Humans: women heterozygous for red–green colour blindness have retinal cells with the normal allele and others with the mutant allele. Vision seems normal, but careful testing shows a patchwork of colour perception.
In the clinic

Because inactivation is random, a female carrier of an X-linked disease (e.g. hemophilia) may occasionally show symptoms if, by chance, most of her cells silenced the normal X (“skewed” inactivation).

The X-inactivation centre: XIST, TSIX, XITE

Each X has an X-inactivation centre (Xic), a region with regulatory genes and non-coding RNAs (no proteins). It works like a switchboard: it counts the X chromosomes and makes sure only one stays active, inactivating all others (as in normal females or Klinefelter males).

ElementWhat it isRole
XIST (X-inactive specific transcript)Long non-coding RNA (not translated)Expressed only from the X to be silenced; spreads along that same chromosome (in cis) like a blanket; recruits Polycomb complexes PRC1 and PRC2, which modify histones, condense chromatin and silence almost all its genes
TSIXGene transcribed in the opposite (antisense) direction to XISTNegative regulator of XIST: its RNA binds the XIST locus and blocks it, so the X expressing TSIX stays active
XITE (X-inactivation intergenic transcription elements)Regulatory DNA near XIST and TSIXEnhances TSIX, stabilising the choice of the active X
Tug of war

The X that wins XIST expression is inactivated; the X that keeps TSIX stays active. TSIX is XIST spelled backwards, because it is read backwards.

Watch out

Watch out: where is the Xic?

Slide 10 places the X-inactivation centre on the short arm “close to the centromere”. The human Xic (with XIST) is on the long arm, Xq13, near the centromere. If asked, choose Xq13.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • A Klinefelter male has a Barr body.
  • Inactivation is random, not always the paternal X.
  • XIST RNA acts in cis (on its own chromosome).
  • XIST is not translated; it works as RNA.
  • Escape genes explain why X aneuploidies still cause symptoms.

Key terms

Dosage compensation
Equalising X gene products between sexes.
X inactivation / Lyonization
Silencing of one X in female cells.
Barr body
Condensed inactive X at the nuclear membrane.
Heterochromatin
Condensed, transcriptionally silent chromatin.
Mosaic
Individual with cell populations expressing different alleles.
Xic
X-inactivation centre controlling which X is silenced.
XIST
lncRNA that coats and silences the inactive X.
TSIX
Antisense gene that blocks XIST.
XITE
Enhancer region boosting TSIX.
Polycomb complexes
PRC1/PRC2 proteins that modify histones to silence genes.
Escape genes
X genes that stay active on the inactive X.
