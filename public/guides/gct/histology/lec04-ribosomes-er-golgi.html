Ribosomes, Endoplasmic Reticulum and Golgi Apparatus

The cytoplasm, ribosomes and polysomes, rough ER (signal sequence, SRP, ERAD), smooth ER (lipids, detoxification, calcium) and the Golgi apparatus (cis and trans faces, COP coats, processing).

Labobo topic: Histo Lec 4: Ribosomes, ER & Golgi

In 60 seconds

  • Cytoplasm holds organelles (membranous, e.g. mitochondria, and non-membranous, e.g. ribosomes), organised by the cytoskeleton. The cytosol has enzymes such as those of glycolysis.
  • Ribosomes: small + large subunit of rRNA + protein; rRNA catalyses peptide bonds. Assembled in the nucleus; several on one mRNA = polysome. RNA makes them basophilic.
  • Free polysomes → proteins for the cytosol and imported organelles. ER-bound → proteins for the ER, Golgi, membrane, lysosomes, secretion.
  • RER: flat cisternae with ribosomes, basophilic; big in pancreas, fibroblasts, plasma cells. Signal sequence + SRP bring the ribosome to the ER. ERAD: misfolded → ubiquitin → proteasome.
  • SER: no ribosomes, tubular; lipid and steroid synthesis, detoxification (cytochrome P450, liver), Ca²⁺ storage (sarcoplasmic reticulum in muscle).
  • Golgi: near the nucleus; cis face receives from RER, trans face ships out. COPII forward, COPI backward (retrograde), Rab proteins target vesicles. Glycosylation, sulfation, phosphorylation, limited proteolysis.

Learning objectives

  • describe the cytoplasm and the structure and assembly of ribosomes;
  • explain why protein-making cells are basophilic;
  • describe RER structure, the signal hypothesis and ERAD;
  • list SER functions with examples;
  • describe Golgi structure, vesicle traffic and processing.

Cytoplasm

  • Inside the cell membrane, the cytoplasm (cytosol) surrounds and supports the organelles.
  • Organelles are either membranous (e.g. mitochondria) or non-membranous protein complexes (e.g. ribosomes).
  • The cytoskeleton organises organelles and controls cell shape and movement.
  • The cytosol contains many enzymes, including those of glycolysis; O2, CO2, ions and metabolites diffuse through it to the organelles that use or make them.

Ribosomes

  • Molecular machines that join amino acids into polypeptides, reading mRNA with the help of tRNA.
  • Two subunits, each of rRNA + proteins: a small subunit and a large one (more rRNA and protein).
  • rRNA gives structure, positions tRNA, and catalyses the peptide bond (a ribozyme). Proteins stabilise the catalytic core.
  1. Ribosomal proteins are made in the cytoplasm.
  2. They are imported into the nucleus (nucleolus) and combined with rRNA into subunits.
  3. Subunits return to the cytoplasm to make proteins.
  4. Several ribosomes on one mRNA = a polyribosome (polysome).
  • RNA’s phosphate groups make ribosome-rich cytoplasm stain intensely basophilic (blue with hematoxylin).
  • Free polysomes make proteins for the cytosol or for import into organelles (nucleus, mitochondria, peroxisomes).
  • ER-bound ribosomes make proteins for the ER, Golgi, plasma membrane, lysosomes and secretory vesicles.
  • Folding is helped by chaperones; misfolded proteins are destroyed by proteasomes.

The endoplasmic reticulum

  • A network of membranes extending from the nuclear envelope, forming connected channels and sacs called cisternae.
  • Its membrane area is much larger than the plasma membrane’s.
  • Central to making proteins and lipids.
  • Two types, distinguished by whether polyribosomes are attached.

Rough ER (RER)

  • Prominent in cells that secrete protein: pancreatic acinar cells, fibroblasts (collagen), plasma cells (antibodies).
  • Flattened sacs in stacks of cisternae, with polyribosomes on the cytosolic surface → basophilic.
  • Makes membrane proteins, secreted proteins and proteins for membranous organelles.
  • Starts glycosylation of glycoproteins and other post-translational modifications.

Signal sequence and SRP

  1. Translation starts in the cytosol.
  2. Proteins for the ER begin with a signal sequence.
  3. The signal-recognition particle (SRP) binds it and pauses translation.
  4. The complex docks on the ER membrane; translation resumes and the protein enters the RER lumen.
  5. The signal sequence is cut off; the protein folds with chaperones and is modified.

Quality control: ERAD

  • ER-associated degradation (ERAD) makes sure only correctly folded proteins move on.
  • Misfolded proteins are tagged with ubiquitin and destroyed by proteasomes.
  • RER products go to lysosomes, secretory vesicles (exocytosis) or become integral membrane proteins. These destinations give cell types their histological look (e.g. granules in secretory cells).

Smooth ER (SER)

  • No ribosomes; continuous with the RER but usually less abundant and less basophilic (paler).
  • Cisternae are tubular or saclike and interconnected (RER cisternae are flattened).

Golgi apparatus

  • Also called the Golgi complex: a dynamic organelle that modifies, packages and sorts proteins from the RER.
  • Made of smooth membrane saccules (flattened or vesicular), usually near the nucleus.
  • Two faces:
    • cis face (entry, toward the ER): receives transport vesicles from the RER;
    • trans face (exit): packs modified proteins into larger vesicles for other organelles or exocytosis.

Golgi traffic and processing

ProteinRole
COPIIForward (anterograde) movement, ER → cis Golgi

Rab proteins, receptors, fusion proteinsGuide vesicles to the correct destination and make them fuse
  • Enzymes in the cis, medial and trans cisternae modify proteins by:
    • glycosylation (adding/trimming sugars);
    • sulfation;
    • phosphorylation (e.g. mannose-6-phosphate on lysosomal enzymes);
    • limited proteolysis (cutting precursors to active forms).
  • The Golgi also concentrates, stores and packages secretory products, sorting them to lysosomes or secretory vesicles.
Key idea: the secretory path

Ribosome → RER lumen → (COPII vesicle) → cis Golgi → medial → trans Golgi → lysosome, membrane or secretory granule → exocytosis.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • The catalyst for the peptide bond is rRNA, not protein.
  • Ribosomal proteins are made in the cytoplasm but assembled in the nucleus.
  • SRP pauses translation; it does not end it.
  • ERAD destroys proteins with ubiquitin + proteasome, not lysosomes.
  • Sarcoplasmic reticulum = SER of muscle.
  • COPII forward, COPI backward (“two goes to, one comes back”).

Key terms

Polysome
Several ribosomes on one mRNA.
Cisterna
Flattened membrane sac of ER or Golgi.
Signal sequence
Peptide directing a protein to the ER.
SRP
Signal-recognition particle.
ERAD
ER-associated degradation of misfolded proteins.
Proteasome
Complex that destroys ubiquitin-tagged proteins.
Sarcoplasmic reticulum
Muscle SER that stores Ca²⁺.
Cis / trans face
Golgi entry / exit side.
COPI / COPII
Coats for backward / forward vesicles.
Rab
Proteins that target vesicles.
