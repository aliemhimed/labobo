Fructose, Galactose and Lactose Metabolism

How fructose and galactose enter glycolysis, how glucose becomes sorbitol and why that matters in diabetes, how lactose is made, and the inherited disorders: essential fructosuria, hereditary fructose intolerance, galactosemia and lactose intolerance.

Labobo topic: Biochem Lec 5: Monosaccharide and Disaccharide Metabolism

In 60 seconds

  • Fructose (~10% of Western calories, mostly from sucrose) is phosphorylated by fructokinase to fructose 1-phosphate, which aldolase B splits to DHAP + glyceraldehyde. This skips PFK-1, so fructose is used faster than glucose.
  • Essential fructosuria (fructokinase) is benign; hereditary fructose intolerance (aldolase B, 1:20,000) traps phosphate as fructose 1-P → low ATP, hypoglycemia, vomiting, hyperuricemia, lactic acidemia, liver failure.
  • Aldose reductase turns glucose into sorbitol (with NADPH); where sorbitol dehydrogenase is low, sorbitol is trapped → osmotic damage in diabetes (cataract, neuropathy, nephropathy, retinopathy).
  • Galactose: galactokinase → galactose 1-P → GALT (with UDP-glucose) → UDP-galactose → 4-epimerase → UDP-glucose.
  • Classic galactosemia = GALT deficiency (severe, newborn screening); galactokinase or epimerase deficiency is milder but causes cataracts (galactitol).
  • Lactose synthase = protein A (galactosyltransferase) + protein B (α-lactalbumin, induced by prolactin), only in the lactating breast.

Fructose metabolism

Fructose supplies about 10% of calories in a typical Western diet (about 55 g/day). The main source is sucrose (equal fructose and glucose); it is also free in fruit, honey and high-fructose corn syrup (about 55% fructose, 45% glucose). Fructose entry into cells is not insulin-dependent, and fructose does not stimulate insulin secretion.

Phosphorylation

  • Hexokinase can phosphorylate fructose but has a high Km for it, and glucose normally saturates it, so it does little.
  • Fructokinase is the main route: low Km, high Vmax, in liver (which handles most dietary fructose), kidney and small intestine. Fructose + ATP → fructose 1-phosphate.

Cleavage by aldolase B

Fructose 1-phosphate is not made into fructose 1,6-bisphosphate. Aldolase B (fructose 1-phosphate aldolase) splits it into DHAP and glyceraldehyde. DHAP enters glycolysis or gluconeogenesis; glyceraldehyde is phosphorylated by triokinase or used in other pathways.

IsoenzymeWhereCleaves fructose 1-P?
Aldolase AMost tissuesNo
Aldolase BLiver, kidney, small intestineYes
Aldolase CBrainNo

All three cleave fructose 1,6-bisphosphate in glycolysis. Because triose formation from fructose 1-phosphate bypasses PFK-1, fructose is metabolised faster than glucose.

Why HFI is dangerous

  1. Fructose 1-phosphate accumulates and traps inorganic phosphate, so ATP production falls.
  2. AMP rises and is degraded → hyperuricemia and lactic acidemia.
  3. Low hepatic ATP reduces gluconeogenesis (→ hypoglycemia with vomiting) and protein synthesis (→ fewer clotting factors). Low Pᵢ also inhibits glycogenolysis, and kidney Pᵢ reabsorption falls.
In the clinic: diagnosing and treating HFI

Diagnosis: fructose in the urine, enzyme assay of liver cells, or DNA testing. Treatment: remove both fructose and sucrose from the diet to prevent liver failure and death. Patients typically have a life-long aversion to sweets.

Mannose

Mannose is the C-2 epimer of glucose and an important part of glycoproteins. Hexokinase makes mannose 6-phosphate, and phosphomannose isomerase converts it reversibly to fructose 6-phosphate. Diet supplies little; most cell mannose is made from fructose or salvaged from glycoprotein breakdown.

Sorbitol and diabetes

Instead of being phosphorylated, a sugar can be reduced to a polyol (sugar alcohol).

EnzymeReactionWhere
Aldose reductaseGlucose + NADPH → sorbitol (glucitol); high KmRetina, lens, kidney, peripheral nerves, ovaries, seminal vesicles
Sorbitol dehydrogenaseSorbitol + NAD⁺ → fructoseLiver, ovaries, seminal vesicles

In seminal vesicles the two steps make fructose for sperm, their main carbohydrate fuel. In the liver they turn any sorbitol into a glycolytic substrate.

Why it matters in diabetes

Glucose enters the retina, lens, kidney and peripheral nerves without insulin. In hyperglycemia, aldose reductase makes much more sorbitol, which cannot leave the cell, especially where sorbitol dehydrogenase is low or absent. Water follows it in: osmotic swelling, contributing to cataracts, peripheral neuropathy, nephropathy and retinopathy. Using up NADPH also lowers reduced glutathione.

Galactose metabolism

The main source is lactose (galactosyl β(1→4)-glucose) from milk; some comes from lysosomal breakdown of glycoproteins and glycolipids. Galactose entry into cells is not insulin-dependent.

  1. Galactokinase: galactose + ATP → galactose 1-phosphate.
  2. Galactose 1-phosphate uridylyltransferase (GALT): galactose 1-P + UDP-glucose → UDP-galactose + glucose 1-P (an exchange). Glucose 1-P → glucose 6-P → glycolysis or free glucose.
  3. UDP-hexose 4-epimerase: UDP-galactose ⇌ UDP-glucose (its C-4 epimer), which can make glycogen or run the GALT step again.

UDP-galactose donates galactose for lactose, glycoproteins, glycolipids and glycosaminoglycans. Without dietary galactose (for example in lactose intolerance), the epimerase makes all the UDP-galactose needed from UDP-glucose.

Lactose synthesis and intolerance

Lactose is β-galactose joined β(1→4) to glucose, made only by the lactating mammary gland. Lactose synthase (UDP-galactose:glucose galactosyltransferase), in the Golgi, transfers galactose from UDP-galactose to glucose, releasing UDP.

SubunitIdentityWhereRole
Protein Aβ-D-galactosyltransferaseMany tissuesAlone, puts galactose on N-acetylglucosamine → N-acetyllactosamine (for N-linked glycoproteins)
Protein Bα-LactalbuminLactating mammary gland only; made in response to prolactinBinds protein A and lowers its Km for glucose, so it makes lactose
In the clinic: lactose intolerance

Also called lactose malabsorption, it affects up to 60% of adults of non-Northern-European ancestry. Lactase (β-galactosidase) deficiency leaves lactose undigested, causing cramping, diarrhea and bloating after dairy. Treatment: lactase supplements and avoiding dairy.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Fructose is metabolised faster than glucose because it skips PFK-1.
  • HFI appears at weaning, not at birth, because breast milk has no fructose.
  • In HFI the problem is phosphate trapping → low ATP, high uric acid.
  • Glucose enters the lens, nerve, kidney and retina without insulin, which is why sorbitol builds up there.
  • GALT uses UDP-glucose, not UTP.
  • α-Lactalbumin is the B protein and changes the specificity of protein A; it has no catalytic activity of its own.

Key terms

Fructokinase: liver enzyme making fructose 1-phosphate · Aldolase B: splits fructose 1-phosphate into DHAP and glyceraldehyde · Essential fructosuria: harmless fructokinase deficiency · Hereditary fructose intolerance: aldolase B deficiency · Phosphomannose isomerase: converts mannose 6-P to fructose 6-P · Polyol: sugar alcohol · Aldose reductase: NADPH enzyme making sorbitol or galactitol · Sorbitol dehydrogenase: oxidises sorbitol to fructose · Galactokinase: makes galactose 1-phosphate · GALT: galactose 1-phosphate uridylyltransferase · UDP-hexose 4-epimerase: interconverts UDP-galactose and UDP-glucose · Classic galactosemia: GALT deficiency · Galactitol: polyol causing cataracts · Lactose synthase: galactosyltransferase plus α-lactalbumin · α-Lactalbumin: prolactin-induced protein B of lactose synthase · N-acetyllactosamine: glycoprotein sugar made by protein A alone.
