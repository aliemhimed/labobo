Gluconeogenesis

How the liver and kidneys make new glucose from lactate, glycerol and amino acids when glycogen runs out, the four reactions that bypass glycolysis, and how glucagon, acetyl CoA and AMP control the pathway.

Labobo topic: Biochem Lec 3: Gluconeogenesis

In 60 seconds

  • Gluconeogenesis makes glucose de novo when liver glycogen is gone (after <24 h). Overnight: 90% liver, 10% kidney; after 48 h the kidneys make 40%.
  • Precursors: glycerol, lactate (Cori cycle) and glucogenic amino acids. Acetyl CoA, leucine and lysine cannot make net glucose.
  • Four bypasses: pyruvate carboxylase (biotin, ATP) + PEPCK (GTP) replace pyruvate kinase; fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase replaces PFK-1; glucose 6-phosphatase replaces hexokinase.
  • Oxaloacetate leaves mitochondria as malate, which also carries the NADH needed.
  • Cost: 6 high-energy phosphate bonds and 2 NADH per glucose from 2 pyruvate.
  • Glucagon: lowers fructose 2,6-bisphosphate, inactivates hepatic PK, induces PEPCK. Acetyl CoA activates pyruvate carboxylase; AMP and fructose 2,6-bisphosphate inhibit fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase.

Why and where

The brain, red cells, kidney medulla, lens and cornea, testes and exercising muscle need a continuous supply of glucose. Liver glycogen covers this for less than 24 hours without dietary carbohydrate; after that, glucose must be made from non-carbohydrate precursors.

Gluconeogenesis is not glycolysis run backwards, because glycolysis strongly favours pyruvate. It is a separate pathway using both mitochondrial and cytosolic enzymes. Deficiency of its enzymes causes hypoglycemia.

Substrates

A gluconeogenic precursor is anything that gives a net synthesis of glucose.

PrecursorSourceRoute into the pathway
GlycerolTriacylglycerol hydrolysis in adipose tissue, carried to the liverGlycerol kinase → glycerol 3-phosphate → glycerol 3-phosphate dehydrogenase → DHAP
LactateExercising muscle and red cells (anaerobic glycolysis)Liver oxidises it to pyruvate: the Cori cycle
Glucogenic amino acidsHydrolysis of tissue protein: the major source in a fastα-Keto acids such as pyruvate, or α-ketoglutarate → TCA cycle → oxaloacetate

All amino acids except leucine and lysine are glucogenic. Acetyl CoA, and compounds that give only acetyl CoA (acetoacetate, leucine, lysine), cannot make net glucose because the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex is irreversible; they are ketogenic.

The alanine cycle (a syllabus topic) works like the Cori cycle: muscle transaminates pyruvate to alanine, which travels to the liver, is turned back into pyruvate and then glucose, while its nitrogen becomes urea.

Pyruvate to PEP

Seven glycolytic reactions are reversible and are reused. The three irreversible ones (hexokinase, PFK-1, pyruvate kinase) are bypassed by four reactions unique to gluconeogenesis. The first two get round pyruvate kinase.

1. Pyruvate carboxylase (mitochondria)

  • Pyruvate + CO₂ (from HCO₃⁻) + ATP → oxaloacetate (OAA). Pyruvate enters mitochondria on the pyruvate carrier.
  • Needs biotin, bound to the ε-amino group of a lysine; ATP hydrolysis forms an enzyme–biotin–CO₂ intermediate. Other biotin carboxylases: acetyl CoA, propionyl CoA and methylcrotonyl CoA carboxylases.
  • Two purposes: substrate for PEP, and anaplerosis (refilling the TCA cycle). Muscle has the enzyme but uses it only for anaplerosis.
  • Allosterically activated by acetyl CoA, which signals that more OAA is needed (fasting). With little acetyl CoA, pyruvate goes instead to PDH and the TCA cycle.

2. Getting oxaloacetate to the cytosol

The inner mitochondrial membrane has no OAA transporter. Mitochondrial malate dehydrogenase reduces OAA to malate, which leaves; cytosolic malate dehydrogenase reoxidises it to OAA and makes NADH, which is needed later by glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase. When lactate is the source, NADH is already made in the cytosol, and OAA is converted to PEP inside mitochondria by the mitochondrial PEPCK; OAA can also leave as aspartate.

3. PEP carboxykinase (cytosol)

PEPCK decarboxylates and phosphorylates OAA to PEP, driven by GTP. Pairing a carboxylation with a decarboxylation makes an unfavourable step favourable; fatty acid synthesis uses the same trick. PEP then runs back up glycolysis to fructose 1,6-bisphosphate.

Fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase

In the liver and kidneys, fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase hydrolyses fructose 1,6-bisphosphate to fructose 6-phosphate, bypassing PFK-1. It is an important regulatory site.

  • Inhibited by a high AMP/ATP ratio (low energy); high ATP and low AMP favour gluconeogenesis.
  • Inhibited by fructose 2,6-bisphosphate. When glucagon is high, hepatic PFK-2 does not make it, so the phosphatase is active.

The same signals act oppositely on glycolysis (PFK-1), giving reciprocal control so glucose is not made and burned at the same time.

Glucose 6-phosphatase

The last step bypasses hexokinase/glucokinase. It needs two ER-membrane proteins found only in gluconeogenic tissue (mainly the liver):

  • glucose 6-phosphate translocase carries glucose 6-phosphate into the ER;
  • glucose 6-phosphatase removes the phosphate, giving free glucose, which transporters move to the cytosol and blood.

The same two proteins finish glycogen breakdown.

In the clinic: GSD Ia and Ib

Deficiency of the phosphatase (Ia) or the translocase (Ib) causes severe fasting hypoglycemia, because free glucose can be made neither by gluconeogenesis nor by glycogenolysis.

Glycolysis versus gluconeogenesis

Of the 11 reactions from pyruvate to free glucose, 7 use reversible glycolytic enzymes. The irreversible gluconeogenic steps push those equilibria towards glucose.

Making one glucose from two pyruvate uses six high-energy phosphate bonds (2 ATP at pyruvate carboxylase, 2 GTP at PEPCK, 2 ATP at phosphoglycerate kinase) and oxidises two NADH.

Regulation

Moment to moment, gluconeogenesis depends mainly on glucagon and on substrate supply; enzyme amounts change more slowly.

Glucagon (from pancreatic α cells)

  1. Allosteric: lowers hepatic fructose 2,6-bisphosphate → fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase activated, PFK-1 inhibited.
  2. Covalent: via its GPCR, cAMP and PKA, phosphorylates and inactivates hepatic pyruvate kinase, so PEP goes to glucose instead of pyruvate.
  3. Induction: increases transcription of PEPCK through CREB. Cortisol also induces PEPCK; insulin represses it.

Substrates and other signals

  • Substrate availability: low insulin mobilises amino acids from muscle protein. The ATP and NADH needed come mainly from fatty acid oxidation.
  • Acetyl CoA activates pyruvate carboxylase: in fasting, fatty acids flood the liver and β-oxidation makes more acetyl CoA than the TCA cycle can burn. Acetyl CoA also inhibits PDH (by activating PDH kinase), so one molecule sends pyruvate towards glucose and away from oxidation.
  • AMP inhibits fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase while activating PFK-1: energy-producing pathways on, energy-using ones off.

Commonly confused

PairHow to tell them apart

Cori vs alanine cycleCori carries lactate (muscle, red cells). Alanine cycle carries alanine and also delivers nitrogen for urea

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Fatty acids (even-chain) and acetyl CoA give no net glucose; glycerol from fat does.
  • Muscle has pyruvate carboxylase but cannot make glucose (no glucose 6-phosphatase); it uses OAA only to refill the TCA cycle.
  • OAA leaves the mitochondrion as malate (or aspartate), never as OAA.
  • PEPCK uses GTP, not ATP.
  • In prolonged fasting the kidney becomes a major glucose producer.
  • Acetyl CoA activates pyruvate carboxylase but inhibits pyruvate dehydrogenase.

Key terms

Gluconeogenesis: de novo synthesis of glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors · Gluconeogenic precursor: compound giving net glucose · Cori cycle: muscle lactate to liver glucose and back · Alanine cycle: muscle alanine to liver glucose, nitrogen to urea · Glucogenic amino acid: one whose carbon can become glucose · Ketogenic: giving only acetyl CoA or ketone bodies · Pyruvate carboxylase: biotin enzyme making oxaloacetate · Biotin: CO₂-carrying coenzyme on a lysine · Anaplerosis: refilling TCA-cycle intermediates · PEPCK: GTP-driven enzyme making PEP from oxaloacetate · Malate shuttle: route moving OAA and NADH to the cytosol · Fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase: bypass of PFK-1 · Glucose 6-phosphatase: ER enzyme releasing free glucose · Reciprocal regulation: one signal turning one pathway on and its opposite off · CREB: transcription factor inducing PEPCK.
