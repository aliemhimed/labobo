Chromosome Mutations

Changes in chromosome number: aneuploidy and euploidy, nondisjunction, monosomy and haploinsufficiency, then the autosomal trisomies: Down syndrome (features, critical region, origin of the extra chromosome, maternal age, testing), Patau and Edwards syndromes.

Labobo topic: MG Lec 5: Chromosome Mutations

In 60 seconds

  • Aneuploidy: one or more chromosomes gained or lost (2n ± x). Euploidy: whole haploid sets; polyploidy = more than two sets (3n, 4n…).
  • Nondisjunction: homologs (meiosis I) or sister chromatids (meiosis II) fail to separate → gametes n+1 or n−1 → trisomy or monosomy.
  • Autosomal monosomy is not tolerated (unmasked lethal alleles, haploinsufficiency); X monosomy = Turner, 45,X.
  • Down syndrome 47,+21: ~1 in 800 births; leukaemia ~20×; early Alzheimer disease; fewer solid tumours (DSCR1 suppresses VEGF).
  • Extra 21: 75% from meiosis I; the ovum in ~95%; risk rises sharply with maternal age.
  • Patau 47,+13 and Edwards 47,+18: severe malformations, early death.

Learning objectives

Types of chromosome mutation

Chromosome mutations are changes in the number of chromosomes, deletion or duplication of genes or segments, and rearrangements within or between chromosomes.

Nondisjunction

  • Paired homologs (or sister chromatids) fail to disjoin during segregation.
  • Gametes get an extra or missing chromosome; fertilisation by a normal gamete gives a trisomic (three copies) or monosomic (one copy) zygote.
  • In first-division nondisjunction, all four gametes are abnormal (two n+1, two n−1). In second-division nondisjunction, two gametes are normal, one n+1 and one n−1.

Monosomy

  • 2n − 1. It occurs for the X chromosome: 45,X, Turner syndrome.
  • Monosomy for any autosome is usually not tolerated in humans and animals:
    • a lethal recessive allele on the single chromosome is no longer masked;
    • one copy of a gene may not give enough product: haploinsufficiency.

Down syndrome

  • Trisomy (2n + 1) is better tolerated than monosomy. Trisomy 21 is the only human autosomal trisomy in which many survive long after birth.
  • 47,+21 (written 47,21+ on the slides); about 1 in 800 live births.
  • Features: epicanthic folds, flat face and round head, furrowed tongue, short broad hands (single palmar crease), widely spaced first and second toes, poor muscle tone, delayed physical, psychomotor and mental development.
  • Prone to respiratory disease and heart malformations; leukaemia ~20 times more common.
  • In older adults, death is often due to Alzheimer disease, with much earlier onset.

Down syndrome critical region

  • Chromosome 21 holds dosage-sensitive genes in the Down syndrome critical region (DSCR).
  • Three copies of this region are necessary but not sufficient for the cognitive deficits.
  • People with Down syndrome have a lower risk of several solid tumours (lung cancer, melanoma).
  • Reason: DSCR1 encodes a protein that suppresses VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor), so tumours cannot build a proper blood supply.

Origin of the extra chromosome and testing

  • Caused by nondisjunction of chromosome 21 in anaphase I or II; 75% in meiosis I.
  • The ovum is the source in about 95% of 47,+21 cases.
  • Strong link with maternal age, probably because of how oocytes develop (arrested in meiosis for decades), though hard to prove directly. Risk rises from ~3/1000 at 35 to ~1/30 at 45 and ~1/15 at 50.
  • Testing: amniocentesis and chorionic villus sampling (fetal cells), and noninvasive prenatal genetic diagnosis (NIPGD) from the mother’s blood.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Down syndrome raises leukaemia but lowers solid tumour risk.
  • DSCR genes are necessary but not sufficient for cognitive deficits.
  • Most trisomy 21 is maternal, meiosis I.
  • Turner is the monosomy humans survive; autosomal monosomy is not.

Key terms

Chromosome mutation: change in chromosome number or structure · Aneuploidy: gain or loss of single chromosomes · Euploidy: complete haploid sets · Polyploidy: more than two sets · Monosomy: 2n − 1 · Trisomy: 2n + 1 · Nondisjunction: failure of chromosomes to separate · Haploinsufficiency: one copy not enough · Turner syndrome: 45,X · Down syndrome: trisomy 21 · Epicanthic fold: skin fold of the upper eyelid · DSCR: Down syndrome critical region · VEGF: vascular endothelial growth factor · Amniocentesis: sampling amniotic fluid · Chorionic villus sampling: sampling placental villi · NIPGD: noninvasive prenatal diagnosis · Patau syndrome: trisomy 13 · Edwards syndrome: trisomy 18.
