Composition and Arrangement of Chromosomes

Structural chromosome aberrations: how breaks lead to deletions, duplications, inversions and translocations, terminal and intercalary deletions, cri du chat syndrome, duplication by unequal crossing over, and gene redundancy and amplification of rRNA genes.

Labobo topic: MG Lec 6: Composition and Arrangement of Chromosomes

In 60 seconds

  • Structural aberrations: deletion, duplication, inversion, and translocation (exchange or transfer to a nonhomologous chromosome).
  • They start from breaks (spontaneous, or more with chemicals or radiation); broken ends are “sticky”. In germ cells they are heritable.
  • Deletion: terminal or intercalary; the piece without a centromere is lost.
  • Cri du chat: 46,5p−, a segmental deletion of 5p; cat-like cry; 1 in 25,000–50,000; usually sporadic; loses TERT.
  • Duplication: from unequal crossing over (makes one duplication + one deletion) or replication error.
  • Gene redundancy: many rRNA genes (rDNA) at the nucleolar organiser region; amplification in oocytes.

Structural aberrations

  • Deletion, addition or rearrangement of substantial parts of one or more chromosomes.
  • A segment can be inverted, exchanged with a nonhomologous chromosome, or transferred to another chromosome; exchanges and transfers are translocations (reciprocal or nonreciprocal).
  • Gene locations in the genome change.

How breaks cause them

  • Structural changes come from one or more breaks along a chromosome.
  • Breakage is spontaneous, but its rate rises with chemicals or radiation.
  • Broken ends are “sticky” and can rejoin in new ways.
  • If the change is in germ cells, gametes carry it and it is heritable.

Deletions

  • A chromosome breaks and a piece is lost: the missing piece is a deletion.
  • Terminal deletion: near one end. Intercalary deletion: within the chromosome (two breaks, the middle piece lost).
  • The part that keeps the centromere is maintained at division; the acentric piece is lost after mitosis or meiosis.
  • In meiosis, the normal homolog pairs with the deleted one by forming a deletion loop (compensation loop) of the unpaired region.

Cri du chat syndrome

  • Deletion of a small terminal part of the short arm of chromosome 5: 46,5p−.
  • It could be called partial monosomy, but the region is small, so it is a segmental deletion.
  • Signs: high-pitched cat-like cry (abnormal glottis and larynx), small head, round face, flat nasal bridge, epicanthic folds, malformations, gastrointestinal and cardiac problems, mental retardation.
  • Incidence 1 in 25,000–50,000; usually not inherited, from a sporadic loss in a gamete.
  • Longer deletions → greater physical, psychomotor and mental impact.
  • The missing part contains TERT (telomerase reverse transcriptase).

Duplications

  • A segment present more than once in the genome is a duplication.
  • Causes: unequal crossing over between misaligned synapsed chromosomes in meiosis, or a replication error before meiosis.
  • Unequal crossing over produces both a duplication and a deletion.

Gene redundancy and amplification

  • Best example: ribosomal RNA. One rRNA gene is not enough for metabolically active cells, so there are multiple copies: rDNA.
  • They lie at the nucleolar organiser region (NOR), repeated units of 18S, 5.8S and 28S coding sequences with intergenic spacers.
  • In some cells, such as oocytes, even this is not enough: gene amplification makes extra copies.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Cri du chat is a 5p (short arm) deletion, usually sporadic.
  • The piece lost after a break is the one without a centromere.
  • Unequal crossing over gives a deletion and a duplication.
  • Only germ-cell aberrations are inherited.

Key terms

Chromosomal aberration: structural chromosome change · Deletion: lost segment · Terminal deletion: lost end · Intercalary deletion: lost internal segment · Acentric: lacking a centromere · Deletion loop: unpaired bulge in meiosis · Duplication: repeated segment · Inversion: segment turned 180° · Translocation: segment moved to a nonhomologous chromosome · Unequal crossing over: crossover between misaligned homologs · Cri du chat: 5p deletion syndrome · TERT: telomerase reverse transcriptase · rDNA: repeated rRNA genes · Nucleolar organiser region: rDNA site forming the nucleolus · Gene amplification: extra gene copies made on demand.
