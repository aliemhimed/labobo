CRISPR, Noncoding RNAs and the Genetic Code

How miRNAs are made and act, miRNAs in cancer, piRNAs in germ cells, CRISPR in bacteria and as a gene-editing tool, hnRNA, the properties of the genetic code, tRNA structure and charging, and polyribosomes.

Labobo topic: MolBio Lec 3: CRISPR, Noncoding RNAs and Encoding Genetic Information

In 60 seconds

  • miRNA: pri-miRNA hairpin → cut by Drosha in the nucleus → pre-miRNA → exported → cut by Dicer → guide strand with Ago1 binds mRNA with partial complementarity → blocks translation.
  • Low miRNA → its target genes are overexpressed → can drive cancer.
  • piRNAs (24–32 nt) bind PIWI proteins in germ cells and silence transposons; no dsRNA, no Dicer (Zucchini cuts them).
  • CRISPR: bacterial loci of phage-derived spacers between repeats, with nearby Cas genes; CRISPR–Cas9 = guide RNA + Cas9 scissors + new DNA, used ex vivo or in vivo (e.g. sickle cell, GAG→GTG).
  • Genetic code: triplet (64 codons), degenerate, nonoverlapping, commaless, unambiguous, universal, read 5′→3′; stops UAA, UAG, UGA.
  • tRNA: 73–93 nt, cloverleaf in 2-D, L-shape in 3-D, 3′ CCA. Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase charges it in two steps using ATP.

MicroRNAs

miRNA genes are as much part of the genome as protein-coding genes; miRNAs are found in cells and in the bloodstream. Other small RNAs: stRNA (small temporal RNA, a kind of miRNA) and scnRNA (scan RNAs, ~28–30 nt, act by an RNAi-like mechanism).

  1. The gene is transcribed into a single strand that folds back into a stem-loop: pri-miRNA.
  2. In the nucleus, a complex containing the endonuclease Drosha cuts near the loop → pre-miRNA (3′ overhang at one end).
  3. The pre-miRNA is exported to the cytoplasm and cut by Dicer → short duplex with 3′ overhangs at both ends.
  4. The duplex joins an Argonaute (usually Ago1); the passenger strand (miRNA*) is removed.
  5. The guide binds a partly complementary region of an mRNA (a bulge) and inhibits translation, or causes deadenylation and degradation.

Most plant miRNAs (and a few animal ones) match perfectly, so the mRNA is cleaved by Ago2 like in RNAi. Some intron-derived miRNAs do not need Drosha.

In the clinic: microRNA and cancer

Mutations can delete or reduce a miRNA. Its target genes are then overexpressed, which can drive cancer development and progression. One miRNA can regulate hundreds or thousands of genes, and abnormal miRNA expression is found in many human cancers.

piRNAs

  • Piwi-interacting RNAs are expressed in animal germ cells and suppress movement of transposable elements. A jump in a germ cell could affect every cell of the next generation; in a somatic cell only that cell line.
  • They bind PIWI proteins, a subclass of the Argonaute family. In fruit flies, losing PIWI lets transposons move and gametes fail to form.

siRNA and piRNA both defend against dangerous elements: siRNA arises from any dsRNA (a hallmark of viruses), while piRNA is a genetically encoded defence, a strategy also used by bacteria in CRISPR.

CRISPR

  • Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats: bacterial loci with short DNA pieces identical to phage sequences (spacers), separated by repeats. Nearby Cas (CRISPR-associated) genes encode nucleases, the “molecular scissors”. It is a bacterial antiviral system.
  • CRISPR–Cas9 is engineered for eukaryotic cells. Three players: a lab-made guide RNA that finds the target; Cas9, which cuts both DNA strands; and the new DNA inserted at the break.
  • Use in disease: sickle cell anaemia is a single point mutation GAG → GTG (glutamic acid, hydrophilic → valine, hydrophobic). Cells can be edited ex vivo (removed, edited, returned) or in vivo (components delivered, e.g. in lipid nanoparticles).

hnRNA and other noncoding RNAs

  • Heterogeneous nuclear RNA = the large pre-mRNAs of many sequences, the immediate product of transcription in mammalian nuclei.
  • Very large: molecular weight can exceed 10⁷, while mRNA is under 2 × 10⁶.
  • About 75% is degraded in the nucleus; only 25% becomes mature mRNA, by capping, tailing, splicing and base modification (e.g. ovalbumin: 7 introns removed).
  • A hypothesis: a processed hnRNA fragment may activate rRNA gene transcription by pairing with the non-transcribed region of the rRNA gene.
  • miRNAs and siRNAs are promising targets for drug development.

The genetic code

George Gamow proposed a triplet code and suggested it overlapped. Studies of mutant proteins such as sickle cell haemoglobin (one amino acid changed) showed it is nonoverlapping: in an overlapping code one base change would alter up to three amino acids.

Triplet4 × 4 × 4 = 64 codons, enough for 20 amino acids
DegenerateOne amino acid, several codons (Arg, Ala, Leu have six each); all except two amino acids have ≥2. Related codons reduce the effect of substitutions
NonoverlappingEach base belongs to only one codon
CommalessNo signal between codons
Non-ambiguousA codon always means the same amino acid
UniversalSame in bacteria and humans
PolarityRead 5′→3′
InitiationAUG (and GUG in E. coli); inside a gene they code Met and Val
TerminationUAA, UAG, UGA: nonsense codons; the other 61 are sense codons

Using the chart: first letter on the left, second on top, third on the right (e.g. UGC = cysteine). Frameshift mutations (insertion or deletion of 1 or 2 bases) shift the reading frame and change every amino acid after the mutation.

Structure of tRNA

  • Robert Holley (1965) sequenced the first RNA: yeast tRNAAla, 77 nt, 10 of them modified.
  • All tRNAs are 73–93 nt, with many modified bases made post-transcriptionally and concentrated in the loops, where they block H-bonding and act as recognition sites.
  • Internal complementary stretches fold them into a 2-D cloverleaf; in 3-D they are an L shape of two double helices.
  • All mature tRNAs end in 3′ CCA: encoded in many prokaryotes, added by an enzyme in eukaryotes.

tRNA charging

  • Each tRNA must carry its correct (cognate) amino acid. An aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase (aaRS) links it to the 3′ end, recognising the acceptor stem and anticodon.
  • Usually 20 synthetases, one per amino acid; each charges all tRNAs for its amino acid.
  1. ATP + amino acid → aminoacyl-AMP + PPᵢ (the main energy-using step of protein synthesis).
  2. Aminoacyl-AMP + tRNA → aminoacyl-tRNA + AMP.

Polyribosomes

  • A polyribosome (polysome) is several ribosomes translating one mRNA at once.
  • Each ribosome assembles at the start codon and moves toward the 3′ end until a stop codon; as it moves on, another ribosome starts.
  • On rough ER, ribosomes appear in loops and spirals: polysomes attached to mRNA.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • piRNA biogenesis uses no Dicer and no dsRNA.
  • piRNAs act in germ cells, against transposons.
  • The code is degenerate but not ambiguous.
  • The code is nonoverlapping (Gamow’s overlap was wrong).
  • CCA is added enzymatically in eukaryotes.
  • The ATP-using step of charging is amino acid activation (aminoacyl-AMP).
