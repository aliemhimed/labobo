Cell–Environment Interactions and the Extracellular Matrix

How multicellular life arose, how cells interact with each other and the extracellular matrix, the basement membrane, collagens and their diseases (osteogenesis imperfecta, Ehlers–Danlos, Alport), Marfan syndrome, proteoglycans, fibronectin and laminin, and how the matrix is remodelled.

Labobo topic: MolBio Lec 7: Cell–Environment Interactions and the Extracellular Matrix

In 60 seconds

  • Life began ~3 billion years ago; multicellular animals (metazoans) ~600 million years ago.
  • The ECM is a network of secreted molecules that scaffolds cells and sends physical, biochemical and mechanical signals.
  • Basement membrane: 50–200 nm sheet under epithelia and endothelium and around muscle, fat and nerve fibres.
  • Main ECM parts: collagens (28 types, triple helix), proteoglycans (core protein + acidic GAGs), fibronectin and laminin.
  • Diseases: type I collagen → osteogenesis imperfecta; type II → dwarfism; other collagens → Ehlers–Danlos; type IV → Alport; FBN1 (fibrillin-1) → Marfan.
  • The ECM is dynamic: remodelled by zinc-containing matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs).

Evolution of multicellular organisms

  • The earliest life arose about 3 billion years ago as simple single cells.
  • For over a billion years single-celled life prospered; some lineages, such as eukaryotes, grew more complex.
  • Fossils suggest the first multicellular animals, metazoans, appeared about 600 million years ago.

Overview of extracellular interactions

  • The plasma membrane is the boundary, but material outside it matters greatly to the cell.
  • Most cells sit in tissues with defined relations to neighbours and to extracellular material; even wandering white blood cells interact specifically with cells and matrix.
  • These interactions regulate migration, growth and differentiation, and shape tissues and organs in development.
  • In skin: epidermal cells join by specialised cell–cell contacts; the basal layer attaches to the basement membrane; the dermis is mostly extracellular material with scattered fibroblasts. Cell-surface receptors (integrins) pass signals inside.

The ECM and the basement membrane

  • The extracellular matrix is an organised network of secreted molecules that scaffolds cells and tissues. It is not inert glue: it gives physical, biochemical and mechanical signals that shape cells and their activity (e.g. the ECM secreted by chondrocytes).
  • The basement membrane (basal lamina) is a continuous sheet 50–200 nm thick that:
    • surrounds nerve fibres, muscles and fat cells;
    • underlies epithelia (epidermis, gut and airway linings);
    • underlies the endothelium of blood vessels.
  • Jobs: mechanical support, survival signals, a substrate for migration, separating tissues, and a barrier to macromolecules.
  • Its collagen network is dense; the dermis collagen network is much looser.

Collagens

  • A family of fibrous glycoproteins found only in the ECM, with high tensile strength (resist pulling).
  • Made mainly by fibroblasts, also smooth muscle and epithelial cells.
  • 28 human types, each restricted to certain places; several types can mix in one ECM or one fibre.
  • All are trimers of three α chains wound, along at least part of their length, into a rod-like triple helix; homotrimers or heterotrimers.
  • A collagen I molecule is 295 nm long; molecules align in staggered rows to form fibrils.

In the clinic: Marfan syndrome

Weak connective tissue. Heart and aorta are the most dangerous: aortic aneurysm and dissection, mitral valve prolapse. Skeleton: tall and thin, long limbs (dolichostenomelia), long fingers and toes (arachnodactyly), flexible joints, scoliosis, sunken or protruding chest. Eyes: severe myopia and lens dislocation (ectopia lentis).

Proteoglycans

  • Abundant in basement membranes and other matrices: a core protein with covalently attached glycosaminoglycan (GAG) chains.
  • Each GAG is a repeating disaccharide (‑A‑B‑A‑B‑), highly acidic from sulfate and carboxyl groups (e.g. chondroitin sulfate, keratan sulfate).
  • Core proteins can attach to a molecule of hyaluronic acid, a nonsulfated GAG, forming gigantic complexes that fill much of the extracellular space.

Fibronectin and laminin

  • ECM proteins each carry binding sites for one another and for cell-surface receptors, so they form an interconnected network bound to the cell.
  • Fibronectin: two similar but nonidentical polypeptides joined by a pair of disulfide bonds near the C-termini. Each has ~30 Fn modules grouped into functional units binding heparin, fibrin, collagen and cells (via the RGD sequence).
  • Laminins: three different chains joined by disulfide bonds into a cross with three short arms and one long arm; at least 15 types. They strongly influence migration, growth and differentiation.
  • Collagen, fibronectin and laminin bind integrins on the cell surface.

Dynamic properties of the ECM

Not covered on the slides; from Karp, section 7.1 (syllabus topic).

  • In space: ECM fibrils can stretch several times their length when cells pull, and contract when released.
  • In time: components are continually degraded and rebuilt, renewing the matrix and remodelling it in development and after injury; even bone matrix is constantly restored.
  • Degradation is mostly by matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs): zinc-containing enzymes, secreted or membrane-anchored; together they digest almost all ECM components.
  • Roles: tissue remodelling, embryonic cell migration, wound healing, blood vessel formation.
  • Excess MMP activity is linked to arthritis, tooth and gum disease, blood clots and heart attack, and tumour progression; at least three inherited skeletal disorders come from MMP gene mutations.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Collagen is found only in the extracellular matrix.
  • Marfan is a fibrillin-1 (FBN1) defect, not collagen, and is autosomal dominant.
  • Alport syndrome is type IV collagen (basement membrane).
  • GAGs are acidic because of sulfate and carboxyl groups.
  • MMPs are zinc-dependent.
  • The ECM sends signals; it is not just packing material.
