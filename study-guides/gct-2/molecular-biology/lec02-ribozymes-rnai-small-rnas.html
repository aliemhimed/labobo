Ribozymes, RNA Interference and Small RNAs

Why RNA probably came before DNA, how rRNA, 5S rRNA and tRNA are made and processed, the ribosome and its A, P and E sites, ribozymes (including the peptidyl transferase), and how siRNAs and miRNAs silence genes.

Labobo topic: MolBio Lec 2: Ribozymes, RNA Interference and Small RNAs

In 60 seconds

  • RNA world: RNA can both store information and catalyse; ribose forms easily from formaldehyde, deoxyribose is made from ribose by an enzyme, so RNA came first; DNA won because it is more stable.
  • rRNA is >80% of cell RNA; rDNA is repeated hundreds of times in five clusters (nucleoli). Pol I makes one pre-rRNA → 28S, 18S, 5.8S; Pol III makes 5S outside the nucleolus.
  • Pre-rRNA is modified: >100 2′-O-methylations and ~95 pseudouridines (dyskerin in snoRNPs).
  • Pol III promoters for 5S rRNA and tRNA lie inside the gene.
  • Ribosome: tRNA sites A, P, E; peptide bonds are made by rRNA (23S in bacteria): a ribozyme.
  • RNAi: Dicer cuts dsRNA into 21–23 nt siRNA → guide strand in RISC with Argonaute (Ago2) → complementary RNA cleaved. miRNAs (21–24 nt) mainly regulate gene expression.

RNA and the origin of life

  • Macromolecules are, by mass, the most abundant organic molecules in a cell; they are polymers of small monomers.
  • An RNA world may have existed before cells with DNA and proteins. RNA still catalyses key reactions; these ribozymes (in the ribosome and splicing machinery) are “molecular fossils”.
  • RNA can store information and catalyse: a strand templates its complement, which templates the original, the basis of replication and transcription.
  • Evidence RNA came first: ribose forms readily from formaldehyde (HCHO) in primitive-Earth experiments; deoxyribose is harder to make and today is made from ribose by a protein enzyme.
  • DNA came later and proved better for storage: deoxyribose makes the backbone chemically more stable, so long chains survive without breaking.

The RNA family

RNA is coding (mRNA) or noncoding; noncoding RNAs are constituent (rRNA, tRNA) or regulatory (siRNA, miRNA, snoRNA, scnRNA).

  • Small RNAs form ribonucleoproteins in the nucleus, cytoplasm or both; they are 20–300 nt, at 100,000–1,000,000 copies per cell.
  • snRNAs join proteins as snRNPs in the spliceosome, which removes introns (the excised intron is a lariat).

Ribosomal RNA synthesis and processing

  • A cell may have millions of ribosomes; rRNA is >80% of cell RNA.
  • rRNA genes (rDNA) are repeated hundreds of times, in five clusters on five chromosomes, gathered in interphase into nucleoli.
  • Human ribosome: large subunit 28S, 5.8S, 5S; small subunit 18S.
  • RNA polymerase I makes one pre-rRNA, cut by nucleases into 28S, 18S and 5.8S.
  • Pre-rRNA is heavily modified post-transcriptionally: over 100 2′-O-methylations of ribose and about 95 uridines turned into pseudouridine (the uracil ring is rotated 120°). The enzyme is dyskerin, a protein of snoRNPs; snoRNAs pick the sites by base pairing.

5S rRNA and transfer RNAs

  • 5S rRNA (~120 nt) is in the large subunit of both prokaryotes and eukaryotes. Its many identical genes lie outside the nucleolus; it is made by Pol III and moved into the nucleolus for assembly.
  • Pol III can bind a promoter within the transcribed part of its gene.
  • About 50 tRNA species; tRNA genes number ~275 in yeast, ~850 in flies, ~1300 in humans, in small scattered clusters (tDNA) with spacers.
  • tRNAs are made by Pol III (internal promoter); the precursor is trimmed at both ends (sometimes an internal piece) and many bases are modified.
  • tRNA carries its amino acid by an ester bond at the 3′ CCA end; the anticodon pairs with the mRNA codon.

The ribosome

  • Eukaryotic ribosome: four rRNAs and more than 80 proteins; prokaryotic ones are very similar.
  • Large and small subunits come together only after the small subunit has bound an mRNA.
  • Proteins outnumber rRNAs, but rRNAs give most of the mass and the shape.
  • One mRNA site and three tRNA sites: A (aminoacyl-tRNA), P (peptidyl-tRNA), E (exit). Only two are occupied at any one time.

Ribozymes

  • A ribozyme is an RNA molecule with catalytic activity; its folded shape works like a protein enzyme, though with only four building blocks it is less diverse.
  • The tRNA sites and the catalytic site of the ribosome are formed by rRNA: the peptidyl transferase is in the 23S rRNA of the bacterial large subunit; no protein is close enough to take part. It positions the growing chain and the charged tRNA precisely.
  • Natural ribozymes catalyse cleavage and ligation of phosphodiester bonds (RNA splicing, tRNA biosynthesis, viral replication) and peptide bond formation.
  • Laboratory ribozymes: in 2020 a ribozyme was made that adds a methyl group to a chosen adenine of a target RNA (m¹A). Ribozymes can also control base pairing and add fluorescent labels for RNA imaging.

RNA interference and siRNA

Gene expression can be controlled at transcription, RNA processing, transport and translation. RNA interference (RNAi) silences specific genes by neutralising their mRNA. Older names: co-suppression, post-transcriptional gene silencing (PTGS), quelling.

  • It probably evolved to block viruses and transposons, both of which can form double-stranded RNA, which the cell treats as foreign.
  • First seen in petunias: an extra pigment-enzyme transgene gave white instead of purple flowers.
  1. Dicer (endonuclease) cuts both strands of dsRNA into 21–23 nt siRNA.
  2. The siRNA joins a pre-RISC; the passenger strand is cut and leaves.
  3. The guide strand with its Argonaute (Ago2 in animals) forms RISC.
  4. The guide binds a complementary target RNA (viral, transposon or mRNA).
  5. Argonaute cleaves the target, which is degraded.
In the clinic: RNAi as a drug strategy

Instead of a small chemical that blocks an enzyme’s active site, an siRNA can silence the gene encoding it. Example: knocking down PLK1 greatly reduced tumour growth in mice.

MicroRNAs

  • miRNAs are 21–24 nt, the same size as siRNAs, and also silence post-transcriptionally (“cousins”).
  • siRNAs mainly protect genome integrity; miRNAs mainly regulate gene expression, e.g. specific miRNAs are expressed in specific tissues during zebrafish development.
  • miRNAs regulate apoptosis and are linked to cancer; the first apoptosis-linked miRNAs, miR-14 and bantam, were found in Drosophila.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Peptide bonds are formed by rRNA, not a ribosomal protein.
  • Deoxyribose is made from ribose, which supports RNA first.
  • DNA is favoured for storage because it is more stable, not because it catalyses.
  • Pol III promoters are internal to the gene.
  • Dicer cuts dsRNA; Argonaute cuts the target.
  • rRNA modifications happen after transcription.
