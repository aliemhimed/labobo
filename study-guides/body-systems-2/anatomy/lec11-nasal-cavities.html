Nasal cavities

The walls, floor and roof of the nasal cavities, the conchae and meatuses, the ethmoid bone, the external nose, the four paranasal sinuses and where each drains, the gateways for nerves and vessels, and the blood supply, veins and nerves. Clinical links: septal deviation, epistaxis, sinusitis and the approach to the pituitary.

Labobo topic: Anatomy Lec 11: Nasal Cavities

In 60 seconds

  • Each cavity opens in front by the naris and behind by the choana (into the nasopharynx). The two cavities are split by the septum.
  • Three conchae make four channels: inferior, middle, superior meatus and the spheno-ethmoidal recess (above the superior concha).
  • Three regions: vestibule (skin, hair), respiratory (largest, ciliated), olfactory (apex).
  • Nerves: I smell; V1 front and V2 back for sensation; VII (greater petrosal) parasympathetic to glands.
  • Drainage: nasolacrimal duct to the inferior meatus; frontal, maxillary and anterior ethmoid to the middle meatus; posterior ethmoid to the superior meatus; sphenoid to the spheno-ethmoidal recess.
  • Largest artery: sphenopalatine. Nosebleed site: front of the medial wall, where four arteries anastomose.
  • Pituitary can be reached through the nose and the sphenoidal sinus.

Learning objectives

Syllabus, Week XII: nasal cavities: walls, conchae, meatuses, openings, vessels, nerves. Source: Gray's Anatomy for Students, pp. 1058-1073. By the end you can:

Overview, regions and functions

The two nasal cavities are the uppermost part of the respiratory tract and contain the olfactory receptors. Each is an elongated wedge: a large base below and a narrow apex above. A framework of bone and cartilage holds them open. The smaller front part lies inside the external nose; the larger back part lies deeper in the skull.

  • Anterior openings: the nares, on the lower surface of the nose. Posterior openings: the choanae, into the nasopharynx.
  • The cavities are separated from each other by the nasal septum, from the mouth below by the hard palate, and from the cranial cavity above by parts of the frontal, ethmoid and sphenoid bones. The orbits lie lateral.
  • Each cavity has a floor, roof, medial wall and lateral wall.
RegionFeatures
Nasal vestibuleSmall dilated space just inside the naris; lined by skin; has hair follicles
Respiratory regionThe largest part; rich nerve and blood supply; respiratory epithelium of mainly ciliated and mucous cells
Olfactory regionSmall; at the apex; olfactory epithelium with the olfactory receptors

Functions: smell; warming and humidifying the air through a rich blood supply; filtering particles through vestibular hair and trapping material in mucus. Cilia move the mucus backward and it is swallowed.

Skeletal framework and the ethmoid bone

  • Unpaired bones: ethmoid, sphenoid, frontal, vomer. Paired bones: nasal, maxillary, palatine, lacrimal, and the inferior conchae.
  • The ethmoid is the key bone and one of the most complex in the skull. It forms part of the roof, lateral wall and medial wall of both cavities and contains the ethmoidal cells.

Ethmoidal labyrinths (2)Box-like, one each side; each is two thin sheets of bone around the ethmoidal cells. Cuboidal bone overall
Orbital plateLateral sheet of the labyrinth; flat; part of the medial wall of the orbit

Ethmoidal infundibulumGroove running forward and up from under the bulla; narrows into a channel opening into the frontal sinus (the frontonasal duct)
Uncinate processThin curved projection on the front of the labyrinth's lower surface; runs back and down over the maxillary hiatus to meet the inferior concha; forms the medial boundary of the ethmoidal infundibulum; part of the osteomeatal complex

The labyrinth's upper surface meets the frontal bone, its front surface the maxilla's frontal process and the lacrimal bone, and its lower surface the upper medial maxilla.

Watch out

The slide on the uncinate process says the ethmoidal infundibulum drains the maxillary, frontal and anterior ethmoidal sinuses. The textbook (Gray's, pp. 1058-1073) gives the openings separately: the frontal sinus through the frontonasal duct and infundibulum, anterior ethmoidal cells into the infundibulum or frontonasal duct, and the maxillary sinus into the semilunar hiatus just below the centre of the bulla. For exams use the textbook list (see Paranasal sinuses). The slide does give the correct idea that the area is the osteomeatal complex.

External nose

  • Pyramid-shaped, apex in front. It extends the cavities onto the face and points the nares downward. Its upper angle between the orbits is continuous with the forehead.
  • Bony part: nasal bones and parts of the maxillae and frontal bone.
  • Cartilage part (the main support in front): lateral processes of the septal cartilage, major alar cartilage, three or four minor alar cartilages, and the single midline septal cartilage forming the front of the septum.

Paranasal sinuses

There are four: ethmoidal cells, sphenoidal, maxillary and frontal, each named for its bone. They grow out from the nasal cavities and erode into the bones during childhood and early adulthood. All are lined by ciliated, mucus-secreting respiratory mucosa, open into the nasal cavity, and are supplied by the trigeminal nerve [V].

In the clinic: reaching the pituitary

Only thin bone separates the sphenoidal sinus from the nasal cavity below and the hypophyseal fossa above. The pituitary can be reached through the roof of the nasal cavity: through the lower front of the sphenoid, into the sinus, then through its top into the fossa.

In the clinic: osteomeatal complex

The uncinate process and the area where the maxillary, frontal and anterior ethmoidal sinuses drain form the osteomeatal complex. Blockage or narrowing there can lead to sinus infection.

Walls, floor and roof

PartFormed by
Medial wall (septum)Mucosa-covered thin septum in the median sagittal plane. Septal cartilage in front; behind, mainly the vomer and the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid. Small contributions: nasal bones, frontal nasal spine, nasal crests of maxilla and palatine, sphenoid rostrum, incisor crest of the maxilla
FloorSmooth, concave, much wider than the roof. Soft tissues of the external nose, plus the palatine process of the maxilla and horizontal plate of the palatine bone (the hard palate). The naris opens into its front; the upper opening of the incisive canal is just lateral to the septum near the front
RoofNarrow; highest centrally where it is the cribriform plate. Front: frontal nasal spine, nasal bones, lateral processes of the septal cartilage and major alar cartilages. Back (slopes down to the choana): front of the sphenoid, ala of the vomer, sphenoidal process of the palatine, vaginal process of the medial pterygoid plate. The roof has perforations of the cribriform plate and, in front of them, a foramen for the anterior ethmoidal nerve and vessels. The sphenoidal sinus opens on its posterior slope
Lateral wallEthmoidal labyrinth, superior and middle conchae, uncinate process; perpendicular plate of the palatine; medial pterygoid plate; medial surfaces of lacrimal and maxilla; inferior concha. In the external nose: cartilage and soft tissue
In the clinic: deviated septum

The septum is usually near the midline but may deviate to one side, often after direct trauma. Extreme deviation can block the nose. It can be corrected surgically.

Lateral wall, conchae and meatuses

The lateral wall has three curved bony shelves, the conchae, one above the other. They project medially and downward; their medial, front and back margins are free. They increase the contact area between the wall and the air. They divide each cavity into four channels:

ChannelLies betweenReceives
Inferior meatusInferior concha and the floorNasolacrimal duct, under the front lip of the inferior concha
Middle meatusInferior and middle conchaeFrontal sinus (frontonasal duct), anterior ethmoidal cells, middle ethmoidal cells (on or above the bulla), maxillary sinus (semilunar hiatus)
Superior meatusMiddle and superior conchaePosterior ethmoidal cells; the sphenopalatine foramen is in its posterolateral wall
Spheno-ethmoidal recessSuperior concha and the roofSphenoidal sinus
  • The superior and middle conchae belong to the ethmoid; the inferior concha is a separate bone.
  • The nasolacrimal duct drains tears from the conjunctival sac. It begins at the lower end of the lacrimal sac on the anteromedial orbital wall.

Vessels

The respiratory region, especially over the conchae and septum, is "erectile" or "cavernous": it swells or shrinks with the blood flow, which adjusts the warmth and humidity of the air.

ArteryFromSupplies
SphenopalatineMaxillary (ECA), terminal branch in the pterygopalatine fossaThe largest nasal artery. Enters via the sphenopalatine foramen. Posterior lateral nasal branches: much of the lateral wall (join the ethmoidal and facial branches in front). Posterior septal branches: over the roof to the septum; the largest runs down the septum to join the greater palatine and the superior labial septal branch
Greater palatineMaxillary (ECA)Front of the medial wall and floor, entering by the incisive canal; joins the sphenopalatine septal branch
Superior labialFacial (ECA)Lip; alar branch round the naris; septal branch to the front of the septum
Lateral nasalFacial (ECA)External nose; alar branches to the vestibule
Anterior ethmoidalOphthalmic (ICA)Through the medial orbital wall, along the cribriform plate groove, down a slit beside the crista galli; septal and lateral wall; ends as the external nasal branch between the nasal bone and lateral nasal cartilage
Posterior ethmoidalOphthalmic (ICA)Down through the cribriform plate; upper parts of the medial and lateral walls
  • The vessels anastomose widely.
  • Veins follow the arteries. Those with maxillary-artery branches drain to the pterygoid plexus; veins from the front join the facial vein. A nasal vein through the foramen cecum to the superior sagittal sinus is an emissary vein. Veins with the ethmoidal arteries drain to the superior ophthalmic vein, a large emissary vein that drains to the cavernous sinus.
In the clinic: epistaxis

At the front of the medial wall the greater palatine, sphenopalatine, superior labial and anterior ethmoidal arteries anastomose close to the surface. This is the main site of nosebleeds.

In the clinic: spread of infection

Emissary veins carry infection from outside the skull into the cranial cavity.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • The nasolacrimal duct opens into the inferior meatus; the frontal, maxillary and anterior ethmoid sinuses open into the middle meatus.
  • The sphenoidal sinus is the only sinus that does not open onto the lateral wall.
  • The superior and middle conchae are ethmoid; the inferior concha is its own bone.
  • The ethmoidal arteries come from the ICA system (ophthalmic); the sphenopalatine, greater palatine and facial branches from the ECA.
  • Parasympathetic fibres to the nasal glands travel in the facial nerve (greater petrosal), not the trigeminal.
  • The floor of the nose is the hard palate; the incisive canal links it to the incisive fossa.
  • Mucus is moved backward by cilia, not forward.
  • The maxillary sinus is the largest; it lies against the roots of the upper molar and premolar teeth, not the incisors.

Check yourself

  1. Which meatus lies between the middle and superior conchae?

    • A. Inferior meatus
    • B. Middle meatus
    • C. Superior meatus
    • D. Spheno-ethmoidal recess
    Show answer

    C. The superior meatus. The recess lies above the superior concha.

  2. Which sinus opens into the posterior wall of the spheno-ethmoidal recess?

    • A. Frontal
    • B. Maxillary
    • C. Posterior ethmoidal cells
    • D. Sphenoidal
    Show answer

    D. The sphenoidal sinus opens high on its front wall into the recess; posterior ethmoidal cells open into the superior meatus.

  3. Which structure forms the medial boundary of the ethmoidal infundibulum?

    • A. Ethmoidal bulla
    • B. Crista galli
    • C. Uncinate process
    • D. Perpendicular plate
    Show answer

    C. The uncinate process, which crosses the maxillary hiatus to reach the inferior concha.

  4. Which cartilage forms the front of the nasal septum?

    • A. Major alar cartilage
    • B. Minor alar cartilage
    • C. Septal cartilage
    • D. Lateral process of the alar cartilage
    Show answer

    C. The single midline septal cartilage forms the front of the septum.

  5. Which nerve supplies the frontal sinus?

    • A. Supra-orbital nerve from V1
    • B. Infra-orbital nerve from V2
    • C. Greater petrosal nerve
    • D. Nasopalatine nerve
    Show answer

    A. The supra-orbital branch of the ophthalmic nerve. Its blood supply is from the anterior ethmoidal arteries.

  6. Which artery supplies the sphenoidal sinus?

    • A. Pharyngeal branch of the maxillary artery
    • B. Anterior ethmoidal artery
    • C. Infra-orbital artery
    • D. Superior labial artery
    Show answer

    A. The sphenoidal sinuses are supplied by pharyngeal branches of the maxillary arteries.

  7. Which structure completely surrounds the choana with bone?

    • A. Palatine, pterygoid plate, vomer and sphenoid
    • B. Alar cartilages and maxilla
    • C. Septal cartilage and nasal bones
    • D. Ethmoid and frontal bones
    Show answer

    A. Palatine below, medial pterygoid plate laterally, vomer medially, sphenoid and vomer ala in the roof.

  8. Which nerve passes through the sphenopalatine foramen?

    • A. Anterior ethmoidal nerve
    • B. Olfactory nerve
    • C. Nasopalatine nerve
    • D. Infra-orbital nerve
    Show answer

    C. The nasopalatine nerve (V2) goes through the foramen with the sphenopalatine artery. It also passes through the incisive canal to the mouth.

  9. The posterior septal branches of the sphenopalatine artery anastomose in front with which arteries?

    • A. Greater palatine and superior labial septal branches
    • B. Posterior ethmoidal and lateral nasal
    • C. Infra-orbital and buccal
    • D. Ascending pharyngeal and facial
    Show answer

    A. The largest posterior septal branch runs down the septum to join the greater palatine and the septal branch of the superior labial.

  10. The superior ophthalmic vein, which drains the ethmoidal veins, empties into which structure?

    • A. Facial vein
    • B. Pterygoid plexus
    • C. Cavernous sinus
    • D. Superior sagittal sinus
    Show answer

    C. It is one of the largest emissary veins and drains into the cavernous sinus.

Key terms

Naris: front opening of the nose · Choana: back opening into the nasopharynx · Concha: curved bony shelf on the lateral wall · Meatus: channel under a concha · Spheno-ethmoidal recess: space above the superior concha · Ethmoidal bulla: bulge of the middle ethmoidal cells · Semilunar hiatus: groove in the middle meatus where sinuses open · Ethmoidal infundibulum: channel leading up to the frontonasal duct · Uncinate process: thin ethmoid leaflet bounding the infundibulum · Cribriform plate: perforated ethmoid roof plate · Crista galli: midline process anchoring the falx cerebri · Perpendicular plate: ethmoid part of the septum · Nasolacrimal duct: tear duct to the inferior meatus · Incisive canal: floor canal to the incisive fossa · Sphenopalatine foramen: link to the pterygopalatine fossa · Epistaxis: nosebleed · Osteomeatal complex: drainage area of the maxillary, frontal and anterior ethmoid sinuses · Emissary vein: vein linking outer and inner venous systems.
