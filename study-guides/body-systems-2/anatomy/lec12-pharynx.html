Pharynx

The musculofascial tube shared by air and food: its three parts, its skeletal attachments, the constrictor and longitudinal muscles, the gaps in its wall, the tonsils, and its blood supply and drainage.

Labobo topic: Anatomy Lec 12: Pharynx

In 60 seconds

  • The pharynx is a musculofascial half-cylinder joining the nasal and oral cavities to the larynx and esophagus. It is a common pathway for air and food. It ends at about CVI.
  • Three parts: nasopharynx (choanae), oropharynx (oropharyngeal isthmus), laryngopharynx (laryngeal inlet).
  • Three constrictors (superior, middle, inferior) overlap like stacked flower pots. All are supplied by the pharyngeal branch of the vagus [X]. They join posteriorly at the pharyngeal raphe.
  • Three longitudinal muscles (stylopharyngeus, salpingopharyngeus, palatopharyngeus) elevate the wall. Only stylopharyngeus is supplied by IX; the others by X.
  • Tonsils form Waldeyer's ring: pharyngeal (adenoids), palatine, lingual and tubal.
  • Upper pharynx: branches of the external carotid. Lower pharynx: inferior thyroid artery. Palatine tonsil: tonsillar branch of the facial artery, draining to the jugulodigastric nodes.

Overview and the three parts

The pharynx is attached above to the base of the skull. Below, at about vertebra CVI, it is continuous with the top of the esophagus. Its walls attach anteriorly to the margins of the nasal cavities, oral cavity and larynx.

These anterior relations divide it into three regions.

PartWhat opens into it
NasopharynxPosterior apertures (choanae) of the nasal cavities
OropharynxPosterior opening of the oral cavity (oropharyngeal isthmus)
LaryngopharynxSuperior aperture of the larynx (laryngeal inlet)
  • The cavity is also related anteriorly to the posterior one-third of the tongue and the posterior aspect of the larynx.
  • The pharyngotympanic tubes open into the lateral walls of the nasopharynx.
  • Lingual, pharyngeal and palatine tonsils lie on the deep surface of the wall.
  • A thin retropharyngeal space of loose connective tissue separates the pharynx from the vertebral column behind.
  • Waldeyer's ring is lymphoid tissue (mainly tonsils) in the nasopharynx and oropharynx. It is part of the mucosal immune system and guards the upper respiratory and digestive tracts. It includes the palatine tonsils, adenoids (pharyngeal tonsils), lingual tonsils and tubal tonsils.

The soft palate as a valve

The soft palate attaches to the posterior margin of the hard palate. It is usually counted as part of the roof of the oral cavity, but it also relates to the pharynx. It works as a "flutter valve":

  • Swings up (elevates): closes the pharyngeal isthmus and seals the nasopharynx from the oropharynx.
  • Swings down (depresses): closes the oropharyngeal isthmus and seals the oral cavity from the oropharynx.

Skeletal framework and attachments

The two sides of the wall are welded in the midline behind by the pharyngeal raphe, a vertical cord-like ligament. It descends from the pharyngeal tubercle on the skull base to CVI, where it blends with the posterior esophageal wall.

Attachment to the skull base

  1. Each arm starts at the posterior margin of the medial plate of the pterygoid process, just below the cartilaginous part of the pharyngotympanic tube.
  2. It crosses below the tube onto the petrous temporal bone, just medial to the roughening for levator veli palatini.
  3. It swings medially onto the occipital bone and meets its partner at the pharyngeal tubercle.

The result is an irregular C-shaped line whose open part faces the nasal cavities.

Anterior line of attachment (three parts)

PartCourse
FirstPosterior edge of the medial pterygoid plate, down the plate to the pterygoid hamulus, then along the pterygomandibular raphe to the mandible
Second (hyoid)Lower aspect of the stylohyoid ligament (styloid process to lesser horn), the lesser horn, then back along the whole upper surface of the greater horn
Third (larynx)Superior tubercle of the thyroid cartilage, down the oblique line to the inferior tubercle, then over the cricothyroid muscle along a fascial thickening to the cricoid cartilage

The pterygomandibular raphe is a cord of connective tissue between the tip of the pterygoid hamulus and a triangular roughening just behind the third molar of the mandible. It joins the superior constrictor with the buccinator.

Muscles of the pharyngeal wall

The wall is made of skeletal muscle and fascia. The muscles fall into two groups by fibre direction: constrictors (circular) and longitudinal muscles (vertical).

Constrictors

  • Three on each side, named by position. Posteriorly they join at the pharyngeal raphe.
  • They overlap like three flower pots stacked: the inferior overlaps the middle, and the middle overlaps the superior.
  • Together they narrow the cavity. Contracting sequentially from top to bottom in swallowing, they push the bolus into the esophagus.
  • All are supplied by the pharyngeal branch of the vagus nerve [X].
MuscleAnterior attachmentNotes
Superior constrictorPterygoid hamulus, pterygomandibular raphe, adjacent mandibleBrackets the upper pharynx. The palatopharyngeal sphincter (Passavant's ridge) is formed by palatopharyngeus and the superior constrictor
Middle constrictorLower stylohyoid ligament, lesser horn and upper surface of greater horn of hyoidIts posterior part overlaps the superior constrictor
Inferior constrictorOblique line of thyroid cartilage, cricoid cartilage, and the ligament between them crossing cricothyroidPosterior part overlaps the middle constrictor. Fibres blend into the esophageal wall. The cricoid parts bracket the narrowest part of the pharyngeal cavity

The palatopharyngeal sphincter arises from the anterolateral surface of the soft palate and circles the inner pharyngeal wall. It helps close the nasopharynx so food does not enter the nasal cavity. Velopharyngeal closure is when the soft palate and the lateral and posterior pharyngeal walls come together to close off the nasal from the oral cavity. It matters for speech, swallowing, blowing and whistling.

Longitudinal muscles

They are named by their origins. They descend into the pharyngeal wall and elevate it, pulling it up and over the bolus during swallowing.

MuscleOriginNerveNotes
StylopharyngeusMedial surface of the base of the styloid processGlossopharyngeal [IX]Descends between the superior and middle constrictors
SalpingopharyngeusInferior aspect of the pharyngotympanic (Eustachian, auditory) tubeVagus [X]Small muscle
PalatopharyngeusUpper surface of the palatine aponeurosis (soft palate)Vagus [X]Forms the palatopharyngeal arch; also depresses the palate and moves the fold to the midline to close the oropharyngeal isthmus

The palatine tonsil lies immediately anterior to the palatopharyngeal arch, so the arch is a landmark for finding it.

Fascia and gaps in the wall

Fascia sandwiches the muscles in two layers.

  • Buccopharyngeal fascia: thin, outside the muscles; part of the pretracheal layer of cervical fascia.
  • Pharyngobasilar fascia: much thicker, lining the inner surface.

Fascia reinforces the wall where muscle is missing. Above the superior constrictor the wall is almost all fascia, reinforced outside by the tensor and levator veli palatini.

GapWhat passes
Above the superior constrictorLevator veli palatini pierces the fascia below the pharyngotympanic tube into the soft palate. The tendon of tensor veli palatini turns around the pterygoid hamulus and passes through the origin of buccinator into the soft palate
Between superior and middle constrictors and the posterior mylohyoid (oropharyngeal triangle)Stylopharyngeus; muscles, nerves and vessels passing between the lateral region and the oral cavity, especially the tongue
Between middle and inferior constrictorsInternal laryngeal vessels and nerve to the thyrohyoid membrane
Below the inferior constrictorRecurrent laryngeal nerves and inferior laryngeal vessels, entering behind the inferior horn of the thyroid cartilage

Nasopharynx

  • Behind the choanae and above the soft palate. It is continuous below with the oropharynx at the pharyngeal isthmus, marked by a mucosal fold over the palatopharyngeal sphincter (part of the superior constrictor).
  • Elevating the soft palate and tightening the sphincter closes the isthmus in swallowing.
  • The pharyngeal tonsil is in the mucosa of the roof. When enlarged (adenoids) it can block the nasopharynx, so breathing is only through the mouth.
  • The pharyngotympanic tube opening is on the lateral wall, posterior to and slightly above the hard palate and lateral to the top of the soft palate.
FeatureWhat it is
Torus tubariusBulge formed by the posterior rim of the tube
Pharyngeal recessDeep recess behind the torus tubarius
Salpingopharyngeal foldSmall vertical fold descending from the torus; overlies salpingopharyngeus
Torus levatoriusBroad elevation from just under the tubal opening, onto the soft palate; overlies levator veli palatini

Oropharynx

  • Behind the oral cavity, below the soft palate and above the upper margin of the epiglottis.
  • The palatoglossal folds (over the palatoglossal muscles) mark the boundary with the oral cavity. The opening between them is the oropharyngeal isthmus. Just behind and medial are the palatopharyngeal folds.
  • The anterior wall below the isthmus is the upper part of the posterior third of the tongue, covered by the lingual tonsils.
  • The valleculae are paired pouches between the tongue base and the epiglottis, formed between a midline fold and two lateral folds joining tongue to epiglottis.
  • The palatine tonsil lies on the lateral wall, on the superior constrictor between the palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal arches. It is visible through the mouth just behind the palatoglossal fold.

Breathing and swallowing

  • Food held in the mouth: the isthmus is closed by soft palate depression, tongue back elevation and the two folds moving to the midline. You can breathe while chewing.
  • Swallowing: the isthmus opens, the palate rises, the larynx closes, and food goes to the esophagus. You cannot breathe and swallow together because the airway is shut at two sites, the pharyngeal isthmus and the larynx.

Laryngopharynx

  • From the upper margin of the epiglottis to the top of the esophagus at CVI.
  • The laryngeal inlet opens into the anterior wall. Below it, the anterior wall is the posterior aspect of the larynx.
  • The piriform fossae are recesses between the central larynx and the lateral thyroid lamina. They channel food and liquid around the raised laryngeal inlet into the esophagus.

Tonsils

Lymphoid tissue around the openings of the nasal and oral cavities forms Waldeyer's tonsillar ring, part of the body's defence.

TonsilSite
Pharyngeal (adenoids when enlarged)Midline, roof of the nasopharynx
PalatineEach side of the oropharynx, between the palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal arches, just behind the oropharyngeal isthmus
LingualMany lymphoid nodules on the posterior third of the tongue

Small nodules also occur in the pharyngotympanic tube near its opening and on the upper surface of the soft palate.

In the clinic: tonsillitis

Inflammation of the tonsils, acute or chronic. Symptoms: sore throat, fever, enlarged tonsils, trouble swallowing, enlarged neck nodes. Most cases are viral; about 5% to 40% are bacterial. Group A streptococcus causes strep throat.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • All constrictors are supplied by X. Only stylopharyngeus is supplied by IX.
  • The inferior constrictor overlaps the middle, which overlaps the superior. Do not reverse it.
  • The palatine tonsil is anterior to the palatopharyngeal arch.
  • The nasopharynx is above the soft palate, the oropharynx below it, and the laryngopharynx starts at the epiglottis.
  • The adenoids are the pharyngeal tonsil, not the palatine tonsils.
  • Upper pharynx is supplied from the external carotid; the lower pharynx from the inferior thyroid artery.

    3. The hyoid bone

  6. Which vessel gives the pharyngeal branches to the lower pharynx?

    2. Lingual artery
    3. Ascending pharyngeal artery
    4. Inferior thyroid artery
    Show answer

    D. It comes from the thyrocervical trunk. The other three are external carotid branches or their branches.

  7. The veins of the pharynx drain upward into the:

    1. Pterygoid plexus
    2. Facial vein only
    3. Retromandibular vein
    4. Subclavian vein
    Show answer

  8. The tendon of tensor veli palatini turns around which structure on its way into the soft palate?

    1. Pharyngeal tubercle
    2. Pterygoid hamulus
    3. Styloid process
    4. Lesser horn of the hyoid
    Show answer

    B. It turns around the pterygoid hamulus and passes through the origin of buccinator, in the gap above the superior constrictor.

  9. The retropharyngeal nodes lie between the nasopharynx and the:

    1. Hyoid bone
    2. Vertebral column
    3. Thyroid gland
    4. Mandible
    Show answer

    B. They are among the nodes draining the pharynx to the deep cervical nodes.

Key terms

Choanae
Posterior apertures of the nasal cavities, opening into the nasopharynx.
Oropharyngeal isthmus
Opening between the palatoglossal folds, from the mouth to the oropharynx.
Pharyngeal raphe
Midline posterior ligament from the pharyngeal tubercle to CVI.
Pterygomandibular raphe
Ligament from the pterygoid hamulus to the mandible, joining superior constrictor and buccinator.
Palatopharyngeal sphincter
Muscle band of palatopharyngeus and superior constrictor (Passavant's ridge).
Pharyngobasilar fascia
Thick inner fascial layer of the pharyngeal wall.
Oropharyngeal triangle
Gap between the upper constrictors and mylohyoid.
Torus tubarius
Bulge from the posterior rim of the pharyngotympanic tube.
Vallecula
Pouch between the tongue base and the epiglottis.
Piriform fossa
Recess beside the laryngeal inlet that guides food to the esophagus.
Waldeyer's ring
Ring of tonsils and lymphoid tissue round the pharynx.
Adenoids
Enlarged pharyngeal tonsil.
Jugulodigastric node
Node receiving lymph from the palatine tonsil.
