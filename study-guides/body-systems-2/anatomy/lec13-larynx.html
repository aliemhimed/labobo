Larynx

The cartilaginous valve and voice box capping the lower airway: its cartilages, ligaments, joints, cavity, intrinsic muscles, functions, and its blood supply and drainage.

Labobo topic: Anatomy Lec 13: Larynx

In 60 seconds

  • The larynx is a valve (sphincter) for the lower respiratory tract and an instrument for sound. Motor and sensory nerve: the vagus [X].
  • Cartilages: 3 unpaired (cricoid, thyroid, epiglottis) and 3 paired (arytenoid, corniculate, cuneiform).
  • The cricoid is a signet ring and the only cartilage that completely encircles the airway. The thyroid is the largest.
  • Two mucosal folds divide the cavity: vestibule (inlet to vestibular folds), a thin middle chamber (between vestibular and vocal folds), and the infraglottic cavity.
  • All intrinsic muscles are supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve except the cricothyroid (superior laryngeal branch). The posterior crico-arytenoid is the main abductor of the vocal folds.
  • Arteries: superior laryngeal (external carotid) and inferior laryngeal (subclavian). In an emergency, the median cricothyroid ligament can be perforated for an airway.

Overview

The larynx is a hollow musculoligamentous structure with a cartilaginous framework. It caps the lower respiratory tract. Its cavity is continuous below with the trachea and opens above into the pharynx, just behind and slightly below the tongue and the oropharyngeal isthmus.

  • It is built from three large unpaired cartilages (cricoid, thyroid, epiglottis), three pairs of smaller cartilages (arytenoid, corniculate, cuneiform), a fibro-elastic membrane and many intrinsic muscles.
  • It hangs from the hyoid bone above and attaches to the trachea below by membranes and ligaments.
  • It is highly mobile: extrinsic muscles move it up, down, forward and back.
  • In swallowing, its upward and forward movement helps close the laryngeal inlet and open the esophagus.
  • Motor and sensory supply: the vagus nerve [X].

Laryngeal cartilages

CartilageKey features
CricoidMost inferior; completely encircles the airway. Signet-ring shape: broad lamina behind, narrow arch in front. The posterior lamina has two shallow oval depressions (for posterior crico-arytenoid muscles) separated by a vertical ridge (for the esophagus). Two facets on each side: one on the sloping superolateral lamina for the arytenoid base, one on the lateral surface near the base for the inferior horn of the thyroid
ThyroidThe largest. Right and left laminae, widely separated behind, joined in front. The top of the fusion is the laryngeal prominence (Adam's apple). The superior thyroid notch lies just above it; a fainter inferior thyroid notch lies at the base. Each lamina has a superior horn and inferior horn. Lateral surface: oblique line with superior and inferior thyroid tubercles
EpiglottisLeaf-shaped. Its stem attaches to the back of the thyroid angle by the thyro-epiglottic ligament, about midway between the laryngeal prominence and the inferior thyroid notch. Projects posterosuperiorly; its upper margin is behind the pharyngeal part of the tongue. The lower half of its posterior surface forms the epiglottic tubercle
Arytenoid (paired)Pyramid-shaped with a base (concave; articulates with the cricoid facet) and an apex (articulates with a corniculate). Vocal process (anterior angle) takes the vocal ligament. Muscular process (lateral angle) takes the posterior and lateral crico-arytenoids. The anterolateral surface has two depressions for the vocalis and the vestibular ligament. The posterior surface is covered by the transverse arytenoid
Corniculate (paired)Small, conical. Bases sit on the arytenoid apices; apices point posteromedially
Cuneiform (paired)Small, club-shaped, anterior to the corniculates, in the membrane joining the arytenoids to the lateral margin of the epiglottis
  • The angle between the thyroid laminae is more acute in men (90°) than in women (120°), so the prominence is more obvious in men.
  • The superior thyroid notch and laryngeal prominence are palpable landmarks.
  • The superior horn is joined to the posterior end of the greater horn of the hyoid by the lateral thyrohyoid ligament.
  • The oblique line attaches the extrinsic muscles sternothyroid, thyrohyoid and the inferior constrictor.

Extrinsic and intrinsic ligaments

Extrinsic ligaments

  • Thyrohyoid membrane: tough fibro-elastic sheet from the superior margin of the thyroid cartilage up to the hyoid. An aperture on each side passes the superior laryngeal artery, the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve and lymphatics. Its posterior borders form the lateral thyrohyoid ligaments; its anterior midline thickening is the median thyrohyoid ligament. A small triticeal cartilage may lie in each lateral ligament.
  • Hyo-epiglottic ligament: midline of the epiglottis anterosuperiorly to the body of the hyoid.
  • Cricotracheal ligament: lower border of the cricoid to the first tracheal cartilage.

Intrinsic ligaments: the fibro-elastic membrane

It links the cartilages and completes the framework. It has a lower conus elasticus and an upper quadrangular membrane.

PartAttachmentsFree margin forms
Conus elasticus (cricovocal membrane)Cricoid arch below. Free upper margin attaches to the thyroid cartilage in front and the vocal processes of the arytenoids behindThe vocal ligament, under the vocal fold (true vocal cord). The anterior midline thickening is the median cricothyroid ligament, from the cricoid arch to the inferior thyroid notch
Quadrangular membraneLateral margin of the epiglottis to the anterolateral arytenoid, also the corniculateLower free margin is the vestibular ligament, under the vestibular fold (false vocal cord). Attached behind to the arytenoid's upper depression and in front to the thyroid angle just above the vocal ligament

A gap separates the vestibular ligament from the vocal ligament below it. From above, the vestibular ligament lies lateral to the vocal ligament, because it attaches to the arytenoid's anterolateral surface while the vocal ligament attaches to the vocal process.

Cavity of the larynx

The central cavity is a mucosa-lined tube, supported by the fibro-elastic membrane and cartilages.

Laryngeal inlet (superior aperture)

  • Opens into the front of the pharynx, just below and behind the tongue.
  • Anterior border: mucosa over the upper margin of the epiglottis.
  • Lateral borders: the aryepiglottic folds, enclosing the superior margins of the quadrangular membranes. Two tubercles on each posterolateral margin mark the cuneiform and corniculate cartilages.
  • Posterior border: a midline mucosal fold forming the interarytenoid notch between the two corniculate tubercles.

The inferior opening is continuous with the trachea, ringed completely by the cricoid, and horizontal. The inlet is oblique and points posterosuperiorly. The lower opening is always open; the inlet can be closed by the epiglottis moving down.

Three regions

The vestibular folds and vocal folds project medially and divide the cavity.

  1. Vestibule: laryngeal inlet to the vestibular folds (enclosing the vestibular ligaments).
  2. Middle chamber: very thin, between the vestibular folds above and the vocal folds below.
  3. Infraglottic cavity: from the vocal folds (enclosing the vocal ligaments) to the inferior opening.

Ventricles, saccules and the two openings

  • Mucosa of the middle chamber bulges sideways through the gap between the vestibular and vocal ligaments: the laryngeal ventricle.
  • A tubular extension, the laryngeal saccule, projects anterosuperiorly between the vestibular fold and thyroid cartilage, as high as the top of the thyroid cartilage. Its mucous glands lubricate the vocal folds.
  • Rima vestibuli: triangular opening between the vestibular folds; apex in front, base formed by the posterior wall.
  • Rima glottidis: similar but narrower triangle between the vocal folds; separates the middle chamber from the infraglottic cavity. Its base is the interarytenoid fold.
  • Both openings open and close by movement of the arytenoids and fibro-elastic membranes.

Cricothyroid ligament and laryngoscopy

In the clinic: emergency airway

If the airway is blocked above the vocal folds, the median cricothyroid ligament can be perforated to make an airway. Few structures lie between it and the skin, apart from small vessels and sometimes a pyramidal lobe of the thyroid.

In the clinic: laryngoscopy and intubation

Laryngoscopy is visualising the larynx with a laryngoscope. Intubation is passing a tube through the mouth or nose, past the vocal cords, into the trachea to help breathing. Direct laryngoscopy is used for intubation, surgery and resuscitation. Indirect laryngoscopy is used for diagnosis and checking vocal cord movement.

In the clinic: tracheostomy

Gray's notes that patients with a long-term tracheostomy cannot speak, because no air passes through the vocal cords.

Intrinsic muscles

They adjust vocal ligament tension, open and close the rima glottidis, change the vestibule size, close the rima vestibuli and help close the inlet. They act on the joints, change the epiglottis-arytenoid distance, pull on the vocal ligaments, and push the soft tissues of the quadrangular membranes toward the midline.

Function of the larynx

The larynx is an elaborate sphincter and a sound producer. The cavity size changes with the rima glottidis, rima vestibuli, vestibule and laryngeal inlet.

ActivityWhat happens
Quiet respirationInlet, vestibule, rima vestibuli and rima glottidis all open. Arytenoids abducted; rima glottidis triangular
Forced inspirationArytenoids rotate laterally, mainly by the posterior crico-arytenoids. The vocal folds abduct and the rima glottidis widens to a rhomboid shape
PhonationArytenoids and vocal folds adducted; air forced through the closed rima glottidis makes the folds vibrate. The upper airway and mouth modify the sound. Tension adjusted by vocalis and cricothyroid
Effort closureAir is held in the thorax to stabilise the trunk (heavy lifting, raising abdominal pressure). Rima glottidis, rima vestibuli and lower vestibule are completely closed: the airway is shut forcefully. Used in coughing, vomiting, defecation
SwallowingRima glottidis, rima vestibuli and vestibule closed; inlet narrowed. The larynx moves up and forward, the epiglottis swings down toward the arytenoids, and the esophagus (attached behind the cricoid lamina) opens. Food passes via the piriform fossae

Arteries and veins

VesselOrigin and course
Superior laryngeal arteryNear the upper margin of the thyroid cartilage, from the superior thyroid branch of the external carotid. Goes with the internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve through the thyrohyoid membrane
Inferior laryngeal arteryFrom the inferior thyroid branch of the thyrocervical trunk of the subclavian. Ascends with the recurrent laryngeal nerve in the groove between esophagus and trachea, entering deep to the margin of the inferior constrictor
Superior laryngeal veinsDrain to the superior thyroid veins, then the internal jugular veins
Inferior laryngeal veinsDrain to the inferior thyroid veins, then the left brachiocephalic vein
Lymphatics above the vocal foldsFollow the superior laryngeal artery to deep cervical nodes at the carotid bifurcation
Lymphatics below the vocal foldsDeep nodes with the inferior thyroid artery, or nodes in front of the cricothyroid ligament or upper trachea

Commonly confused

PairHow to tell them apart
Conus elasticus vs quadrangular membraneConus: lower, ends in the vocal ligament. Quadrangular: upper, ends in the vestibular ligament
Vocal fold vs vestibular foldVocal: true cord, lower, encloses vocal ligament. Vestibular: false cord, above, encloses vestibular ligament
Rima glottidis vs rima vestibuliGlottidis: between vocal folds, narrower. Vestibuli: between vestibular folds, above

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Only the cricothyroid is supplied by the superior laryngeal nerve. The rest of the intrinsic muscles use the recurrent laryngeal nerve.
  • The cricoid encircles the airway completely; the thyroid does not (open behind).
  • The posterior crico-arytenoid is the only primary abductor. The lateral one adducts.
  • The thyroid angle is 90° in men and 120° in women, not the other way round.
  • The laryngeal inlet is oblique; the inferior opening is horizontal and always open.
  • The vestibular ligament is lateral to the vocal ligament when seen from above.
  • The inferior laryngeal veins drain to the left brachiocephalic vein; the superior ones to the internal jugular.

Check yourself

  1. Which small paired cartilage lies in the membrane joining the arytenoids to the lateral margin of the epiglottis?

    1. Corniculate
    2. Cuneiform
    3. Triticeal
    4. Arytenoid
    Show answer

    B. The cuneiform is small and club-shaped, anterior to the corniculate. The corniculate is conical and sits on the arytenoid apex.

  2. The esophagus attaches to which part of the cricoid?

    1. The anterior arch
    2. The vertical ridge on the posterior lamina
    3. The arytenoid facet
    4. The inferior horn facet
    Show answer

    B. The posterior crico-arytenoids attach to the depressions on either side of the ridge.

  3. Which structure passes through the aperture in the thyrohyoid membrane?

    1. Recurrent laryngeal nerve
    2. External branch of the superior laryngeal nerve
    3. Internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve
    4. Inferior laryngeal artery
    Show answer

    C. It goes with the superior laryngeal artery and lymphatics.

  4. Which ligament forms the free margin of the conus elasticus?

    1. Vocal ligament
    2. Vestibular ligament
    3. Hyo-epiglottic ligament
    4. Cricotracheal ligament
    Show answer

    A. The vestibular ligament belongs to the quadrangular membrane.

  5. Forward movement and downward tilt of the thyroid cartilage on the cricoid will:

    1. Abduct the vocal folds
    2. Lengthen and tense the vocal ligaments
    3. Close the laryngeal inlet
    4. Relax the vocal ligaments
    Show answer

    B. This is the action of the cricothyroid muscle at the cricothyroid joint.

  6. Which space lies between the vestibular folds and the vocal folds?

    1. Vestibule
    2. Middle chamber of the laryngeal cavity
    3. Infraglottic cavity
    4. Piriform fossa
    Show answer

    B. It is very thin and gives off the ventricle on each side.

  7. The mucous glands in the laryngeal saccules mainly serve to:

    1. Lubricate the vocal folds
    2. Moisten the epiglottis
    3. Seal the laryngeal inlet
    4. Clear the trachea
    Show answer

    A. Mucus from the saccules lubricates the vocal folds.

  8. In quiet respiration, what is the position of the arytenoids?

    1. Adducted, with the vocal folds touching
    2. Tilted forward on the cricoid
    3. Abducted, with the rima glottidis triangular
    4. Rotated laterally to make a rhomboid opening
    Show answer

    C. In quiet respiration the arytenoids are abducted and the rima glottidis is triangular. The rhomboid shape belongs to forced inspiration.

  9. The inferior laryngeal artery enters the larynx:

    1. Through the thyrohyoid membrane
    2. Deep to the margin of the inferior constrictor
    3. Between the middle and inferior constrictors
    4. Through the median cricothyroid ligament
    Show answer

    B. It travels with the recurrent laryngeal nerve in the groove between the esophagus and trachea.

  10. Lymph from below the vocal folds drains to:

    1. Deep cervical nodes at the carotid bifurcation
    2. Nodes with the inferior thyroid artery, or in front of the cricothyroid ligament or upper trachea
    3. Jugulodigastric nodes only
    4. Retropharyngeal nodes
    Show answer

    B. Lymph from above the vocal folds goes to the carotid bifurcation nodes.

Key terms

Laryngeal prominence
Adam's apple, at the top of the thyroid fusion.
Oblique line
Ridge on the thyroid lamina for extrinsic muscles.
Vocal process
Anterior angle of the arytenoid; holds the vocal ligament.
Muscular process
Lateral angle of the arytenoid; holds the posterior and lateral crico-arytenoids.
Thyrohyoid membrane
Sheet from the thyroid cartilage to the hyoid.
Conus elasticus
Lower part of the fibro-elastic membrane; also called the cricovocal membrane.
Quadrangular membrane
Upper part of the fibro-elastic membrane.
Median cricothyroid ligament
Midline thickening of the conus elasticus, used for emergency airway access.
Aryepiglottic fold
Mucosal fold forming the lateral border of the laryngeal inlet.
Interarytenoid notch
Midline posterior notch of the laryngeal inlet.
Rima glottidis
Opening between the vocal folds.
Rima vestibuli
Opening between the vestibular folds.
Laryngeal ventricle
Lateral bulge of the middle chamber between the two folds.
Effort closure
Forceful complete closure of the larynx to hold air in the thorax.
