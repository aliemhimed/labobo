Face and scalp vasculature, maxillary and lingual arteries

The arteries, veins and lymph nodes of the face and scalp, the three parts of the maxillary artery and what each gives, the pterygoid plexus, the blood supply of the palate, nasal cavity and tongue, and the clinical links: danger area infections, scalp bleeding, extradural haematoma and nosebleeds.

Labobo topic: Anatomy Lec 10: Face & Scalp Vasculature, Maxillary & Lingual Arteries

In 60 seconds

  • The facial artery is the major artery of the face; pulse at the lower mandibular border just in front of masseter; it ends as the angular artery.
  • The facial vein (from the angular vein) has no valves and links to the cavernous sinus: the danger area of the face.
  • Scalp: supratrochlear and supra-orbital arteries (ophthalmic, front); superficial temporal, posterior auricular and occipital arteries (ECA, rest). Scalp wounds bleed from arteries and the vessels do not close.
  • The maxillary artery has three parts: before, on, and beyond the lateral pterygoid. Part 1 gives the middle meningeal and inferior alveolar arteries.
  • Middle meningeal artery: foramen spinosum; torn by a lateral blow = extradural haematoma.
  • Part 3 (pterygopalatine fossa): posterior superior alveolar, infra-orbital, greater palatine, pharyngeal, sphenopalatine, artery of the pterygoid canal. The sphenopalatine is the terminal branch and the largest nasal artery.
  • Epistaxis site: anterior medial nasal wall, where greater palatine, sphenopalatine, superior labial and anterior ethmoidal arteries meet.
  • Lingual artery: from the ECA at the tip of the greater horn of the hyoid, deep to hyoglossus, then between hyoglossus and genioglossus.

Learning objectives

Syllabus, Week XI: superficial vasculature of face and scalp; maxillary artery; lingual artery. Source: Gray's Anatomy for Students. By the end you can:

  • name the arteries, veins and lymph nodes of the face and of the scalp;
  • divide the maxillary artery into its three parts and list the branches of each;

  • name the arteries of the palate, nasal cavity and tongue and the veins that drain them.

Arteries of the face

Facial artery

  • The major vessel of the face. It arises from the front of the ECA, passes up through deep neck structures, passes behind the submandibular gland and appears at the lower border of the mandible.
  • It curves round the mandible just in front of masseter, where its pulse can be felt, then runs up and medially in a tortuous course beside the nose and ends as the angular artery at the medial corner of the eye.
  • On the face it is deep to platysma, risorius, zygomaticus major and minor; superficial to buccinator and levator anguli oris; it may pass over or through levator labii superioris.

Veins and lymphatics of the face

  • The facial vein is the major vein of the face. It starts near the medial corner of the orbit where the supratrochlear and supra-orbital veins form the angular vein.
  • It lies just behind the facial artery and descends with it to the lower mandibular border. There the two part: the vein passes superficial to the submandibular gland and enters the internal jugular vein.
  • It receives tributaries from the eyelids, external nose, lips, cheek and chin.
  • The transverse facial vein accompanies the artery and empties into the superficial temporal vein inside the parotid gland.
In the clinic: the danger area
  • The facial vein connects to the ophthalmic veins near the medial orbit, to veins entering the infra-orbital foramen, and through the deep facial vein to the pterygoid plexus.
  • These channels reach the cavernous sinus through emissary veins. There are no valves in the facial vein or any head vein, so blood can flow in any direction.
  • Infections of the face, mainly above the mouth (the "danger area"), must be treated with great care to stop infection spreading into the skull.

In the clinic: scalp laceration

The scalp has a very rich supply from the ECAs, so lacerations bleed heavily. The bleeding is mainly arterial: venous pressure is very low when upright, and the vessels do not retract and close because the connective tissue holds them open.

Maxillary artery: parts 1 and 2

The maxillary artery is the largest branch of the ECA in the neck. It supplies the nasal cavity, the lateral wall and roof of the oral cavity, all teeth and the dura. It starts inside the parotid gland, passes into the infratemporal fossa, and ascends obliquely to enter the pterygopalatine fossa through the pterygomaxillary fissure. It may pass lateral or medial to the lower head of the lateral pterygoid; if medial, it loops laterally between the two heads.

Middle meningeal artery and extradural haematoma

  • It rises vertically from the maxillary artery, passes between the sphenomandibular ligament (medial) and the lateral pterygoid (lateral), and just below the foramen spinosum passes between the two roots of the auriculotemporal nerve.
  • It enters the skull through the foramen spinosum. It is the largest meningeal vessel and supplies much of the dura, bone and marrow of the cranial walls.
In the clinic: extradural haematoma

Its branches run in the outer (periosteal) layer of the dura, tightly stuck to bone. A lateral blow can tear them. Blood under arterial pressure slowly strips the dura from the bone and forms an extradural haematoma.

Pterygoid plexus

  • A network of veins between the medial and lateral pterygoids, and between the lateral pterygoid and temporalis.
  • It receives veins from regions supplied by the maxillary artery: nasal cavity, roof and lateral wall of the oral cavity, all teeth, infratemporal muscles, paranasal sinuses, nasopharynx. The inferior ophthalmic vein can drain to it through the inferior orbital fissure.
  • Connections: behind, a short maxillary vein to the retromandibular vein; in front, the deep facial vein to the facial vein.
  • Small emissary veins link it to the cavernous sinus through the foramen ovale, the cartilage in the foramen lacerum and a small sphenoidal foramen medial to the lateral pterygoid plate.
In the clinic

Infection from the teeth can spread into the skull by these emissary veins. Because head veins have no valves, anaesthetic injected under pressure into the plexus can flow back into the tissues or into the cranial cavity.

Maxillary artery: part 3

In the pterygopalatine fossa the artery lies in front of the pterygopalatine ganglion. Its branches go with branches of the maxillary nerve [V2] and the ganglion. Together they supply much of the nasal cavity, the roof of the mouth and all upper teeth, and help supply the sinuses, oropharynx and orbital floor.

Veins of these areas run with the arteries into the pterygopalatine fossa, then through the pterygomaxillary fissure to the pterygoid plexus. The infra-orbital vein may go straight to the infratemporal fossa through the lateral inferior orbital fissure.

Palate lymph goes to the deep cervical nodes.

In the clinic: epistaxis

Branches of the greater palatine, sphenopalatine, superior labial and anterior ethmoidal arteries anastomose in the front of the medial wall, close to the surface. This is the main site of nosebleeds.

Lingual artery and tongue veins

  • The major artery of the tongue. It arises from the ECA in the neck beside the tip of the greater horn of the hyoid.
  • It bends upward, then loops down and forward deep to hyoglossus, passes through the aperture (oropharyngeal triangle) between the mylohyoid, superior constrictor and middle constrictor into the floor of the mouth.
  • It then runs forward between hyoglossus and genioglossus to the tongue tip. It also supplies the sublingual gland, gingiva and floor-of-mouth mucosa.
VeinCourse
Deep lingualVisible under the tongue. Goes with the artery in front, but behind is separated from it by hyoglossus. Runs with the hypoglossal nerve [XII] on the outer surface of hyoglossus, leaves through the oropharyngeal triangle, joins the IJV
Dorsal lingualFollows the artery between hyoglossus and genioglossus; drains to the IJV

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • The facial vein runs behind the artery and passes over the submandibular gland; the artery passes behind it.
  • The superficial temporal artery is the smaller terminal branch; the maxillary is the larger.
  • The supra-orbital and supratrochlear arteries come from the ICA system (ophthalmic); the other scalp arteries come from the ECA.
  • Scalp bleeding is arterial because the vessels cannot retract, not because the veins are numerous.
  • The middle meningeal artery is a branch of the maxillary artery, not the ICA.
  • The ethmoidal arteries to the nose come from the ICA system; the sphenopalatine comes from the ECA system.
  • The sphenopalatine artery, not the greater palatine, is the largest nasal artery.
  • The lingual artery passes deep to hyoglossus; the lingual vein with XII lies on its outer surface.

Check yourself

  1. The mental artery, which supplies the chin, is a branch of which artery?

    • A. Facial artery
    • B. Maxillary artery
    • C. Superficial temporal artery
    • D. Ophthalmic artery
    Show answer

    B. The maxillary artery gives the infra-orbital, buccal and mental arteries to the face.

  2. Which artery supplies the skin over the zygomatic bone through the zygomaticofacial foramen?

    • A. Transverse facial artery
    • B. Zygomaticofacial branch of the lacrimal branch of the ophthalmic artery
    • C. Infra-orbital artery
    • D. Superior labial artery
    Show answer

    B. It comes from the lacrimal branch of the ophthalmic artery, a branch of the ICA.

  3. Submental nodes receive lymph from which area?

    • A. Medial part of the lower lip and chin
    • B. Eyelids
    • C. Lateral cheek
    • D. Medial orbital corner
    Show answer

    A. They drain the medial lower lip and chin, from both sides.

  4. Which part of the maxillary artery gives the deep temporal and masseteric arteries?

    • A. First part
    • B. Second part
    • C. Third part
    • D. All parts equally
    Show answer

    B. The second part, related to the lateral pterygoid, gives deep temporal, masseteric, buccal and pterygoid branches.

  5. The accessory meningeal artery enters the cranial cavity through which foramen?

    • A. Foramen spinosum
    • B. Foramen ovale
    • C. Foramen lacerum
    • D. Jugular foramen
    Show answer

    B. Foramen ovale. The middle meningeal uses the foramen spinosum.

  6. Which structure does the middle meningeal artery pass between just below the foramen spinosum?

    • B. The two heads of the lateral pterygoid
    • C. Masseter and temporalis
    • D. The mylohyoid and hyoglossus
    Show answer

    A. It passes between the two roots of the auriculotemporal nerve, from V3.

  7. The artery of the pterygoid canal ends in the mucosa of which region?

    • A. Soft palate
    • B. Maxillary sinus
    • C. Nasopharynx
    • D. Nasal septum
    Show answer

    C. It runs back in the pterygoid canal and ends in nasopharyngeal mucosa after passing through the cartilage of the foramen lacerum.

  8. Which artery gives the external nasal branch that emerges between the nasal bone and the lateral nasal cartilage?

    • A. Posterior ethmoidal artery
    • B. Sphenopalatine artery
    • C. Anterior ethmoidal artery
    • D. Lateral nasal artery
    Show answer

    C. The anterior ethmoidal artery ends as the external nasal branch.

  9. Which of these arteries supplying the nose comes from the internal carotid system?

    • A. Sphenopalatine
    • B. Greater palatine
    • C. Superior labial
    • D. Posterior ethmoidal
    Show answer

    D. The ethmoidal arteries arise from the ophthalmic artery, a branch of the ICA.

  10. Which structure separates the deep lingual vein from the lingual artery in the posterior tongue?

    • A. Genioglossus
    • B. Mylohyoid
    • C. Hyoglossus
    • D. Middle constrictor
    Show answer

    C. Hyoglossus separates them; the vein lies on its outer surface with XII.

Key terms

Angular artery: end of the facial artery at the medial eye corner · Angular vein: start of the facial vein · Danger area: upper face where infection can reach the cavernous sinus · Pterygoid plexus: venous network round the pterygoid muscles · Emissary vein: valveless vein joining extracranial and intracranial veins · Pterygopalatine fossa: fossa holding the third part of the maxillary artery · Pterygomaxillary fissure: entrance to the pterygopalatine fossa · Sphenopalatine artery: terminal branch of the maxillary artery · Extradural haematoma: bleed between dura and bone · Epistaxis: nosebleed · Oropharyngeal triangle: gap bounded by mylohyoid, superior constrictor and middle constrictor · Mastoid node: retro-auricular node behind the ear · Incisive canal: canal from the palate to the nasal floor · Foramen spinosum: foramen for the middle meningeal artery.
