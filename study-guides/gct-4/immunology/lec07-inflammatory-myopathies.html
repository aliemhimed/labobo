Idiopathic inflammatory myopathies

Autoimmune inflammation of skeletal muscle: polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion body myositis and immune-mediated necrotising myopathy. Who gets them, the pattern of weakness, the skin signs of dermatomyositis, systemic features, antibodies such as anti-Jo-1, and how biopsy tells them apart.

Labobo topic: Immuno Lec 7: Inflammatory Myopathies

In 60 seconds

  • Four forms: polymyositis (PM), dermatomyositis (DM), inclusion body myositis (IBM), immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM).
  • Symmetric proximal weakness (hair combing, rising from a chair, stairs) with raised CK; later dysphagia and respiratory weakness.
  • PM and DM: women 2:1; IBM: older men, often asymmetric, finger flexors and knee extensors.
  • DM skin: heliotrope eyelid rash, Gottron papules, shawl, V and holster signs, mechanic’s hands. DM, PM and IMNM carry a cancer risk; ILD and heart involvement occur.
  • Anti-Jo-1 (histidyl-tRNA synthetase) is the commonest myositis antibody; anti-Mi-2 (DM), anti-SRP. Biopsy is the gold standard: DM = perimysial CD4+, perifascicular atrophy; PM = endomysial CD8+.

The four myopathies

Idiopathic inflammatory myopathies (IIM) are rare systemic diseases with progressive weakness from chronic skeletal muscle inflammation.

FormWhoKey features
DermatomyositisWomen (2:1); peaks 5–15 (juvenile) and 40–60 yearsProximal weakness + characteristic skin; skin may come first
PolymyositisWomen (2:1); 30–60 yearsProximal weakness, high CK, myositis on EMG and biopsy, none of the other forms’ features: a rare diagnosis of exclusion
Inclusion body myositisOlder men (2:1)Finger flexors and knee extensors, often asymmetric; no skin signs
Immune-mediated necrotising myopathyAdultsLike PM clinically; biopsy shows necrosis and regeneration with sparse inflammation

Traditional classification: I primary PM; II primary DM; III DM/PM with malignancy; IV childhood DM/PM; V myositis with another connective tissue disease; VI IBM; VII miscellaneous (eosinophilic, nodular).

Causes and differential diagnosis

  • Immunological: myositis-specific autoantibodies bind and inhibit enzymes that make proteins, for example anti-Jo-1 against histidyl-tRNA synthetase (attaches histidine to its tRNA). DM is probably antibody-mediated; PM is cell-mediated cytotoxicity. Associated with viral infections (HIV, HTLV-1, Coxsackie) and malignancy (paraneoplastic).
  • Drugs and toxins: corticosteroid myopathy (type II fibre atrophy, improves when the dose falls); colchicine myopathy and painful neuromyopathy.
  • Infections: some bacteria and parasites invade muscle directly.
  • Differential: drug-induced myopathy, neuromuscular disorders, muscular dystrophies, metabolic and endocrine myopathies. Age, sex, pattern of weakness and associated symptoms guide the diagnosis.

Muscle weakness

  • Symmetric proximal weakness and atrophy of the pelvic and shoulder girdles: trouble combing hair, reaching overhead, rising from a chair, climbing stairs. Neck flexors more than extensors; possible myalgia.
  • Advanced: oropharyngeal weakness (dysphagia, aspiration) and respiratory muscle weakness (restrictive lung disease, respiratory failure).

Skin signs of dermatomyositis

  • Heliotrope rash: violaceous erythema of the upper eyelids, often with periorbital oedema.
  • Gottron sign / papules: erythema or scaly papules over the extensor surfaces of hand joints, elbows and knees.
  • Shawl sign (upper back, neck, shoulders), V sign (upper chest, anterior neck), holster sign (hips, lateral thighs), mid-face erythema (photodistributed).
  • Mechanic’s hands: thick, cracked skin on finger sides and palms with “dirty” fissures.
  • Periungual telangiectasia; poikiloderma in chronic disease; calcinosis cutis in children.

Systemic manifestations

  • Interstitial lung disease.
  • Heart (mainly DM and PM): myocarditis, AV block, heart failure.
  • Constitutional symptoms, Raynaud, arthritis, GI symptoms (pain, haematemesis, melena).
  • Increased risk of malignancy (DM, PM, IMNM), especially in DM: look for occult cancer.

Diagnosis

  • Labs: raised CK (also aldolase, myoglobin, LDH, AST, ALT); ESR sometimes raised. ANA positive in 80–90% but not specific. Anti-Jo-1 (commonest myositis antibody), anti-Mi-2 (specific for DM), anti-SRP.
  • EMG: shows whether weakness comes from muscle or from nerve.
  • MRI: finds inflammation when muscle involvement is subtle (and guides biopsy).
  • Muscle biopsy: the gold standard, and the only way to tell PM from IMNM.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Anti-Jo-1 targets histidyl-tRNA synthetase.
  • New dermatomyositis in an adult: screen for occult cancer.
  • Steroid myopathy shows type II fibre atrophy and improves when the dose is lowered.
  • Neck flexors are weaker than extensors.
  • EMG separates muscle from nerve disease; biopsy is the gold standard.

Key terms

Dermatomyositis: myositis with characteristic rash · Polymyositis: myositis without skin signs · Inclusion body myositis: asymmetric myositis of older men · IMNM: necrotising myopathy with little inflammation · Heliotrope rash: violaceous eyelid rash · Gottron papules: scaly papules over knuckles · Mechanic’s hands: cracked finger skin · Anti-Jo-1: anti-histidyl-tRNA synthetase · Anti-Mi-2: DM-specific antibody · Perimysium: sheath around a fascicle · Endomysium: sheath around a fibre · Perifascicular atrophy: DM biopsy sign · CK: creatine kinase.
