Muscles of the back

The three groups of back muscles: superficial (trapezius, latissimus dorsi, levator scapulae, rhomboids) moving the shoulder, intermediate (serratus posterior) moving the ribs, and the deep intrinsic muscles in four layers (splenius, erector spinae, transversospinales, segmental) moving the spine.

Labobo topic: Lec 11: Muscles of the Back

In 60 seconds

  • Three groups: superficial (shoulder), intermediate (thoracic cage), deep (vertebral column).
  • Superficial and intermediate are extrinsic (anterior rami or CN XI); deep muscles are the intrinsic, true back muscles (posterior rami).
  • Nerves: trapezius CN XI; latissimus thoracodorsal; levator scapulae and rhomboids dorsal scapular; serratus posterior intercostal.
  • Serratus posterior superior elevates ribs 2–5; inferior depresses ribs 9–12.
  • Deep layers: splenius → erector spinae (iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis, lateral to medial) → transversospinales (semispinalis, multifidus, rotatores) → segmental (interspinales, intertransversarii).
  • Splenius turns the head to the same side; semispinalis to the opposite side.

Learning objectives

Syllabus week XIII: muscles of the back, superficial and deep, and their functions. By the end you can:

  • sort the back muscles into their groups and say what each group moves and which nerves supply it;
  • give the origin, insertion, nerve and action of each superficial and intermediate muscle;
  • name the layers of the deep back muscles and the parts and actions of each.

Superficial group

These lie under the skin and superficial fascia, run from the vertebral column to the shoulder bones (clavicle, scapula, humerus), and so all move the upper limb. Trapezius and latissimus dorsi are the most superficial; trapezius covers the rhomboids and levator scapulae.

Watch out

This deck gives serratus posterior inferior as T11–L3; the imaging lecture on back muscles gives T11–L2. Texts vary; T11–L2 is the commoner textbook figure. Expect "lower thoracic and upper lumbar spines".

Deep (intrinsic) muscles

They lie on both sides of the vertebral column, deep to the thoracolumbar fascia, from the skull to the pelvis. They maintain posture and move the vertebral column. All are supplied by posterior rami.

Erector spinae

A column posterolateral to the spine, between the spinous processes and the costal angles. Common tendinous origin: lower thoracic and lumbar vertebrae, sacrum, posterior iliac crest, sacroiliac and supraspinous ligaments. Each column is named by its upper attachment (lumborum, thoracis, cervicis, capitis).

ColumnPositionPartsInsertion
IliocostalisLateralLumborum, thoracis, cervicisCostal angles of ribs; cervical transverse processes
LongissimusMiddle; the largestThoracis, cervicis, capitisLower ribs; transverse processes C2–T12; mastoid process
SpinalisMedial; the smallestThoracis, cervicis (absent in some), capitisSpinous processes of C2 and T1–T8; occipital bone

All three: one side bends the spine laterally; both sides extend the spine and head. Posterior rami.

Mnemonic

"I Love Spaghetti", lateral to medial: Iliocostalis, Longissimus, Spinalis.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Trapezius is supplied by a cranial nerve (XI).
  • Intrinsic back muscles are supplied by posterior rami; extrinsic by anterior rami or named nerves.
  • Latissimus dorsi medially rotates and extends the arm.
  • Multifidus is thickest in the lumbar region.
  • Semispinalis spans 4–6 segments; multifidus 2–4.

Key terms

Extrinsic back muscles: superficial and intermediate groups · Intrinsic (true) back muscles: deep group, posterior rami · Ligamentum nuchae: midline ligament of the neck · Thoracolumbar fascia: fascia covering the deep back muscles · Accessory nerve: CN XI · Dorsal scapular nerve: levator scapulae and rhomboids · Thoracodorsal nerve: latissimus dorsi · Spinotransversales: splenius muscles · Erector spinae: iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis · Costal angle: bend of the rib behind · Transversospinales: semispinalis, multifidus, rotatores · Mamillary process: knob on lumbar articular processes · Segmental muscles: interspinales, intertransversarii · Proprioception: sense of position.
