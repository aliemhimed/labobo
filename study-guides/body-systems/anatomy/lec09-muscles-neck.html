Fascia and muscles of the neck; neck triangles

Platysma and sternocleidomastoid; the suprahyoid and infrahyoid muscles with their nerves; the suboccipital muscles and triangle; the prevertebral and scalene muscles; the layers of cervical fascia; and the anterior and posterior triangles with all their subdivisions.

Labobo topic: Lec 9: Muscles of the Neck

In 60 seconds

  • Platysma (CN VII) and SCM (CN XI) are superficial. SCM alone: flexes to the same side and rotates the face to the opposite side.
  • Suprahyoid: digastric, stylohyoid, mylohyoid, geniohyoid; they lift the hyoid and open the mouth. Nerves: V3 (mylohyoid, anterior digastric), VII (posterior digastric, stylohyoid), C1 via XII (geniohyoid).
  • Infrahyoid: sternohyoid, omohyoid (superficial), sternothyroid, thyrohyoid (deep); ansa cervicalis except thyrohyoid (C1 via XII).
  • Four suboccipital muscles (suboccipital nerve) extend and rotate the head; the suboccipital triangle holds the vertebral artery.
  • Scalenes: anterior and middle lift rib 1; posterior lifts rib 2; middle is the largest.
  • SCM splits the neck into anterior (muscular, carotid, submandibular, submental) and posterior (occipital, omoclavicular) triangles.

Learning objectives

Syllabus week XI: fascia and muscles of the neck; superficial, middle and deep muscles; neck triangles. By the end you can:

  • name the neck muscles by group, with attachments, actions and nerves;
  • describe the layers of the deep cervical fascia;
  • give the borders, floor and contents of the suboccipital triangle;
  • give the borders of the anterior and posterior triangles and each subdivision.

Groups of neck muscles

Neck muscles mainly move the head in all directions. By position they are anterior, lateral and posterior, subdivided by depth and function. By depth: superficial (platysma, SCM), middle (suprahyoid and infrahyoid) and deep (prevertebral, scalenes, suboccipital).

Suprahyoid muscles

With the mandible fixed they elevate the hyoid and larynx for swallowing and voice; with the hyoid fixed they depress the mandible and open the mouth.

MuscleOrigin → insertionActionNerve
DigastricAnterior belly: digastric fossa of mandible. Posterior belly: mastoid process. Intermediate tendon slung to the hyoidDepresses mandible, elevates hyoidAnterior: V3 (via the inferior alveolar nerve). Posterior: VII
StylohyoidStyloid process → lateral hyoid; just above the posterior digastric bellyInitiates swallowing, pulling the hyoid back and upVII
MylohyoidMylohyoid line of mandible → hyoid; broad, triangularForms the floor of the mouth; elevates hyoid and floorV3 (via the inferior alveolar nerve)
GeniohyoidInferior mental spine → hyoid; deep to mylohyoidDepresses mandible, elevates hyoidC1 fibres running with XII
Watch out

The slides give the nerve of mylohyoid and the anterior digastric as the "inferior alveolar nerve". Strictly it is the nerve to mylohyoid, a branch of the inferior alveolar nerve (V3). Either way the answer is V3.

Suboccipital muscles and triangle

Four muscles under the occipital bone, deep to SCM, trapezius, splenius and semispinalis. All are supplied by the suboccipital nerve (posterior ramus of C1) and together extend and rotate the head.

Scalene muscles

Three paired muscles in the lateral neck. They form part of the floor of the posterior triangle, are accessory muscles of respiration and flex the neck: one side gives same-side flexion and opposite-side rotation; both sides flex.

Commonly confused

PairHow to tell them apart
Anterior vs posterior digastricAnterior: mandible, V3. Posterior: mastoid, VII
Geniohyoid vs thyrohyoidBoth C1 via XII; one suprahyoid, one infrahyoid
Rectus capitis posterior major vs minorMajor from C2; minor from C1, more medial
Obliquus capitis superior vs inferiorInferior has no skull attachment (C2 to C1)
Submandibular vs submentalSubmandibular: between the digastric bellies under the mandible. Submental: between the two anterior bellies, midline

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • SCM turns the face to the opposite side.
  • Thyrohyoid is the infrahyoid muscle not supplied by the ansa cervicalis.
  • The posterior scalene inserts on rib 2.
  • Stylohyoid and the posterior digastric are facial nerve.
  • The vertebral artery lies in the suboccipital triangle.

Key terms

Platysma: superficial neck sheet (CN VII) · Sternocleidomastoid: divides the neck into triangles (CN XI) · Suprahyoid / infrahyoid: above / below the hyoid · Intermediate tendon: link between two bellies · Ansa cervicalis: C1–C3 loop to the infrahyoids · Deglutition: swallowing · Suboccipital nerve: posterior ramus of C1 · Inferior nuchal line: attachment of the recti capitis · Prevertebral muscles: head and neck flexors in front of the spine · Scalene tubercle: on rib 1 · Investing layer: outer deep fascia · Pretracheal layer: around the visceral column · Prevertebral layer: around the spine · Carotid sheath: artery, vein, vagus · Omoclavicular triangle: supraclavicular triangle.
