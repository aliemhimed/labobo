Muscles of the upper limb

Girdle and shoulder muscles (deltoid, rotator cuff, teres major), the walls of the axilla, the arm (musculocutaneous and radial nerves), the forearm flexors (median and ulnar) and extensors (radial), and the intrinsic muscles of the hand, ending with the carpal tunnel.

Labobo topic: Lec 13: Muscles of the Upper Limb

In 60 seconds

  • Rotator cuff (SITS): supraspinatus (abducts 0–15°), infraspinatus and teres minor (lateral rotation), subscapularis (medial rotation, lesser tubercle). Deltoid abducts beyond 15° (axillary nerve).
  • Arm: anterior compartment (biceps, coracobrachialis, brachialis) = musculocutaneous; triceps = radial. Biceps supinates; brachialis flexes the elbow.
  • Anterior forearm: superficial muscles from the medial epicondyle; mostly median nerve, except FCU and the medial half of FDP (ulnar).
  • Posterior forearm: all radial nerve; superficial extensors from the lateral epicondyle. Brachioradialis is an extensor-group muscle that flexes the elbow.
  • Hand: thenar = median (recurrent branch); hypothenar, interossei, adductor pollicis, medial two lumbricals = ulnar; lateral two lumbricals = median.
  • Lumbricals flex MCP and extend IP; dorsal abduct, palmar adduct (middle finger is the axis).
  • Carpal tunnel: carpal bones + transverse carpal ligament, holding flexor tendons and the median nerve.

Learning objectives

Syllabus week XV: muscles of the upper limb; girdle muscles; brachial, antebrachial and hand muscles. By the end you can:

  • give the attachments, actions and nerves of the shoulder, arm, forearm and hand muscles;
  • name the walls of the axilla;
  • predict which muscles a median, ulnar, radial, axillary or musculocutaneous nerve injury weakens;
  • describe the carpal tunnel and how carpal tunnel syndrome develops.

The carpal tunnel

A narrow fibro-osseous passage on the palmar side of the wrist, bounded by the carpal bones and the transverse carpal ligament (flexor retinaculum). It holds the flexor tendons (FDS, FDP, FPL) and the median nerve. The ulnar nerve and artery pass outside it, in Guyon's canal.

  1. Pressure inside the tunnel rises.
  2. The contents, especially the median nerve, are compressed.
  3. Blood flow to the nerve falls and its small vessels change.
  4. Inflammation, oedema and hypoxia follow.
  5. Axons degenerate.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Supraspinatus starts abduction (0–15°); deltoid continues.
  • Subscapularis is the only cuff muscle on the lesser tubercle and the only medial rotator of the four.
  • Biceps inserts on the radial tuberosity; brachialis on the ulnar tuberosity.
  • Only FDP flexes the DIP joints.
  • Brachioradialis is supplied by the radial nerve but flexes the elbow.
  • FCU and ECU both adduct the wrist; FCR and ECRL/ECRB abduct it.

Key terms

Rotator cuff: SITS muscles stabilising the shoulder · Glenoid fossa: socket of the shoulder · Greater / lesser tubercle: humeral attachments of the cuff · Axilla: armpit space with four walls · Bicipital aponeurosis: roof of the cubital fossa · Cubital fossa: hollow in front of the elbow · Common flexor / extensor origin: medial / lateral epicondyle · Supination / pronation: palm up / palm down · Extensor hood: tendon expansion over the finger · Thenar / hypothenar eminence: thumb / little-finger muscle mass · Lumbricals: from FDP tendons to the hoods · Interossei: between the metacarpals · Flexor retinaculum: transverse carpal ligament · Carpal tunnel: passage of the median nerve · Guyon's canal: passage of the ulnar nerve.
