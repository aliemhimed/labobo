Abdominal muscles and the inguinal canal

The layers of the abdominal wall and its superficial fascia; the three flat muscles and two vertical muscles of the anterolateral wall; the five muscles of the posterior wall; and the inguinal canal: its walls, rings and contents, and direct and indirect inguinal hernias.

Labobo topic: Lec 12: Abdominal Muscles & Inguinal Canal

In 60 seconds

  • Wall layers: skin → superficial fascia (Camper's fatty, Scarpa's membranous) → muscles → transversalis fascia → extraperitoneal fat → peritoneum.
  • Flat muscles, outside in: external oblique (fibres down and in), internal oblique (up and in), transversus abdominis (transverse). Their aponeuroses meet at the linea alba.
  • Vertical muscles: rectus abdominis (tendinous intersections, linea semilunaris) and pyramidalis (tenses the linea alba, T12).
  • Posterior wall: quadratus lumborum, psoas major, psoas minor (60%), iliacus, diaphragm. Iliopsoas → lesser trochanter, the main hip flexor.
  • Inguinal canal: anterior wall external oblique aponeurosis; posterior wall transversalis fascia; roof internal oblique and transversus; floor inguinal ligament.
  • Deep ring above the midpoint of the inguinal ligament; superficial ring above the pubic tubercle. Contents: spermatic cord or round ligament, ilioinguinal nerve, genital branch of genitofemoral.
  • Indirect hernia: through the deep ring (congenital). Direct: through the posterior wall, in Hesselbach's triangle (acquired).

Learning objectives

The abdominal wall and its layers

The wall encloses the abdominal cavity and has anterolateral and posterior parts. It holds the viscera in place against gravity, protects them, helps forced expiration by pushing the viscera up, and raises intra-abdominal pressure for coughing, vomiting and defecation. In inspiration the diaphragm contracts and the abdominal muscles relax; in forced expiration the reverse.

  1. Skin
  2. Superficial fascia: fatty layer (Camper's) and membranous layer (Scarpa's)
  3. Muscles: external oblique, internal oblique, transversus abdominis
  4. Transversalis fascia
  5. Extraperitoneal fat (fascia)
  6. Parietal peritoneum

Scarpa's membranous layer continues below as the superficial fascia of the penis, the dartos fascia and the superficial perineal (Colles') fascia, and attaches to the fascia lata of the thigh and the ischiopubic rami.

The syllabus also names a "mixed area"; none of the decks explains it, so it is not covered here.

Flat muscles

Three flat muscles on each side, stacked with fibres crossing in different directions, which strengthens the wall and lowers the risk of hernia. In front, each becomes an aponeurosis covering rectus abdominis; the aponeuroses interlace in the midline as the linea alba, from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis.

Posterior wall muscles

Five muscles: iliacus, psoas major, psoas minor, quadratus lumborum and the diaphragm.

The inguinal canal

A short passage running inferiorly and medially through the lower abdominal wall, above and parallel to the inguinal ligament. It carries structures from the abdomen to the external genitalia and is a weak spot where hernias are common.

Rings and contents

RingWhereFormed by
Deep (internal)Above the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, lateral to the inferior epigastric vesselsInvagination of transversalis fascia, which gives a covering to the contents
Superficial (external)Just above the pubic tubercleTriangular opening in the external oblique aponeurosis; intercrural fibres stop it widening

The spermatic cord enters through the deep ring and leaves through the superficial ring. Contents of the canal:

  • Spermatic cord (males): ductus deferens, testicular artery and pampiniform plexus, cremasteric vessels, artery to the ductus, with external spermatic, cremasteric and internal spermatic fasciae.
  • Round ligament of the uterus (females): from the uterine horn to the labia majora.
  • Both sexes: ilioinguinal nerve and the genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve.

Inguinal hernias

Weakness of the anterior abdominal wall lets abdominal contents push out: an inguinal hernia. The weakness is commonest in Hesselbach's triangle, bounded by the inferior epigastric artery, rectus abdominis and the inguinal ligament.

IndirectDirect
RouteThrough the deep ring into the canalStraight through the posterior wall of the canal
Relation to inferior epigastric vesselsLateralMedial (in Hesselbach's triangle)
OriginCongenitalAcquired
In the clinic

A bulge in the groin that appears on coughing is an inguinal hernia. A child's hernia is usually indirect (along the path of the testis); an older man's bulge medial to the inferior epigastric vessels is direct.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • The floor of the canal is the inguinal ligament; the posterior wall is transversalis fascia.
  • Iliacus is supplied by the femoral nerve, psoas major by anterior rami L1–L3.
  • Pyramidalis tenses the linea alba and is supplied by T12.
  • Quadratus lumborum fixes rib 12 during inspiration.
  • The round ligament ends in the labia majora.

Key terms

Camper's fascia: fatty superficial fascia · Scarpa's fascia: membranous superficial fascia · Transversalis fascia: fascia deep to transversus abdominis · Aponeurosis: flat tendon sheet · Linea alba: midline raphe · Linea semilunaris: lateral border of rectus · Tendinous intersections: bands across rectus · Conjoint tendon: joined internal oblique and transversus aponeuroses · Iliopsoas: psoas major + iliacus · Inguinal ligament: floor of the canal · Deep / superficial inguinal ring: entry / exit of the canal · Intercrural fibres: strengthen the superficial ring · Spermatic cord: testicular vessels, ductus deferens and coverings · Round ligament: female canal content · Hesselbach's triangle: site of direct hernia.
