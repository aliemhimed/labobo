The muscular system and the muscles of the head

The three kinds of muscle tissue, how a skeletal muscle is built and named, then the head: the facial muscles in their five groups (all facial nerve), occipitofrontalis and the auricular muscles, and the four muscles of mastication (all mandibular nerve).

Labobo topic: Lec 8: Muscles of the Head & Face

In 60 seconds

  • Muscle tissue: skeletal (striated, voluntary), cardiac (striated, heart), smooth (no stripes, hollow organs and vessels). Properties: excitability, contractility, extensibility, elasticity.
  • Wrappings: endomysium (fibre) → perimysium (fascicle) → epimysium (muscle) → fascia; tendons attach muscle to bone.
  • About 20 facial (mimetic) muscles lie under the skin, from skull to skin; all facial nerve (CN VII), supplied by the facial artery.
  • Five facial groups: oral, nasal, orbital, cranium and neck, auricular.
  • Buccinator presses the cheek to the teeth: a facial muscle (CN VII), not a muscle of mastication.
  • Mastication: masseter, temporalis, medial and lateral pterygoid; 1st pharyngeal arch, mandibular nerve (V3). All close the jaw except the lateral pterygoid, which protracts it and depresses the chin.

Learning objectives

Syllabus week X: overview of the muscular system; groups of muscles; muscles of the head: cranial and facial muscles; muscles of mastication and of facial expression. By the end you can:

  • compare the three muscle tissues and describe the structure of a skeletal muscle;
  • explain how muscles are named;
  • give the attachments, actions and nerves of the main facial and cranial muscles;
  • give the attachments, actions and nerve of the four muscles of mastication.

Skeletal muscle and bone together form the musculoskeletal system for voluntary movement. All muscle tissue has four properties: excitability (responds to stimuli), contractility, extensibility (stretches without tearing) and elasticity (returns to its shape).

Structure of a skeletal muscle

  1. Muscle cells form muscle fibres, each wrapped in endomysium.
  2. Bundles of fibres form fascicles, wrapped in perimysium.
  3. Many fascicles form the muscle, wrapped in epimysium.
  4. Muscles with their epimysium are covered by fascia; tendons attach them to bone.

Facial muscles: overview

  • About 20 flat skeletal muscles under the skin of the face and scalp. Most arise from bone or fibrous tissue of the skull and insert into skin.
  • They make smiling, grinning and frowning: the muscles of facial expression (mimetic muscles).
  • All are supplied by the facial nerve (CN VII) and the facial artery.
  • Five groups: oral (buccolabial), nasal, orbital (eyelid), cranium and neck, auricular (external ear).

Oral group

These move the mouth and lips, for speech, singing and whistling.

  • Orbicularis oris: the main sphincter of the lips; from the maxilla and other cheek muscles to the skin and mucosa of the lips; closes the lips and narrows the mouth.
  • Buccinator: thin and square, between maxilla and mandible, deep to the other facial muscles; fibres run inferomedially to blend with orbicularis oris. It pulls the cheek against the teeth so food does not collect there.
  • Minor muscles, upper group: risorius, zygomaticus major and minor, levator labii superioris, levator labii superioris alaeque nasi, levator anguli oris. Lower group: depressor anguli oris, depressor labii inferioris, mentalis.
  • All are supplied by buccal branches of the facial nerve.

Occipitofrontalis and auricular muscles

  • Occipitofrontalis covers the scalp from the eyebrows to the superior nuchal lines: a frontal and an occipital part joined by the epicranial aponeurosis (galea aponeurotica). Each part has a pair of quadrangular bellies.
  • Frontal part: from the skin of the eyebrows; joins the aponeurosis at the level of the coronal suture. With the forehead fixed it pulls the scalp forward and wrinkles the forehead; with the aponeurosis fixed it raises the eyebrows (surprise).
  • Occipital part: from the lateral two-thirds of the superior nuchal line; joins the aponeurosis at the lambdoid suture. It retracts the scalp. Both parts: facial nerve.
  • Auricular muscles (anterior, superior, posterior): thin, fan-shaped, linking the auricle to the scalp. Anterior: epicranial aponeurosis to the spine of the helix. Posterior: mastoid process to the eminentia conchae. Superior: aponeurosis to the upper auricle. Facial nerve; mainly the posterior auricular artery; they move the ear slightly when smiling or yawning.

Muscles of mastication

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Facial expression = CN VII; mastication = V3.
  • Buccinator is not a muscle of mastication.
  • The lateral pterygoid inserts on the neck of the mandible; temporalis on the coronoid process.
  • Orbicularis oculi has three parts; the orbital part closes the eye tightly.
  • Masseter is the most powerful; nasalis is the largest nasal muscle.

Key terms

Fascicle: bundle of muscle fibres · Endo-, peri-, epimysium: wrappings of fibre, fascicle, muscle · Tendon: attaches muscle to bone · Mimetic muscles: muscles of facial expression · Tarsal plates: fibrous plates of the eyelids · Nasalis: compressor and dilator of the nostril · Orbicularis oris: lip sphincter · Buccinator: cheek muscle · Modiolus: meeting point of muscles at the angle of the mouth · Epicranial aponeurosis (galea): tendon sheet of the scalp · Temporomandibular joint: jaw joint · Protraction: moving the jaw forward · Retraction: moving it back · Mandibular nerve (V3): nerve of mastication · Pharyngeal arch: embryonic origin of head muscles.
