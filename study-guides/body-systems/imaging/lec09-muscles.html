Muscle radiology and imaging techniques

What muscle is made of, and how it looks on each method: a plain radiograph (soft-tissue density), ultrasound (hypoechoic with bright perimysium; choosing a probe), CT (+35 to +55 HU, feathery) and MRI (the reference tissue, with fat bright on T1).

Labobo topic: Lec 9: Muscle Radiology & Techniques

In 60 seconds

  • Muscle is mesodermal; the body has more than 600 muscles, about 40% of body weight.
  • Wrappings: endomysium (fibre) → perimysium (fascicle) → epimysium (whole muscle).
  • X-ray: muscle is non-specific soft-tissue density, seen only where fat outlines it.
  • Ultrasound: muscle is hypoechoic with bright lines of perimysium; muscle needs the linear probe (5–10 MHz).
  • CT: soft-tissue density with a feathery look; muscle = +35 to +55 HU.
  • MRI: best soft-tissue contrast; normal muscle is the reference (isointense). Fat is bright on T1 and dark on fat-saturated T2; fluid is bright on T2.

Learning objectives

Muscle tissue

  • Muscle is a specialised tissue of mesodermal origin. The body has more than 600 muscles; the slide gives muscle as 40–50% of adult body weight in one line and about 40% in the next.
  • Properties: contractility (shortening), extensibility, elasticity, flexibility, conductivity. Muscle cells are long and called muscle fibres.
  • Three kinds: striated (skeletal), smooth and cardiac; smooth and cardiac move involuntarily.
  • Skeletal muscle: hundreds to thousands of fibres grouped in fascicles. Each level has a connective tissue wrap: endomysium round each fibre, perimysium round each fascicle, epimysium round the whole muscle.
  • These layers continue into tendons and aponeuroses, support the fibres against the force of contraction, and carry nerves and vessels.

Plain radiograph

Muscle has non-specific soft-tissue density. It cannot be told apart from neighbouring soft tissue unless a layer of fat separates them, because fat is darker. A plain film shows only five densities: air, fat, soft tissue, bone and metal.

Ultrasound

Skeletal muscle is usually hypoechoic (dark), crossed by hyperechoic (bright) lines: the perimysium between fascicles. The slides show the calf (gastrocnemius heads, soleus, Achilles tendon) in long and transverse views, and vessels in both planes.

CT

On CT, skeletal muscle is soft-tissue density with a fibrillar, feathery structure. On the Hounsfield scale (an arbitrary scale of tissue density, from air in black to bone in white) muscle measures +35 to +55 HU.

The syllabus also names CT windows, which the deck does not teach. A window chooses which part of the Hounsfield range is spread across the grey scale: a soft-tissue window separates muscle from fat, a lung window shows air-filled lung, a bone window shows cortex and marrow.

MRI

  • On high-resolution MRI muscles have a feathery pattern with fine connective tissue between fascicles.
  • Normal muscle is the reference tissue: other tissues are described as brighter (hyperintense), the same (isointense) or darker (hypointense) than muscle.
  • T1: fat is bright, so anatomy is clear. T2 fat-sat: fat is suppressed (dark) and fluid is bright, so oedema and injury stand out. Tendons are dark on both.
  • The slides show axial T1 and T2 fat-sat of the thighs, and coronal T1, T2 and T2 fat-sat of the legs.
Watch out

The table on slide 12 lists fat as hypointense on T1. That is wrong: fat is bright on T1, as the slide's own T1 picture shows (Grainger & Allison). Fluid is dark on T1 and bright on T2; tendon is dark on both. Use these in the exam.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Muscle is hypoechoic on ultrasound; the bright lines inside it are perimysium.
  • The linear probe, not the curvilinear, is used for DVT and musculoskeletal work.
  • Muscle is positive on the Hounsfield scale (+35 to +55); fat is negative.
  • On MRI, muscle is the reference tissue for "iso-", "hypo-" and "hyperintense".

Key terms

Fascicle: bundle of muscle fibres · Endomysium / perimysium / epimysium: wraps of fibre / fascicle / muscle · Aponeurosis: flat sheet-like tendon · Soft-tissue density: grey on a radiograph · Echogenicity: brightness on ultrasound · Hypoechoic / hyperechoic / anechoic: darker / brighter / black · Transducer: ultrasound probe · Hounsfield unit (HU): CT density scale · CT window: the HU range shown in grey · Isointense: same signal as muscle on MRI · T1-weighted: fat bright · T2 fat-sat: fat suppressed, fluid bright · DVT: deep vein thrombosis.
