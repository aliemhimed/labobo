Imaging the muscles of the head and neck

Facial muscles as sphincters and dilators of the eyes, nose and mouth; the extra-ocular muscles on orbital MRI; the muscles of mastication; the scalenes, sternocleidomastoid, splenius, platysma and hyoid muscles on CT and MRI; and the carotid and jugular vessels on ultrasound.

Labobo topic: Lec 10: Skull & Neck Muscles (CT, MRI)

In 60 seconds

  • Facial muscles are subcutaneous and work as sphincters or dilators of three openings: palpebral fissure, nostrils, oral fissure. Expression is secondary.
  • Sphincters: orbicularis oculi (eye), compressor naris (nose), orbicularis oris (mouth).
  • Coronal orbit MRI shows the four recti (SR, MR, LR, IR) round the optic nerve.
  • Jaw closers: masseter and temporalis; temporalis's horizontal posterior fibres retract the jaw.
  • Neck: scalenes flex the neck and lift the top two ribs; SCM from manubrium and medial clavicle to the mastoid; splenius extends the head; platysma is a thin superficial sheet.
  • The hyoid is the only bone that articulates with no other bone.
  • Ultrasound shows the CCA dividing into ICA and ECA, beside the IJV; plaque is hypoechoic.

Learning objectives

Syllabus week XII: imaging of the skull and neck muscles, CT and MRI. By the end you can:

  • identify the extra-ocular, facial and masticatory muscles on MRI;

Facial muscles: the three openings

The muscles of the face (of facial expression) are subcutaneous. Their main job is to open and close the three openings of the face; expression is a secondary function.

OpeningSphincterDilators
Palpebral fissureOrbicularis oculi (orbital and palpebral parts)Levator palpebrae superioris, frontalis
NostrilsCompressor naris (part of nasalis)Dilator naris, depressor septi, medial slip of levator labii superioris alaeque nasi
Oral fissureOrbicularis orisAll muscles round the mouth except orbicularis oris and mentalis

Eyelids and extra-ocular muscles

  • Round the eye the slides also show corrugator supercilii and procerus; levator palpebrae superioris attaches to the superior tarsal plate.
  • Extra-ocular muscles: four recti (superior, inferior, medial, lateral) from the common tendinous ring, and two obliques. The superior oblique tendon passes through the trochlea.
  • On coronal MRI the four recti sit round the optic nerve behind the globe. Axial CT of the orbit shows the medial and lateral recti.
In the clinic

Paralysis of orbicularis oculi (facial palsy): the eye cannot close, the cornea is exposed and dries; the lower lid falls away and tears pool and spill over the face; grit collects in the conjunctival sac and causes infection.

Nose and mouth

  • Nasalis has two parts: compressor naris (sphincter) and dilator naris.
  • Round the mouth the slides show orbicularis oris, levator labii superioris, buccinator, depressor labii inferioris, depressor anguli oris and mentalis.
  • On MRI, orbicularis oris merges with depressor labii inferioris, and the platysma merges with the SMAS (superficial musculoaponeurotic system, the fibromuscular layer of the face).

Muscles of mastication

MuscleAction on the slide
MasseterCloses the jaw
TemporalisAlso closes the jaw and holds the mandible at rest; its more horizontal posterior fibres retract the jaw
BuccinatorKeeps the cheeks flat and holds food between the teeth
Watch out

Slide 10 lists buccinator with the muscles of mastication. It helps chewing by keeping food between the teeth, but anatomically it is a muscle of facial expression (facial nerve). The four muscles of mastication are masseter, temporalis, medial and lateral pterygoid (trigeminal nerve).

Neck muscles

MuscleKey facts
Scalenes (anterior, middle, posterior)Flex and rotate the neck; elevate the top two ribs, adding chest volume
SternocleidomastoidOrigins manubrium and medial clavicle; insertion mastoid process. Bends the neck, turns and tilts the head
Splenius capitis and cervicisBack of the neck; together they extend the head (the muscles that tire when you sit close to a cinema screen)
PlatysmaThin, sheet-like, superficial in the anterior neck

On axial CT and MRI of the neck, the back is layered: trapezius outermost, then splenius capitis, semispinalis capitis and cervicis, and multifidus next to the spine; levator scapulae lies laterally. In front: the hyoid and the ventral (strap) muscles, with the scalenes beside the spine.

Hyoid bone and hyoid muscles

The hyoid is the only bone in the body that does not articulate with any other bone. It is held only by muscles, ligaments and cartilage, so it is "free floating".

GroupMuscles on the slide
Suprahyoid (above)Mylohyoid, digastric (anterior and posterior bellies), geniohyoid, stylohyoid
Infrahyoid (below)Thyrohyoid, sternohyoid, sternothyroid, omohyoid (superior and inferior bellies)

Neck vessels on ultrasound

  • The common carotid artery (CCA) divides into the internal (ICA) and external (ECA) carotid arteries; the internal jugular vein (IJV) runs beside them.
  • A large hypoechoic atherosclerotic plaque can be seen at the carotid bifurcation, with colour Doppler showing flow round it.

Commonly confused

PairHow to tell them apart
Orbicularis oculi vs levator palpebraeOculi closes the eye; levator lifts the upper lid
Compressor vs dilator narisBoth parts of nasalis: one closes, one opens the nostril
Masseter vs buccinatorMasseter closes the jaw; buccinator flattens the cheek
Splenius vs SCMSplenius extends the head (back); SCM turns and tilts it (side)
ICA vs ECABoth branch from the CCA; the ECA has neck branches, the ICA has none in the neck

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Mentalis is not a dilator of the mouth, although it lies round it.
  • Frontalis is a dilator of the palpebral fissure (lifts the brows and lids).
  • The posterior fibres of temporalis retract, not protrude, the jaw.
  • SCM inserts on the mastoid process, not the occiput.
  • The hyoid articulates with no bone.

Key terms

Palpebral fissure: gap between the eyelids · Sphincter / dilator: closes / opens an opening · Nasalis: compressor and dilator naris · Common tendinous ring: origin of the recti · Trochlea: pulley of superior oblique · SMAS: superficial musculoaponeurotic system of the face · Masseter / temporalis: jaw closers · Scalenes: lateral neck muscles to the upper ribs · Sternocleidomastoid: sternum and clavicle to mastoid · Splenius: head extensor · Platysma: superficial neck sheet · Hyoid: free-floating bone · Suprahyoid / infrahyoid: above / below the hyoid · CCA, ICA, ECA: common, internal, external carotid arteries · IJV: internal jugular vein.
