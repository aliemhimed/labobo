CT and MRI of the back muscles

The superficial, intermediate and deep muscles of the back, from trapezius and latissimus dorsi to erector spinae, multifidus, quadratus lumborum and psoas; how they look on axial lumbar CT and MRI; and two CT cases: a fat-density mass in trapezius and an abdominal wall haematoma.

Labobo topic: Lec 11: Back Muscles (CT, MRI)

In 60 seconds

  • Back muscles are the main structural support of the trunk; superficial ones are near the skin, deep ones near bone and organs.
  • Superficial: trapezius, latissimus dorsi (also an accessory muscle of respiration), with levator scapulae and the rhomboids under trapezius.
  • Intermediate: serratus posterior superior (C7–T3) and inferior (T11–L2).
  • Erector spinae, lateral to medial: iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis. Multifidus stabilises the spine.
  • Quadratus lumborum: iliac crest → L1–L5 transverse processes and 12th rib. Psoas joins iliacus as iliopsoas.
  • On axial lumbar CT/MRI: psoas beside the vertebral body, multifidus next to the spinous process, erector spinae lateral to it.

Learning objectives

Syllabus week XIII: CT and MRI of the muscles of the back, superficial and deep. By the end you can:

  • give the key attachments of serratus posterior and quadratus lumborum and the columns of erector spinae;
  • identify the back muscles, spine and great vessels on axial lumbar CT and MRI;
  • read the two CT cases.

Overview

The back muscles are the main structural support for the trunk. They move the head, neck, shoulders, arms and legs, and let us bend, twist, turn the head and extend the back. Superficial muscles lie close to the skin; muscles close to bone or organs are deep.

Superficial muscles

MuscleKey facts
TrapeziusBroad, flat, superficial; from the cervical to the thoracic region on the back of the neck and trunk
Latissimus dorsiBroad, flat; covers most of the lower posterior thorax; mainly moves the upper limb but is also an accessory muscle of respiration
Levator scapulaeLinks the upper limb to the vertebral column; lies in the posterior triangle of the neck, covered above by SCM and below by trapezius
Rhomboid major and minorDeep to trapezius; parallel bands running inferolaterally from the vertebrae to the medial border of the scapula
DeltoidLarge, triangular, over the glenohumeral joint; gives the shoulder its round contour (anterior, lateral and posterior parts)

Deep muscles

Erector spinae

A large paraspinal muscle just deep to the thoracolumbar fascia, in the groove beside the vertebral column. Its fibres run roughly vertically through the lumbar, thoracic and cervical regions. In the upper lumbar region it divides into three columns:

ColumnPosition
IliocostalisMost lateral
LongissimusIntermediate
SpinalisMost medial
Mnemonic

I Love Spine, from lateral to medial: Iliocostalis, Longissimus, Spinalis.

Multifidus, quadratus lumborum and psoas

  • Multifidus: a series of long, narrow muscles on each side of the spine, from the atlas down to the sacrum; they stabilise the spine.
  • Quadratus lumborum: flat and quadrangular; the slide calls it the deepest back muscle. Arises from the iliac crest; inserts on the transverse processes of L1–L5 and the lower 12th rib.
  • Psoas: deep, very close to the spine and the brim of the lesser pelvis; joins iliacus below to form iliopsoas.
Watch out

The deck heads levator scapulae and the rhomboids "Deep muscles" and calls erector spinae both superficial and deep. The standard grouping, used in your anatomy lectures, is: superficial = trapezius, latissimus dorsi, levator scapulae, rhomboids; intermediate = serratus posterior; deep (intrinsic) = splenius, erector spinae, transversospinalis (including multifidus).

Axial lumbar CT and MRI

On an axial slice of the lumbar spine read from front to back: aorta (left) and IVC (right) in front of the vertebra; psoas beside the vertebral body; the canal with the cauda equina or thecal sac; then posteriorly multifidus beside the spinous process and erector spinae lateral to it.

CT cases

In the clinic

A mass in the left trapezius measures −130 HU on CT. A negative value in this range is fat density, so a fatty lesion such as a lipoma is the likely meaning (the slide does not name it).

In the clinic

A 72-year-old develops a large haematoma of the right anterolateral abdominal wall 3 days after starting anticoagulation. Bruising is seen on the skin; CT angiography confirms the haematoma in the wall.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Latissimus dorsi is also an accessory respiratory muscle.
  • The rhomboids insert on the medial border of the scapula.
  • Quadratus lumborum reaches the 12th rib, not the 11th.
  • A negative HU value such as −130 means fat, not fluid or muscle.

Key terms

Thoracolumbar fascia: sheet over the deep back muscles · Erector spinae: iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis · Paraspinal muscle: muscle beside the spine · Multifidus: deep stabiliser beside the spinous processes · Transversospinalis: deep group including multifidus and semispinalis · Serratus posterior: intermediate back layer · Quadratus lumborum: iliac crest to L1–L5 and 12th rib · Iliopsoas: psoas + iliacus · Cauda equina: nerve roots below the cord · Thecal sac: dural sac with CSF · Ligamentum flavum: ligament between laminae · Facet joint: joint between articular processes · Haematoma: collection of blood in tissue · CT angiography: CT with contrast to show vessels.
