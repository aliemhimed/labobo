Muscles of the upper limb on CT and MRI

The arm (coracobrachialis, biceps, brachialis, triceps), the anterior and posterior forearm in their layers, and the hand (thenar, hypothenar, lumbricals, interossei, adductor pollicis, palmaris brevis), with labelled axial MRI of the arm and forearm.

Labobo topic: Lec 13: Upper Limb Muscles (CT, MRI)

In 60 seconds

  • Arm, anterior compartment: coracobrachialis, biceps brachii, brachialis. Posterior: triceps brachii.
  • Biceps: long head lateral, short head medial; acts across three joints (glenohumeral, elbow, radio-ulnar). Brachialis is the primary flexor of the elbow; triceps (3 heads) extends it.
  • Anterior forearm: superficial FCU, PL, FCR, pronator teres; intermediate FDS; deep FDP, FPL, pronator quadratus.
  • Posterior forearm: superficial layer of seven, four sharing the lateral epicondyle common origin; deep layer of five (supinator + four thumb/index muscles).
  • Hand: 3 thenar and 3 hypothenar muscles; 4 lumbricals; interossei: dorsal abduct, palmar adduct.
  • On axial MRI of the arm, the brachial artery and median nerve lie medially between the flexors and extensors; the radial nerve lies posteriorly by the humerus.

Learning objectives

Syllabus week XV: CT and MRI of the muscles of the upper limb; brachial, antebrachial and hand muscles. By the end you can:

  • name the arm muscles by compartment and give their main actions;
  • list the anterior and posterior forearm muscles by layer;
  • name the muscle groups of the hand and what they do;
  • identify the main muscles, vessels and nerves on axial MRI of the arm and forearm.

The arm on MRI

On an axial slice through the mid-arm, the humerus sits in the middle. In front: the two heads of biceps and coracobrachialis. Behind: the three heads of triceps. Medially, between them, runs the neurovascular bundle: brachial artery, median nerve, ulnar nerve, basilic vein. The radial nerve lies posteriorly near the humerus; the cephalic vein is superficial laterally.

Posterior forearm

  • Superficial layer: seven muscles. The slide pictures show brachioradialis, extensor carpi radialis longus and brevis, extensor digitorum, extensor digiti minimi, extensor carpi ulnaris and anconeus.
  • Four of them share a common tendinous origin at the lateral epicondyle: extensor carpi radialis brevis, extensor digitorum, extensor carpi ulnaris, extensor digiti minimi.
  • Deep layer: five muscles: supinator, abductor pollicis longus, extensor pollicis brevis, extensor pollicis longus, extensor indicis. All except supinator act on the thumb and index finger.

MRI and ultrasound of muscle

The deck shows MRI only. Grainger & Allison (p. 496): MRI is ideal for ligaments, tendons, cartilage and muscle; high-resolution ultrasound is ideal for tendon, ligament and muscle injuries. Normal tendon is dark on routine MRI and shows hyperechoic parallel lines on ultrasound; a tear is bright on T2 MRI and a hypoechoic gap on ultrasound.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • The primary flexor of the elbow is brachialis, not biceps.
  • FDS is the only muscle of the intermediate layer.
  • Supinator is in the deep posterior layer but does not act on the thumb or index.
  • The common extensor origin is the lateral epicondyle.
  • The radial artery forms the deep palmar arch between the heads of adductor pollicis.
Watch out

Slide 11 says the superficial posterior layer has seven muscles but names only the four with the common origin. Learn the four by name and the count of seven.

Key terms

Anterior compartment of the arm: coracobrachialis, biceps, brachialis · Bicipital aponeurosis: sheet from the biceps tendon to the forearm fascia · Triceps brachii: three-headed elbow extensor · Common extensor origin: lateral epicondyle · FDS / FDP: flexor digitorum superficialis / profundus · Pronator quadratus: deep distal forearm pronator · Supinator: deep posterior forearm muscle · Thenar eminence: thumb-base muscle mass · Hypothenar eminence: little-finger muscle mass · Lumbricals: four muscles linking flexor and extensor tendons · Interossei: muscles between the metacarpals · Unipennate / bipennate: fibres on one or both sides of a tendon · Deep palmar arch: formed by the radial artery · Palmaris brevis: subcutaneous hypothenar muscle · Axial MRI: cross-sectional slice.
