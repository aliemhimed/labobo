Muscles of the lower limb on CT, MRI and ultrasound

The thigh in three compartments (anterior extensors, medial adductors, posterior hamstrings) and the leg (anterior dorsiflexors, superficial and deep posterior calf), read on axial MRI; and how MRI and ultrasound show normal and injured tendons.

Labobo topic: Lec 14: Lower Limb Muscles (CT, MRI, US)

In 60 seconds

  • Thigh compartments: anterior extends the knee, medial adducts the hip, posterior flexes the knee.
  • Quadriceps = rectus femoris + 3 vasti → quadriceps tendon → patella → patellar ligament → tibial tuberosity. Sartorius is the longest muscle in the body.
  • Medial: adductor magnus largest; gracilis most superficial and medial, crossing hip and knee.
  • Hamstrings: biceps femoris (2 heads) lateral; semitendinosus superficial to semimembranosus, both medial.
  • Anterior leg: tibialis anterior is the strongest dorsiflexor. Superficial calf: gastrocnemius, soleus, plantaris → calcaneal tendon.
  • Deep calf: popliteus (knee only); tibialis posterior deepest, between FDL (medial) and FHL (lateral).
  • Normal tendon: dark on MRI, hyperechoic parallel lines on ultrasound; a tear is bright on T2 and a hypoechoic gap on ultrasound.

Learning objectives

Syllabus week XVII: imaging of the muscles of the lower and upper extremities with CT, MRI and ultrasound. By the end you can:

  • name the thigh muscles by compartment and identify them on axial MRI;
  • name the leg muscles by compartment and layer and identify them on axial MRI;
  • describe how MRI and ultrasound show normal tendons and tendon injuries.

Anterior thigh

The anterior compartment muscles mostly extend the knee.

  • Iliopsoas = psoas major + iliacus. They arise in the pelvis, pass under the inguinal ligament into the anterior thigh and share a common tendon.
  • Quadriceps femoris: four muscles, three vasti (lateralis, medialis, intermedius) and rectus femoris. The main bulk of the anterior thigh and one of the most powerful muscles in the body.
  • All four insert on the patella by the quadriceps tendon; the patella is fixed to the tibial tuberosity by the patellar ligament.
  • Sartorius: the longest muscle in the body; long and thin, running inferomedially across the thigh, more superficial than the other thigh muscles.
  • Pectineus also appears in the slide's legend of the anterior thigh.

The thigh on MRI

On an axial MRI of the mid-thigh, the femur is central. In front: rectus femoris over vastus intermedius, with vastus lateralis and medialis to each side. Medially: sartorius over the superficial femoral artery and vein, then adductor longus, adductor magnus and gracilis. Behind: biceps femoris laterally, semitendinosus and semimembranosus medially, with the sciatic nerve between them. The iliotibial band is lateral and the great saphenous vein superficial and medial.

Anterior leg

  • Tibialis anterior: along the lateral surface of the tibia; the strongest dorsiflexor of the foot.
  • Extensor digitorum longus: lateral and deep to tibialis anterior; its four tendons can be felt on the dorsum of the foot.
  • Extensor hallucis longus: deep to both; its tendon emerges between them to reach the big toe.
  • Laterally the slide also shows fibularis (peroneus) longus and brevis passing behind the lateral malleolus.

Posterior leg

Superficial layer: three muscles, one tendon

  • All three insert on the calcaneus by the calcaneal (Achilles) tendon.
  • Gastrocnemius: most superficial; medial and lateral heads; gives the calf its shape.
  • Soleus: flat, under gastrocnemius; named after the sole, a flat fish.
  • Plantaris: small; absent in about 10% of people.

Deep layer: four muscles

  • Popliteus: acts only on the knee; lies behind the knee at the floor of the popliteal fossa.
  • The other three act on the ankle and foot: flexor digitorum longus (thin, medial), flexor hallucis longus (lateral, on the side opposite the great toe), and tibialis posterior (deepest, between them).

The leg on MRI

On an axial MRI of the upper leg, the tibia is anteromedial and the fibula lateral. In front: tibialis anterior and extensor digitorum longus with the anterior tibial vessels. Laterally: peroneus longus and brevis with the superficial peroneal nerve. Deep behind: tibialis posterior, flexor hallucis longus and popliteus, with the tibial nerve and posterior tibial and peroneal vessels. Superficial behind: soleus and the two heads of gastrocnemius, with the small saphenous vein and sural nerve.

MRI and ultrasound of tendons

The deck has no ultrasound slide, so this comes from Grainger & Allison (p. 496).

MRIUltrasound
Normal tendon or ligamentNo signal (black) on routine sequencesHyperechoic parallel lines in the long axis
TendinitisEnlarged tendon, increased signal inside itThicker, with altered echogenicity
TearPartial: irregular, high T2 signal. Complete: tendon discontinuous or absentHypoechoic gap, often fluid in the sheath
OtherVery sensitive to bone oedema (low T1, high T2); fat suppression makes injuries stand outNeeds a high-resolution probe; wrong probe angle can make a normal tendon look dark

Ligament injury on MRI is graded: I, increased signal around a normal ligament; II, thickening with abnormal signal; III, complete disruption.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • The longest muscle in the body is sartorius; the largest medial thigh muscle is adductor magnus.
  • Biceps femoris is the lateral hamstring; the other two are medial.
  • Popliteus acts only on the knee, not the ankle.
  • Tibialis posterior is the deepest calf muscle.
  • A normal tendon is dark on routine MRI; brightness on T2 means fluid or injury.
Watch out

Slide 3 says sartorius is more superficial than the other muscles "in the leg". Sartorius is a thigh muscle; it is the most superficial muscle of the anterior thigh.

Key terms

Iliopsoas: psoas major + iliacus · Quadriceps femoris: rectus femoris + 3 vasti · Patellar ligament: patella to tibial tuberosity · Sartorius: longest muscle · Adductors: medial thigh muscles · Gracilis: superficial medial, crosses hip and knee · Hamstrings: biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus · Sciatic nerve: between the hamstrings · Iliotibial band: lateral thigh fascia · Calcaneal tendon: shared tendon of the superficial calf · Popliteal fossa: space behind the knee · Tibialis posterior: deepest calf muscle · Dorsiflexion: lifting the foot upward · Hyperechoic / hypoechoic: brighter / darker on ultrasound · Fat suppression: MRI technique that makes fluid stand out · Bone oedema: low T1, high T2 marrow signal.
