Imaging the pelvis

The bones, joints and ligaments of the pelvis and how they look on an AP radiograph; inlet and outlet views; the triradiate cartilage; fracture patterns from open-book injuries to avulsions; and the vessels, urethra and bladder of the pelvis on angiography, urethrography, CT IVP and ultrasound.

Labobo topic: Lec 8: Pelvis Imaging

In 60 seconds

  • Each innominate bone = ilium + ischium + pubis; the two innominate bones plus sacrum (5) and coccyx (3–4) form the pelvic ring.
  • Joints: pubic symphysis, sacroiliac, lumbosacral, sacrococcygeal.
  • The triradiate cartilage is the Y-shaped growth plate in the acetabulum; it fuses at 15–16 years in males, 13–14 in females.
  • Ligaments: sacrotuberous, sacrospinous (to the ischial spine), iliolumbar (from L5 transverse process), anterior and posterior sacroiliac.
  • Special views: inlet and outlet (tube angled 25° and 60°).
  • Fracture patterns by force: lateral compression, AP compression (open-book), vertical shear. Open-book fractures are high energy with life-threatening vascular injury.
  • Avulsion fracture: a chip of bone pulled off by a tendon or ligament.

Learning objectives

Syllabus week X: pelvic bones (ilium, ischium, pubis), pubic symphysis, pelvic radiological lines and sizes, pubic symphysis dislocation, simple fractures. By the end you can:

  • name the bones, joints and ligaments of the pelvis and find them on an AP radiograph;
  • explain the triradiate cartilage and its fusion ages;
  • describe the inlet and outlet views;
  • classify pelvic fractures by mechanism and recognise open-book, diastasis and avulsion injuries;
  • name the imaging tests for the pelvic vessels, urethra and bladder.

Bones and joints

  • The slide calls the sacrum (5 fused bones) and coccyx (3–4 bones) the "pelvic spine".
  • On each side the pubis, ischium and ilium join to form an innominate (hip) bone; there are two, right and left.
  • The two halves form the pelvic ring.
  • Pelvic joints: pubic symphysis, lumbosacral, sacroiliac, sacrococcygeal.

Triradiate cartilage

A Y-shaped growth plate where the ilium, ischium and pubis meet in the acetabulum of a growing child. It usually fuses by 15–16 years in males and 13–14 years in females. On a child's pelvic X-ray (the slide shows a 22-month-old boy) the gap must not be mistaken for a fracture.

In the clinic

A fracture through the triradiate cartilage in a child can disturb growth of the acetabulum (Grainger & Allison p. 520).

The AP radiograph

Read a normal AP pelvis in order: the ring (iliac crests, sacroiliac joints, sacrum, pubic rami, symphysis), then the obturator foramina, then each hip (acetabulum, femoral head with its fovea, neck, greater and lesser trochanters).

The deck has no slide on pelvic radiological lines or sizes. The book names two lines on the AP view: the iliopectineal line (anterior column) and the ilioischial line (posterior column); a break in one points to a fracture of that column of the acetabulum.

Inlet and outlet views

Two angled AP views of the supine patient show the ring better than a plain AP. The slide's diagram angles the tube at 25° and 60° from the vertical. The inlet view looks down into the ring (good for displacement front to back); the outlet view looks at it face-on (good for vertical displacement and the sacrum).

Fracture patterns

Pelvic fractures are grouped by the direction of the force (the red arrows on the slide). Names and grades below are filled in from Grainger & Allison (pp. 519–520).

PatternTypical causeWhat happens
Lateral compression (I–III)Side impact; the commonestPubic rami fracture; the hemipelvis rotates inward. III = "roll-over": the opposite side opens outward
AP compression (I–III)Head-on collisionI: symphysis widening under 2.5 cm, stable. II: open-book, over 2.5 cm, anterior SI ligaments torn. III: posterior SI ligaments torn too, unstable in all directions
Vertical shearFall from heightHemipelvis displaced upward; unstable
In the clinic

Open-book fractures come from high-energy trauma and often bring life-threatening vascular injury: the opened ring tears pelvic veins and arteries, so the patient may bleed into the pelvis.

Diastasis, avulsion and cases

  • Avulsion fracture: a small chunk of bone, attached to a tendon or ligament, is pulled away from the main bone. Slide example: the lateral quarter of the right iliac crest.
  • Diastasis of the pubic symphysis = separation of the joint (the "dislocation" in the syllabus). CT case: the left pubis displaced upward and overriding the right, with comminuted fractures of both sacral alae and a fracture of the left L5 transverse process.
  • Second CT case: horizontal fractures of the left superior and inferior pubic rami, an oblique fracture of the left posterior ilium into the left SI joint, and a left sacral impaction fracture; the right side normal.

Vessels, urethra and bladder

  • Angiogram: distal abdominal aorta with lumbar branches → common iliac → internal and external iliac → common femoral artery. The veins mirror this: femoral → external iliac + internal iliac → common iliac → inferior vena cava.
  • Urethrogram: contrast outlines the whole urethra.
  • Urinary bladder: distal part of the urinary tract, extraperitoneal, in the true pelvis; a reservoir for urine. Normal bladder shown on ultrasound.
  • CT IVP (intravenous pyelogram): for blood in the urine or flank/lower back pain. It can show stones, an enlarged prostate, tumours of kidney, ureter or bladder, scarring from urinary infection, and birth defects of the tract.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • The triradiate cartilage fuses earlier in females (13–14) than in males (15–16).
  • The iliolumbar ligament starts on the L5 transverse process.
  • Open-book = AP compression, not lateral compression.
  • The bladder is extraperitoneal.
  • An avulsion fracture is caused by a pull, not a direct blow.

Key terms

Innominate bone: hip bone of ilium, ischium and pubis · Pelvic ring: the two hip bones and the sacrum · Pubic symphysis: midline joint of the pubic bones · Triradiate cartilage: Y-shaped growth plate of the acetabulum · Sacrotuberous ligament: sacrum to ischial tuberosity · Sacrospinous ligament: sacrum to ischial spine · Iliolumbar ligament: L5 to iliac crest · Inlet / outlet view: angled AP views of the ring · Open-book fracture: AP compression opening the ring · Vertical shear: upward displacement of a hemipelvis · Diastasis: separation of a joint · Avulsion fracture: bone pulled off by a tendon or ligament · Comminuted: broken into several fragments · Urethrogram: contrast study of the urethra · CT IVP: CT of kidneys, ureters and bladder after IV contrast.
