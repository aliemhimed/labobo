Nondisjunction and Sex Chromosome Aneuploidies

What goes wrong when chromosomes fail to separate in meiosis, and the four main results for the sex chromosomes: Klinefelter (47,XXY), Turner (45,X), triple X (47,XXX) and 47,XYY, plus how FISH confirms them.

Labobo topic: MG Wk 7: Sex Chromosome Aneuploidies

In 60 seconds

  • Nondisjunction = homologues (meiosis I) or sister chromatids (meiosis II) fail to separate → trisomy (2n+1) or monosomy (2n−1).
  • Risk rises with maternal age (weak cohesin, poorer spindle checkpoint in long-arrested oocytes).
  • Klinefelter 47,XXY: ~1 in 650 males; commonest cause of primary testicular failure; tall (extra SHOX), low testosterone, infertile.
  • Turner 45,X: ~1 in 2000 females; mostly paternal nondisjunction; only ~1% of 45,X conceptuses survive; short (SHOX loss), streak gonads, webbed neck, coarctation, bicuspid aortic valve.
  • Triple X 47,XXX: ~1 in 1000 females; usually mild; 2 Barr bodies.
  • 47,XYY: ~1 in 1000 males; paternal meiosis II; tall, usually normal; no link to criminality.
  • Diagnosis: karyotype, confirmed by FISH.

Nondisjunction

Normal meiosis

  • Meiosis I: homologous chromosomes (XX or XY) pair and separate; each cell gets one of each pair.
  • Meiosis II: sister chromatids separate → haploid gametes with one copy of each chromosome (X or Y in sperm, X in eggs).

When it fails

Error inWhat fails to separateGametes
Meiosis IHomologous chromosomesOne cell gets both members of the pair, the other gets none
Meiosis IISister chromatidsGametes with two copies or no copy

An abnormal gamete + a normal gamete → an aneuploid zygote: trisomic (2n+1) or monosomic (2n−1).

  • Klinefelter usually from maternal meiosis I (per the slides); XYY from paternal meiosis II.
  • Maternal age effect: oocytes are arrested in prophase I for decades; their cohesin proteins weaken and spindle checkpoint control drops, so errors rise with age.
  • Mosaics (e.g. 46,XY/47,XXY) come from mitotic nondisjunction after fertilisation and are usually milder.

Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY)

  • Male phenotype with one or more extra X chromosomes; most commonly 47,XXY.
  • About 1 in 650 male births: the most frequent cause of primary testicular failure.
  • Origin: mainly maternal meiosis I nondisjunction (XX egg + Y sperm); less often paternal meiosis I or II. Mosaic 46,XY/47,XXY is milder.

Mechanism

  • One X is inactivated (one Barr body), but ~15% of genes escape, especially in PAR1/PAR2.
  • Extra dose of the escape gene SHOX → tall stature and long limbs.
  • Seminiferous tubules degenerate → Leydig cell function falls → low testosterone.

Clinical picture (slide 6 is an image; from the textbook): tall with long legs, small firm testes, infertility (azoospermia), gynecomastia, sparse body hair, raised FSH and LH, sometimes learning or language difficulties. Treated with testosterone.

Turner syndrome (45,X)

  • Complete or partial monosomy X: female with one X (45,X) or mosaic 45,X/46,XX.
  • About 1 in 2000 live female births.
  • Usually from paternal nondisjunction: a sperm with no sex chromosome.
  • Only about 1% of 45,X conceptuses survive to birth.

Mechanism

  • Haploinsufficiency of genes that normally escape inactivation, especially SHOX in PAR1 → short stature and skeletal abnormalities.
  • Too little X gene expression → defective ovaries → streak gonads.

Triple X (47,XXX)

  • Females with three X chromosomes; about 1 in 1000 live female births; often undiagnosed because signs are mild.
  • Usually maternal meiosis I nondisjunction (XX egg + X sperm); mosaics 46,XX/47,XXX from post-zygotic errors.
  • Two Xs are inactivated (2 Barr bodies), but escape genes give subtle effects.
AreaFeatures
PhysicalUsually normal; sometimes tall, mild epicanthal folds
DevelopmentMore speech/language delay, mild learning difficulty, subtle executive-function problems; IQ usually normal
ReproductionPuberty and fertility usually normal; some premature ovarian failure
BehaviourSlightly more anxiety and attention problems

Diagnosis by karyotype or FISH, often found incidentally in prenatal screening. Prognosis is excellent.

47,XYY

  • Males with an extra Y; about 1 in 1000 live male births; often found by chance.
  • Origin: paternal meiosis II: Y sister chromatids fail to separate → YY sperm + X egg → 47,XYY. Mosaics are milder.
  • There is no inactivation mechanism for the Y, but the Y has few active genes, so the dose effect is small.
AreaFeatures
PhysicalTall; normal sexual development and fertility

Life expectancy is normal; support with education and speech if needed.

FISH (fluorescence in situ hybridisation)

  1. Cells (e.g. blood, amniotic fluid) are spread on a slide.
  2. A fluorescent DNA probe, made to bind (hybridise) only to a specific gene or chromosome region, is added.
  3. Under a fluorescence microscope the probe glows, showing where (and how many copies of) that sequence are present.

Sex chromosome abnormalities are found by karyotype and confirmed by FISH with X- and Y-specific probes.

Watch out

Watch out: parental origin of Klinefelter

The slides say Klinefelter arises “primarily” from maternal meiosis I. Many textbooks report that roughly half of 47,XXY cases are paternal (meiosis I, an XY sperm). For this course’s exam, follow the slide (maternal MI most common), but don’t be surprised by an option saying both parents contribute.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Turner is usually paternal; Klinefelter and triple X usually maternal MI; XYY paternal MII.
  • XYY can only come from the father (only he has a Y).
  • SHOX: extra → tall (Klinefelter); missing → short (Turner).
  • Turner intelligence is usually normal.
  • XYY is not linked to criminality.
  • Klinefelter males have one Barr body; XYY males have none.

Key terms

Nondisjunction
Failure of chromosomes or chromatids to separate.
Aneuploidy
Abnormal number of individual chromosomes.
Trisomy / monosomy
One extra / one missing chromosome.
Mosaic
Two or more cell lines with different karyotypes.
Cohesin
Protein holding chromatids/homologues together.
SHOX
Short-stature homeobox gene in PAR1.
Haploinsufficiency
One working copy is not enough for normal function.
Streak gonads
Undeveloped fibrous ovaries in Turner syndrome.
Coarctation of the aorta
Narrowing of the aorta, common in Turner.
FISH
Fluorescence in situ hybridisation.
Karyotype
Picture of a cell’s chromosomes arranged by size.
