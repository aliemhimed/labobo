Chromatin Structure and Organization

How about two metres of DNA fit in a nucleus: nucleosomes, the levels of folding from 2 nm to the 1400 nm metaphase chromosome, how chromatin is opened (remodelling and histone marks), euchromatin vs heterochromatin, and satellite DNA at centromeres.

Labobo topic: MG Wk 11: Chromatin Structure and Organization

In 60 seconds

  • Nucleosome = histone octamer (2 × H2A, H2B, H3, H4) + ~147 bp DNA in 1.7 left-handed turns. H1 binds linker DNA (20–80 bp).
  • Histones are rich in lysine and arginine (positive) → bind DNA phosphates (negative).
  • Levels: 2 nm DNA → 11 nm beads-on-a-string (~7× shorter) → 30 nm fibre (H1) → 300 nm loops on a scaffold → 700 nm chromatid → 1400 nm metaphase chromosome; ~10,000× overall.
  • Acetylation (HATs) opens chromatin; deacetylation (HDACs) closes it. Marks form a histone code.
  • ATP-dependent remodellers slide, remove or swap histones (e.g. CENP-A), without changing DNA sequence.
  • Euchromatin: open, light, active, replicates early. Heterochromatin: dense, dark, silent, replicates late; constitutive (centromeres, telomeres) or facultative (Barr body).
  • Satellite DNA: short tandem repeats at centromeres; with CENP-A builds the kinetochore.

What chromatin is

  • Chromatin is how eukaryotic DNA is organised in the nucleus.
  • A human cell has about 6 billion bp (diploid), almost 2 m of DNA, in a nucleus a few micrometres wide.
  • Chromatin = DNA + histones (small, basic, positive proteins) + many non-histone proteins (replication, transcription, repair, condensation, structure).
  • It changes through the cell cycle:
    • Interphase: relaxed and extended → access for transcription, replication and repair.
    • Division: condenses into metaphase chromosomes, about 10,000-fold compaction.

The nucleosome

  • The basic repeating unit of chromatin and the first level of compaction.
  • Core: a histone octamer = two each of H2A, H2B, H3 and H4.
  • About 147 bp of DNA wraps around it in 1.7 left-handed superhelical turns.
  • Histones are rich in lysine and arginine, giving a strong positive charge that binds the negative DNA phosphates. They are small and highly conserved across species.
  • Between nucleosomes is linker DNA (about 20–80 bp), bound by histone H1 (the “linker histone”) where DNA enters and leaves the nucleosome, stabilising higher-order structure.
  • In the electron microscope: “beads on a string” = the 11 nm fibre.

Levels of packaging

LevelDiameterWhat happens
1. DNA double helix2 nmNaked DNA, no proteins
2. Nucleosome fibre (“beads on a string”)11 nmDNA wrapped on octamers; shortens DNA about 7-fold
3. 30 nm fibre (solenoid or zig-zag)30 nmNucleosomes pack regularly; needs H1; another ~6–7-fold compaction
4. Looped domains300 nm30 nm fibre forms loops attached to a non-histone protein scaffold; many genes sit in loops and loop anchors help regulate them
5. Chromatid fibre700 nmIn mitosis loops coil further; very compact, stable, transcriptionally inactive; each chromatid arm
6. Metaphase chromosome1400 nmTwo 700 nm sister chromatids joined at the centromere; maximum condensation for segregation
Memory hook: 2, 11, 30, 300, 700, 1400

“2 strands, 11 beads, 30 coils, 300 loops, 700 per chromatid, double it for the chromosome (1400).”

Chromatin remodelling

Packed chromatin blocks transcription factors, polymerases and repair enzymes. Chromatin remodelling = active, regulated changes that open or close chromatin. It is needed for transcription, replication, recombination and repair, and to restore gene expression after mitosis.

Histone tail modifications

Histones have amino-terminal tails sticking out of the nucleosome; they are modified covalently.

ModificationEnzymeEffect
Acetylation of lysineHistone acetyltransferases (HATs)Removes positive charge → weaker grip on DNA → open chromatin, genes on
DeacetylationHistone deacetylases (HDACs)Restores condensation → genes off
Methylation, phosphorylationVariousRecruit proteins that activate or repress

Together these marks form a combinatorial histone code deciding which DNA is accessible.

ATP-dependent remodelling complexes

  • Slide nucleosomes along DNA to expose hidden sequences.
  • Remove or partly disassemble histones to open longer stretches.
  • Swap standard histones for variants such as CENP-A (replaces H3 at centromeres).

None of this changes the DNA sequence: remodelling is an epigenetic process.

TypeBehaviourWhere / example
ConstitutivePermanently condensed in all cellsCentromeres and telomeres; chromosome stability, kinetochores, protecting ends

Satellite DNA

  • Highly repetitive, tandem repeats in large blocks, especially at centromeres (sometimes near telomeres).
  • Repeat units are short (a few to several dozen bp) but repeated thousands to millions of times.
  • Named “satellite” because they form separate bands in density-gradient centrifugation (different base composition).
  • They don’t code for protein; they are a main part of constitutive heterochromatin.
  • Centromeric repeats bind the histone variant CENP-A (replacing H3), which recruits kinetochore proteins that attach chromosomes to the spindle.
  • Their condensed state protects against recombination and strengthens centromeres; they may produce small RNAs that guide chromatin-modifying enzymes.
  • Changes in satellite length or organisation can disrupt centromeres → genomic instability.

Watch out

Watch out: “six to seven times thicker”

Slide 6 says the 30 nm fibre is “six to seven times thicker” than the 11 nm fibre. By diameter it is about 3 times thicker; the 6–7-fold figure is the extra compaction (shortening) of the DNA at this step.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • H1 is not part of the octamer.
  • DNA wraps 1.7 turns (left-handed) of ~147 bp.
  • Acetylation opens chromatin (neutralises lysine’s positive charge).
  • Heterochromatin replicates late.
  • The Barr body is facultative heterochromatin; centromeres are constitutive.
  • Chromatin remodelling doesn’t change the DNA sequence.

Key terms

Chromatin
DNA plus histone and non-histone proteins.
Nucleosome
DNA wrapped around a histone octamer.
Linker DNA
DNA between nucleosomes, bound by H1.
30 nm fibre
Coiled nucleosome chain (solenoid/zig-zag).
Scaffold
Non-histone protein frame anchoring chromatin loops.
Chromatin remodelling
Active opening/closing of chromatin.
Histone code
Combination of histone tail marks that sets accessibility.
HAT / HDAC
Histone acetyltransferase / deacetylase.
Euchromatin
Open, active chromatin.
Heterochromatin
Condensed, silent chromatin.
Satellite DNA
Tandem repeats at centromeres.
CENP-A
Centromere-specific H3 variant.
Kinetochore
Protein complex on the centromere that binds spindle fibres.
