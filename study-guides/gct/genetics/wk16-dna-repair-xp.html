DNA Repair Systems and Xeroderma Pigmentosum

Why cells must repair DNA, the main kinds of damage, the five repair systems (BER, NER, MMR, HR, NHEJ) and what each fixes, and xeroderma pigmentosum, where a broken NER system makes sunlight dangerous.

Labobo topic: MG Wk 16: DNA Repair and Xeroderma Pigmentosum

In 60 seconds

  • Repair fixes damage before replication makes it permanent; without repair the mutation rate would be incompatible with life.
  • Damage types: altered bases (deamination, oxidation, alkylation), bulky lesions (UV thymine dimers), strand breaks (single, double).
  • BER: small base damage; DNA glycosylase → AP site → AP endonuclease → polymerase → ligase.
  • NER: bulky, helix-distorting lesions; detects distortion, cuts both sides, removes a short stretch, refills.
  • MMR: mismatches and small loops escaping proofreading.
  • Double-strand breaks: HR (accurate, uses sister chromatid, S/G2) and NHEJ (fast, any phase, error-prone).
  • Xeroderma pigmentosum: autosomal recessive NER defect (XPA–XPG), no unscheduled DNA synthesis, extreme UV sensitivity, skin cancer risk thousands of times higher.

Why cells need DNA repair

  • Mutations arise constantly from replication errors, tautomeric shifts, slippage, and mutagens (UV, ionising radiation, chemicals). Most are neutral or harmful.
  • Polymerases and proofreading are accurate but not perfect; damage can block replication or cause wrong pairing.
  • Damage not fixed before replication becomes a permanent mutation in both daughter cells.
  • Repair systems monitor the genome, recognise abnormal DNA, remove it, and restore the sequence using the undamaged strand as a template.
  • They don’t undo mutations already fixed, but they greatly reduce how many form. Without them the mutation rate would be incompatible with life.
  • When repair fails, damage accumulates → more mutations → genetic disease and cancer (e.g. xeroderma pigmentosum).

Base excision repair (BER)

Fixes small chemical changes that don’t distort the helix but alter pairing. These arise often from spontaneous reactions, so BER works all the time.

  1. DNA glycosylase recognises the damaged base and removes it by cutting the base–sugar bond → an abasic (AP) site.
  2. AP endonuclease cuts the backbone at the AP site.
  3. DNA polymerase inserts the correct nucleotide using the complementary strand.
  4. DNA ligase seals the nick.

Nucleotide excision repair (NER)

Fixes bulky lesions that distort the helix. The best-known is the UV thymine dimer: abnormal covalent bonds between adjacent thymines.

  1. Recognition: NER does not detect a specific chemical change; it detects the distortion in DNA shape.
  2. Unwinding: repair proteins bind and locally unwind the DNA.
  3. Excision: endonucleases cut the damaged strand on both sides of the lesion, removing a short stretch of nucleotides.
  4. Resynthesis: DNA polymerase fills the gap using the undamaged strand.
  5. Ligation: DNA ligase seals the strand.
BER vs NER

BER removes one damaged base, found by a specific glycosylase. NER removes a stretch of nucleotides, found by helix distortion.

If it fails—Chromosomal instability and cell death

Double-strand breaks are so dangerous because both strands are broken, removing the usual template.

Xeroderma pigmentosum (XP)

Genetic basis

  • Autosomal recessive.
  • Mutations in genes for NER proteins, the XP genes, grouped in complementation groups XPA through XPG.
  • UV makes thymine dimers; normal cells remove them by NER. In XP the dimers accumulate; replication across them causes errors → a huge rise in mutations, especially in sun-exposed skin.
  • Lab hallmark: XP cells fail to carry out unscheduled DNA synthesis (the repair synthesis normally seen after UV), linking XP directly to defective NER.

Clinical features

StageFeatures
Early childhoodSevere sunburn, redness and blistering after minimal sun
ProgressionDry, thin, fragile skin; freckling and abnormal pigmentation young; premature ageing of sun-exposed skin
CancerBasal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, melanoma, often in childhood or adolescence; risk thousands of times higher than normal
Some formsNeurological problems: developmental delay, hearing loss, progressive neurodegeneration (repair also matters in long-lived neurons)
In the clinic

Management is strict UV protection (avoid sunlight, protective clothing, sunscreen, UV-filtering windows) and frequent skin checks to remove cancers early. Children with XP are sometimes called “children of the night”.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Thymine dimers are repaired by NER, not BER.
  • The first enzyme of BER is a glycosylase, making an AP site.
  • HR needs a sister chromatid, so it works in S/G2.
  • NHEJ is the error-prone one.
  • XP is autosomal recessive and shows no unscheduled DNA synthesis.

Key terms

Proofreading
Polymerase checking and correcting its own errors.
Thymine dimer
Covalent link between adjacent thymines from UV.
DNA glycosylase
Removes a damaged base, leaving an AP site.
AP site
Abasic (apurinic/apyrimidinic) site.
BER / NER / MMR
Base excision / nucleotide excision / mismatch repair.
Homologous recombination
Accurate DSB repair using a homologous template.
NHEJ
Error-prone DSB repair joining ends directly.
Unscheduled DNA synthesis
Repair DNA synthesis outside S phase.
Complementation group
Group of patients whose defect lies in the same gene.
Xeroderma pigmentosum
Recessive NER defect with extreme UV sensitivity.
