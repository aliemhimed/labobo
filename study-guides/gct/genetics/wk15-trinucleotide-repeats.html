Expandable Trinucleotide Repeats

Mutations that grow: how short repeats such as CAG, CGG and CTG expand, why their position in the gene decides the damage (toxic protein, toxic RNA or silencing), the three classic diseases (Huntington, fragile X, myotonic dystrophy) and genetic anticipation.

Labobo topic: MG Wk 15: Trinucleotide Repeat Disorders

In 60 seconds

  • Expandable repeats: a 3-nucleotide unit (CAG, CGG, CTG) repeated many times; normal = small, stable; disease often > 50–200.
  • They expand through replication slippage and repair errors, and can grow from one generation to the next.
  • In coding regions (CAG) → long polyglutamine tracts → misfolding, aggregation, neurodegeneration.
  • In non-coding regions → toxic RNA that traps proteins, or DNA methylation that silences the gene.
  • Huntington: CAG in HTT (coding, polyQ). Fragile X: CGG in FMR1 5′UTR (silencing). Myotonic dystrophy: CTG in DMPK 3′UTR (toxic RNA).
  • Anticipation: the disease starts earlier and is more severe in each generation, because the repeat lengthens.

Learning objectives

By the end you can:

What expandable repeats are

  • Short DNA sequences, usually 3 nucleotides (e.g. CAG, CGG, CTG), repeated many times in a row.
  • In healthy people the number is small and stable.
  • In disease the number becomes abnormally large, often > 50–200, making the gene unstable and disturbing its function.
Single base-pair mutationRepeat expansion
StabilityStable once formed; passed on unchangedDynamic: can grow when passed on
SeverityFixedIncreases with repeat length

How repeats expand

  • Replication slippage: at repeat regions the strands misalign during replication and extra repeats are added.
  • Repair errors: repair machinery mis-copies repeat regions, progressively lengthening them.
  • Over generations the number can keep increasing: the basis of anticipation.

Why they are harmful

The damage depends mainly on where the repeat sits.

LocationMechanismResult
Coding regionCAG codes glutamine → abnormally long polyglutamine tractProtein misfolds and aggregates, disrupts protein–protein interactions → progressive neurodegeneration
Non-coding (UTR, intron)Repeat RNA folds into abnormal structures that bind and trap regulatory proteinsToxic RNA effect (e.g. faulty splicing of other genes)
Non-coding, very largeAbnormal DNA methylation and chromatin condensationTranscriptional silencing of the gene

Effects build up over time and worsen with length, so these diseases are progressive, often late-onset, multisystem, and show anticipation.

Mnemonics for the repeats
  • CAG = Caudate has less AG (Ach and GABA) → Huntington.
  • CGG = Chin, Giant Gonads → fragile X.
  • CTG = Cataracts, Toupee (balding), Gonadal atrophy → myotonic dystrophy.

Genetic anticipation

  • Anticipation: in successive generations the disease starts earlier and is more severe.
  • Cause: the repeat gets longer as it is passed on, and longer repeats give worse, earlier disease.
  • Example (textbook): a grandparent with mild cataracts from myotonic dystrophy, a parent with adult muscle disease, and a child born with the severe congenital form.
In the clinic

A family history where each generation is affected younger should make you think of a trinucleotide repeat disorder. Genetic testing measures the repeat number.

Watch out

Watch out: thin slides

This lecture’s slides cover the mechanisms only; the disease details, repeat numbers and mnemonics come from the textbook and standard references. Learn the repeat, gene, location and mechanism for each disease: those are the most testable facts.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Only coding CAG repeats make polyglutamine.
  • Fragile X repeats are in the 5′ UTR and silence the gene by methylation.
  • Myotonic dystrophy acts through RNA, not an abnormal protein.
  • Anticipation is due to repeat expansion.
  • Huntington tends to expand through the father; fragile X through the mother.

Key terms

Trinucleotide repeat
Three-base unit repeated in tandem.
Dynamic mutation
Mutation that can change size when passed on.
Polyglutamine
Long run of glutamines from expanded coding CAG.
Toxic RNA
Repeat RNA that traps regulatory proteins.
Premutation
Intermediate repeat size that can expand in offspring.
Full mutation
Repeat size causing disease.
Anticipation
Earlier/worse disease in later generations.
FMRP
Protein made by FMR1, lost in fragile X.
Myotonia
Delayed muscle relaxation after contraction.
