Lysosomes, Endocytosis and Phagocytosis

How lysosomal enzymes are sorted (mannose 6-phosphate), what lysosomes do (digestion, autophagy, ageing), how cells take things in (pinocytosis, receptor-mediated endocytosis of LDL, phagocytosis), how some bacteria escape, and the lysosomal storage diseases.

Labobo topic: MB Lec 11: Endocytosis and Phagocytosis

In 60 seconds

  • At the TGN, lysosomal enzymes get mannose 6-phosphate → bound by M6P receptors → clathrin vesicles with GGA adaptors → endosome → lysosome; receptors return.
  • A lysosome has > 50 acid hydrolases; lysosomal pH ≈ 4.6.
  • Autophagy: phagophore → double-membrane autophagosome → autolysosome → residual body → lipofuscin (ageing). Liver cells degrade 1–1.5% of proteins per hour this way.
  • Endocytosis: bulk-phase (pinocytosis) vs receptor-mediated (clathrin); receptors are 10–20× concentrated in coated pits; adaptor AP2.
  • Housekeeping receptors (LDL, transferrin) recycle; signalling receptors (EGF, insulin) are ubiquitinated and destroyed (down-regulation).
  • LDL: ~1500 cholesterol esters + one apoB-100. HDL: apoA-I, carries cholesterol back to the liver. PCSK9 destroys LDL receptors.
  • Phagocytosis: particles > 0.5 μm, actin-driven. TB blocks fusion; Coxiella survives the lysosome; Listeria escapes it. > 40 storage diseases, ~1 in 5000 infants.

Sorting lysosomal enzymes

The trans Golgi network (TGN) is the main sorting station. The best-understood route from it carries lysosomal enzymes, made on RER ribosomes.

  1. In the Golgi, enzymes recognise lysosomal proteins and add a phosphate to certain mannose residues of their N-linked sugars in two steps → mannose 6-phosphate (M6P), the sorting signal.
  2. M6P receptors (MPRs), integral proteins of the TGN membrane, capture them.
  3. They are packed into clathrin-coated vesicles: outer clathrin lattice + inner adaptors; at the TGN these adaptors are GGAs.
  4. The receptors let go of the enzymes and return to the Golgi.
  5. The enzymes go to an endosome, then a lysosome.
  6. MPRs on the plasma membrane also recapture any enzymes accidentally secreted and send them to lysosomes.

Lysosomal enzymes and phagocytes

  • A lysosome holds at least 50 hydrolytic enzymes that together digest virtually every macromolecule.
  • All work best at acid pH (acid hydrolases), matching the lysosome’s pH of about 4.6.
  • Kupffer cells (liver phagocytes that engulf ageing red cells) contain many lysosomes (at least 10 in one section) of very variable size, shape and density.
  • Macrophages and neutrophils act as scavengers; ingested bacteria are inactivated by the low pH, then digested.

Autophagy and ageing

  • Organelle turnover: regulated destruction and replacement of the cell’s own organelles.
  1. An organelle (e.g. a mitochondrion) is wrapped by a double membrane (phagophore) → a double-membrane autophagosome. In mammals autophagosomes form from contact sites between the ER and mitochondrial outer membrane.
  2. Its outer membrane fuses with a lysosome → autolysosome; the inner membrane and contents are digested and the products reused.
  3. When digestion ends, it is a residual body: emptied by exocytosis or kept as a lipofuscin granule.
  • About 1–1.5% of a healthy liver cell’s proteins are degraded by autophagy per hour.
  • Lipofuscin granules increase with age, especially in long-lived cells such as neurons: a hallmark of ageing.

Endocytosis

The plasma membrane folds inward to form vesicles. This lets cells take in materials too large to cross the membrane and recycle membrane proteins. Two processes: endocytosis (receptors and their ligands) and phagocytosis (particles).

Bulk-phase endocytosis (pinocytosis)Receptor-mediated endocytosis (RME)
WhatNon-specific uptake of extracellular fluid and anything dissolved in itSpecific uptake of ligands bound to surface receptors
Also called“Cell drinking”Clathrin-mediated endocytosis
Benefit—Efficient even at low ligand concentrations (hormones, growth factors, enzymes, nutrient carriers)
  • Receptors gather in coated pits, at 10–20 times their concentration elsewhere.
  • The coated vesicles have adaptors between the clathrin and the membrane; the best studied is AP2, which has multiple subunits (unlike the single-subunit GGAs at the TGN).

Endosomes

  • A dynamic network of tubules and vesicles: distribution centres of the endocytic pathway.
  • Their lumen is acidified by an H⁺-ATPase.
  • Early endosomes: near the cell periphery. Late endosomes: nearer the nucleus; they also receive new lysosomal enzymes from the TGN (via MPRs, which return).
  • Housekeeping receptors drop their ligands because of the high H⁺, gather in tubular recycling compartments, and return to the surface. Released ligands (e.g. LDL) go to a sorting compartment, then late endosome, then lysosome.
  • Ubiquitinated signalling receptors are packed into small vesicles inside the late endosome.

LDL uptake and cholesterol

The first and best-understood example of RME: delivering cholesterol, needed for membranes and steroid hormones. Cholesterol is hydrophobic, so it travels in lipoproteins.

LDLHDL
StructureCore of ~1500 cholesterol molecules esterified to fatty acids; surrounded by a single layer of phospholipid and cholesterol with one apolipoprotein B-100, which binds the LDL receptorSimilar build, but with apolipoprotein A-I
Carries cholesterolFrom the liver to body cellsFrom cells back to the liver for excretion
High blood level↑ heart disease risk (“bad”)Thought to ↓ risk (“good”); raising it has less clear benefit
  1. LDL receptors sit in coated pits even without LDL, ready to bind it.
  2. LDL binds; the pit invaginates into a coated vesicle; the clathrin coat comes off.
  3. Receptors pass through early endosomes and return to the surface.
  4. LDL goes to late endosomes and lysosomes: protein degraded, cholesterol de-esterified and used (membranes, steroid hormones).
In the clinic: atherosclerosis and its drugs
  • High blood LDL is linked to atherosclerosis: plaques in the artery lining narrow vessels and seed clots; clots in coronary arteries are the leading cause of myocardial infarction. It is thought to be a chronic inflammatory response to LDL deposited in the vessel wall.
  • Statins (lovastatin, Lipitor) block HMG-CoA reductase, lowering LDL and heart attack risk.
  • PCSK9 is a protease that destroys LDL receptors in the liver; people with inactivating PCSK9 mutations have low LDL and less heart disease, so PCSK9 inhibitors lower LDL.
  • Niemann–Pick type C: lacks a protein that moves cholesterol out of lysosomes → cholesterol builds up → nerve degeneration and death in early childhood.

Phagocytosis

  • “Cell eating”: uptake of large particles (> 0.5 μm) by a few specialised cells.
  • Protists (amoebas, ciliates) feed this way: membrane folds enclose food and fuse into a phagosome, which fuses with a lysosome → phagolysosome.
  • In animals it is mostly protective: “professional” phagocytes (macrophages, neutrophils) engulf microbes, damaged and dead cells, and debris, recognised by surface receptors.
  • Microbes are killed by lysosomal enzymes or oxygen free radicals made in the phagosome.
  • Engulfment is driven by actin microfilaments under the plasma membrane.

Lysosomal storage diseases

Lack of one lysosomal enzyme → its undegraded substrate builds up. More than 40 are known, affecting about 1 in 5000 infants.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • The lysosomal tag is mannose 6-phosphate, added in the Golgi.
  • LDL receptors sit in coated pits even without LDL.
  • Ubiquitin targets signalling receptors for destruction, not recycling.
  • Pompe is a glycogen (lysosomal α-glucosidase) disease.
  • Phagocytosis uses actin, not clathrin.
  • PCSK9 destroys LDL receptors; inhibiting it lowers LDL.

Key terms

TGN
Trans Golgi network, the sorting station.
Mannose 6-phosphate
Sorting tag of lysosomal enzymes.
Acid hydrolase
Lysosomal enzyme active at low pH.
Autophagy
Lysosomal digestion of the cell’s own components.
Residual body
Lysosome after digestion is complete.
Lipofuscin
Age pigment from residual bodies.
Coated pit
Clathrin-coated membrane region concentrating receptors.
Receptor down-regulation
Destruction of signalling receptors to lower sensitivity.
Endosome
Acidic sorting compartment of the endocytic pathway.
LDL / HDL
Low- / high-density lipoprotein.
Phagolysosome
Phagosome fused with a lysosome.
Lysosomal storage disease
Disease from a missing lysosomal enzyme.
