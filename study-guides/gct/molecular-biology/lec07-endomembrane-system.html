The Endomembrane System and Protein Sorting

The membrane organelles that work as one unit (ER, Golgi, lysosomes, endosomes, vacuoles), the biosynthetic, secretory and endocytic pathways, signal sequences, the jobs of rough and smooth ER, the Golgi, lysosomes and peroxisomes, and the diseases added for the midterm.

Labobo topic: MB Lec 7: Protein Sorting & Endomembrane

In 60 seconds

  • Endomembrane system = ER, Golgi, lysosomes, endosomes, vacuoles; not mitochondria or chloroplasts. Discovered with the electron microscope (1940s).
  • Pathways: biosynthetic/secretory (ER → Golgi → out) and endocytic (surface → endosomes → lysosomes).
  • Secretion: constitutive (continuous) or regulated (stored, released on a signal: hormones, digestive enzymes, neurotransmitters).
  • Signal sequence at the N-terminus (6–15 hydrophobic residues) → SRP → SRP receptor on the ER.
  • ~⅓ of proteins are made on RER (co-translational); free ribosomes make cytosolic, nuclear, mitochondrial and peroxisomal proteins.
  • SER: lipids, steroids, detoxification (cytochrome P-450), Ca²⁺ storage (sarcoplasmic reticulum). Reticulons curve SER tubules.
  • Golgi: cis receives, trans ships; makes lysosomes. Peroxisomes: VLCFA oxidation, plasmalogens, H₂O₂/catalase. Zellweger = empty peroxisome “ghosts”.

The endomembrane system

  • The cytoplasm’s extensive membrane network was discovered after the electron microscope was developed in the 1940s: membrane-bound channels forming connected canals, and stacks of flattened sacs.
  • The cytoplasm is divided into specialised membrane compartments, recognisable from yeast to plants and animals.
  • ER, Golgi complex, lysosomes, endosomes and vacuoles work together as the endomembrane system.
  • Mitochondria and chloroplasts are not part of it.
  • It is dynamic: materials shuttle between compartments in vesicles. Vesicles bud from a donor membrane, carrying selected membrane proteins and soluble proteins bound to receptors.

EndocyticOpposite direction: cell surface → endosomes → lysosomes, where material is usually degraded

ConstitutiveRegulated
ReleaseContinuous, straight after synthesisStored in packages, released only on a stimulus

Proteins, lipids and complex polysaccharides all travel along the biosynthetic/secretory pathway.

Sorting signals

  • Cargo (secreted proteins, lysosomal enzymes, membrane proteins) reaches the right place thanks to “addresses” (sorting signals) in the amino acid sequence or in attached oligosaccharides.
  • Most ER-bound proteins have a signal sequence at the N-terminus, including 6–15 hydrophobic residues.
  • The signal recognition particle (SRP) recognises it and binds the SRP receptor on the ER, moving the protein into the RER lumen.
  • Later, sorting signals are read by receptors in budding vesicles, sending each protein to the right compartment.
  • Another example: nuclear localisation signals, recognised by nuclear import receptors.

Rough and smooth ER

The ER is a network of membranes through much of the cytoplasm, probably evolved from infoldings of the plasma membrane.

Rough ER (RER)Smooth ER (SER)
RibosomesBound to its cytosolic surfaceNone
ShapeFlattened sacs (cisternae) joined by helicoidal membranes; continuous with the outer nuclear membraneHighly curved tubules, a network of pipelines; curvature made by reticulons (few in RER)
SharedMany proteins and some activities, such as synthesis of certain membrane lipids
Abundant inProtein-secreting cells: pancreas, salivary glandsSkeletal muscle, kidney tubules, steroid-producing glands

Functions of the SER

  • General: makes membrane lipids (phospholipids, cholesterol).
  • Steroid hormones in the gonads and adrenal cortex.
  • Detoxification in the liver (barbiturates, ethanol) by oxygenases, including the cytochrome P-450 family; chronic use makes liver SER proliferate.
  • Ca²⁺ storage; in muscle it is the sarcoplasmic reticulum, whose regulated Ca²⁺ release triggers contraction.

Golgi apparatus

  • A stack of flattened membrane discs that sorts, modifies, packages and ships ER products, like a post office.
  • In plants it also makes carbohydrates (polysaccharides, glycosaminoglycans).
  • Two faces: cis receives vesicles from the ER; trans releases repackaged products in new vesicles.
  • Products go to other parts of the cell or are exocytosed (the vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane).
  • The Golgi packages hydrolytic enzymes into lysosomes.

Lysosomes

  • Contain hydrolytic enzymes from the Golgi; vesicles form new lysosomes or fuse with existing ones.
  • Digest unneeded parts, such as old or damaged organelles: autophagy (“self-eating”).
  • Digest foreign material: white blood cells phagocytose bacteria and deliver them to lysosomes. Phagocytes have many lysosomes.
  • In damaged cells, lysosomes may burst and release enzymes into the cytoplasm, killing the cell: autolysis.

Peroxisomes

  • Simple membrane vesicles (0.1–1.0 μm), sometimes with a dense crystalline core of oxidative enzymes; more than 50 enzymes.
  • Jobs:
    • oxidise very-long-chain fatty acids (24–26 carbons);
    • make plasmalogens (one fatty acid joined by an ether bond), abundant in myelin; defects cause severe neurological disease;
    • firefly luciferase is a peroxisomal enzyme.
  • Named for hydrogen peroxide: made by urate oxidase, glycolate oxidase and amino acid oxidases (using O₂), and destroyed by abundant catalase.

Diseases for the midterm

Zellweger syndrome
  • Rare inherited disease: neurological, visual and liver problems; death in early infancy.
  • Liver and kidney cells seemed to lack peroxisomes, but actually had empty membrane “ghosts”: enzymes fail to be imported and stay in the cytosol, useless.
  • Caused by mutations in at least 12 genes, all for peroxisomal enzyme import.
Alzheimer’s disease and tau

Defined by extracellular amyloid-β (cut from APP), neurofibrillary tangles of hyperphosphorylated tau inside neurons, and neuron loss. Normally tau, a microtubule-associated protein, assembles and stabilises microtubules; in AD and other tauopathies it forms insoluble filaments.

Most complications (cardiovascular disease, blindness, kidney disease, amputations) come from blood-vessel damage.

Watch out

Watch out: peroxisome size

Slide 27 says peroxisomes are “0.1 to 10 μm”. Karp gives 0.1 to 1.0 μm; the “10” is a typo.

Watch out: autolysis and apoptosis

Slide 26 links lysosomal autolysis to apoptosis. Apoptosis is mainly driven by caspases and mitochondria; uncontrolled release of lysosomal enzymes is more typical of cell death by necrosis. Remember the term autolysis = self-digestion by the cell’s own lysosomal enzymes.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Mitochondria and chloroplasts are not in the endomembrane system.
  • Signal sequences are at the N-terminus and are hydrophobic.
  • Peripheral membrane proteins (spectrin) are made on free ribosomes.
  • Cytochrome P-450 detoxification is in the SER.
  • The cis Golgi faces the ER; trans ships out.
  • Zellweger peroxisomes are present but empty.

Key terms

Endomembrane system
ER, Golgi, lysosomes, endosomes, vacuoles working together.
Biosynthetic pathway
ER → Golgi → destinations.
Endocytic pathway
Surface → endosomes → lysosomes.
Signal sequence
N-terminal hydrophobic address for the ER.
SRP
Signal recognition particle.
Cisternae
Flattened membrane sacs.
Reticulons
Membrane-bending proteins of the SER.
Sarcoplasmic reticulum
Muscle SER storing Ca²⁺.
Co-translational translocation
Moving a protein into the ER while it is being made.
Autophagy
Digestion of the cell’s own parts by lysosomes.
Plasmalogen
Ether phospholipid made in peroxisomes.
