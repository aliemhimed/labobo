Co-translational Translocation and the Rough ER

How a protein is guided into the ER while it is still being made (SRP, receptor, translocon, G proteins), what happens to it inside (signal peptidase, glycosylation, disulfide bonds), how N-linked sugars are built on dolichol, and how membrane proteins and lipids are made with the right orientation.

Labobo topic: MB Lec 8: Co-translational Translocation to ER

In 60 seconds

  • RER makes secretory, lysosomal/vacuolar and integral membrane proteins, processes and glycosylates them, and makes most membrane lipids.
  • Signal sequence (N-terminal, 6–15 hydrophobic residues) → SRP (6 polypeptides + RNA) binds signal and ribosome → SRP receptor → translocon; the signal displaces the plug and opens the channel.
  • SRP and its receptor are G proteins: GTP/GDP switches.
  • In the lumen: signal peptidase removes the signal; oligosaccharyltransferase adds sugars; PDI forms disulfide bonds.
  • N-linked core Glc₃Man₉GlcNAc₂ is built on dolichol (first 7 sugars on the cytosolic side, flip, then 7 more in the lumen) and moved onto Asn in Asn-X-Ser/Thr.
  • No N-glycosylation at all = embryo dies before implantation; partial defects = serious multi-organ disorders.
  • Membrane proteins keep the orientation they get in the ER; sugars always face the lumen → exoplasmic side of the plasma membrane.

Functions of the rough ER

  • Protein synthesis: secretory proteins; soluble proteins of endomembrane organelles (lysosomal, plant vacuolar); integral membrane proteins.
  • Processing of new proteins.
  • Glycosylation.
  • Synthesis of most membrane lipids.

Co-translational translocation

Secretory and lysosomal proteins enter the ER while they are being translated.

  1. The new protein starts with a signal sequence at its N-terminus (6–15 hydrophobic amino acids).
  2. Step 1: the signal recognition particle (SRP), made of 6 polypeptides and an RNA molecule, binds both the signal sequence and the ribosome (pausing translation).
  3. Step 2: the SRP–ribosome–nascent chain complex is brought to the RER by SRP binding the SRP receptor.
  4. Step 3: the ribosome moves onto the translocon, a protein channel in the ER membrane through which the chain passes into the lumen.
  5. Step 4: contact of the signal sequence with the translocon displaces the plug and opens the channel; the chain threads in as it grows.

G proteins as switches

  • Several steps are controlled by binding or hydrolysis of GTP, by GTP-binding proteins (G proteins). SRP and the SRP receptor are both G proteins.
  • A G protein exists in two shapes: GTP-bound and GDP-bound, which bind other proteins differently.
  • So they act as molecular switches, turning processes on and off (e.g. GTP hydrolysis releases SRP once the ribosome is docked).

Disulfide bonds keep proteins stable on the outer cell surface and in the extracellular space.

N-linked glycosylation

  • Almost all proteins made on membrane-bound ribosomes become glycoproteins.
  • Sugars serve as binding sites for other molecules and help folding and stability.
  • Glycosyltransferases add sugars one at a time from nucleotide sugars (e.g. GDP-mannose, UDP-N-acetylglucosamine).
  • N-linked = oligosaccharide attached to the nitrogen of asparagine (Asn), in the sequence Asn–X–Ser/Thr.

Building the core on dolichol

  1. On the cytosolic side, the first 7 sugars (2 GlcNAc + 5 mannose) are added one by one to dolichol pyrophosphate, a lipid anchor in the membrane.
  2. The dolichol-oligosaccharide flips to the luminal side.
  3. The last 7 sugars (4 mannose + 3 glucose) are added in the lumen, each first attached to dolichol phosphate on the cytosolic side and flipped across.
  4. The finished core, Glc₃Man₉GlcNAc₂ (14 sugars), is transferred by oligosaccharyltransferase onto Asn in N–X–S/T of the new protein.
  5. Dolichol-PP flips back, ready to start again.
In the clinic

Complete absence of N-glycosylation kills the embryo before implantation. Partial defects cause serious inherited disorders affecting nearly every organ system (from the textbook: the congenital disorders of glycosylation).

Making integral membrane proteins

  • SRP and the same machinery are used.
  • The chain enters the translocon like a secretory protein, but its hydrophobic transmembrane segment blocks further passage.
  • Depending on the charges flanking that segment, the protein ends up with:
    • N-terminus in the lumen, C-terminus in the cytosol, or
    • C-terminus in the lumen, N-terminus in the cytosol (the translocon flips the segment to match its reversed charged flanks).
  • The translocon then opens sideways and releases the segment into the lipid bilayer.

Orientation is kept

  • A protein’s orientation, set by its amino acid sequence in the ER, is kept throughout the endomembrane system.
  • Sugar chains, first added in the ER lumen, end up on the exoplasmic (outer) side of the plasma membrane: lumen side = outside of the cell.

Membrane biosynthesis

  • Membranes are never made from scratch; they grow from pre-existing membranes as new proteins and lipids are inserted, mainly in the ER.
  • Most membrane lipids are made entirely in the ER. Exceptions:
    • sphingomyelin and glycolipids: started in the ER, finished in the Golgi;
    • some unique lipids of mitochondrial and chloroplast membranes.
  • Lipids travel from the ER to the Golgi and plasma membrane as part of the walls of transport vesicles.

Watch out

Watch out: “N-linked” is not “N-terminus”

Slide 12 says sugars are added “at the N-terminus of the protein”. N-linked means attached to the nitrogen of an asparagine side chain (in Asn–X–Ser/Thr), wherever it is in the chain, not to the N-terminus.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • SRP contains RNA as well as protein.
  • SRP and SRP receptor are G proteins.
  • The first oligosaccharide sugars are added on the cytosolic side, then flipped.
  • The donor lipid is dolichol, not a phospholipid.
  • PDI works in the ER lumen.
  • Sphingomyelin synthesis is finished in the Golgi.

Key terms

Co-translational translocation
Entry of a protein into the ER during its synthesis.
SRP
Signal recognition particle.
Translocon
Protein channel in the ER membrane.
G protein
GTP/GDP-binding molecular switch.
Signal peptidase
Removes the signal peptide.
Oligosaccharyltransferase
Transfers the core sugar to Asn.
PDI
Protein disulfide isomerase.
Dolichol
Lipid carrier for building N-linked sugars.
Glycosyltransferase
Adds a specific sugar from a nucleotide sugar.
Exoplasmic side
Membrane face toward the lumen/outside.
