Types of Epithelia and Glands

How covering epithelia are classified (layers and surface cell shape), the special types (transitional, pseudostratified), and secretory epithelia: exocrine and endocrine glands, gland shapes, serous and mucous secretion, and merocrine, apocrine and holocrine release.

Labobo topic: Histo Lec 14: Types of Epithelia and Glands

In 60 seconds

  • Two groups: covering (lining) and secretory (glandular) epithelia.
  • Covering epithelia are named by number of layers (simple = 1, stratified = 2+) and the shape of the outermost cells (squamous, cuboidal, columnar).
  • Stratified squamous: keratinised (epidermis; stops water loss) or non-keratinised (mouth, oesophagus, cornea; surface cells keep nuclei). Stratified cuboidal: gland ducts. Stratified columnar: conjunctiva.
  • Transitional (urothelium): dome-shaped umbrella cells, stretches; urinary tract. Pseudostratified: all cells touch the basement membrane, nuclei at different levels; airways, ciliated.
  • Exocrine glands keep a duct; endocrine glands have no duct and release hormones into blood. Pancreas is both.
  • Shapes: simple vs compound (branched duct); tubular, acinar, tubulo-acinar. Secretion: serous (protein) or mucous.
  • Release: merocrine (exocytosis), apocrine (with apical cytoplasm; mammary), holocrine (whole cell ruptures; sebaceous).

Learning objectives

  • classify covering epithelia and give a location for each;
  • describe transitional and pseudostratified epithelium;
  • distinguish exocrine and endocrine glands;
  • classify exocrine glands by duct, secretory unit, product and mechanism.

Classifying covering epithelia

  • Cells in one or more layers that cover surfaces or line cavities.
  • Named by:
    1. number of layers: simple (one) or stratified (two or more);
    2. shape of the cells in the outer (surface) layer: squamous (flat), cuboidal (as tall as wide), columnar (taller than wide).

Transitional and pseudostratified

Transitional epithelium (urothelium)

  • Lines the urinary tract only (renal pelvis, ureters, bladder, part of urethra).
  • Several layers with large, dome-shaped surface cells: umbrella cells.
  • Protects underlying tissue from urine and stretches as the bladder fills: relaxed = many layers with domed cells; stretched = fewer, flatter layers.

Pseudostratified columnar epithelium

  • Every cell touches the basement membrane, but not all reach the lumen; nuclei sit at different levels, so it looks stratified but is really one layer.
  • Found in the upper respiratory tract (trachea, bronchi), usually ciliated with goblet cells (“respiratory epithelium”).

Secretory epithelia

  • Epithelial cells that secrete macromolecules, either in glands or scattered within other epithelia.
  • Products:
    • proteins (e.g. pancreas);
    • lipids (e.g. adrenal and sebaceous glands);
    • carbohydrate + protein (e.g. salivary glands);
    • mostly water and electrolytes (sweat glands, little synthesis).
  • Scattered secretory cells: goblet cells in the intestine and airways secrete mucus for lubrication (unicellular glands).
  • Glands develop in the fetus as epithelium grows down into the connective tissue and differentiates.

  • Myoepithelial cells around some exocrine units contract to push secretion into the ducts.
  • Some glands, such as the pancreas, are both exocrine and endocrine.
  • Epithelia also transport: absorbing ions and water in the kidney, and transcytosis across cells.

Gland structure

  • Simple gland: duct not branched. Compound gland: duct with two or more branches.
  • Secretory units are:
    • tubular: short, long or coiled (e.g. sweat gland);
    • acinar (alveolar): rounded, sac-like.
  • Either kind of secretory unit may be branched even if the duct is not.
  • Compound glands may have tubular, acinar or tubulo-acinar units.

Mnemonic

Mero = “part” of nothing lost; Apo = loses its Apex; Holo = the whole cell goes.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Stratified epithelia are named by the surface layer, not the basal one.
  • Pseudostratified is really one layer.
  • Transitional = urinary tract only.
  • Non-keratinised surface cells keep their nuclei.
  • Sebaceous = holocrine; mammary = apocrine.
  • Endocrine glands have no ducts.

Key terms

Simple / stratified
One layer / two or more layers.
Keratinisation
Filling of cells with keratin as they die at the surface.
Urothelium
Transitional epithelium of the urinary tract.
Pseudostratified
One layer that looks layered.
Terminal bar
Light-microscope view of apical junctions.
Exocrine / endocrine
With duct / ductless, into blood.
Acinus
Rounded sac-like secretory unit.
Serous / mucous
Watery protein / viscous mucus secretion.
Myoepithelial cell
Contractile cell squeezing glands.
