Lysosomes, Proteasomes and Peroxisomes

The cell’s three clean-up and detox systems: lysosomes (acid hydrolases, M6P, heterolysosomes, residual bodies, autophagy), proteasomes (ubiquitin, ATP) and peroxisomes (H2O2, catalase, β-oxidation). Mitochondria from the same deck are in the Lecture 6 guide.

Labobo topic: Histo Lec 5: Lysosomes & Peroxisomes

In 60 seconds

  • Lysosomes: single-membrane spheres, 0.05–0.5 μm, ~40 acid hydrolases, work best at pH ~5 (cytosol 7.2 keeps leaked enzymes inactive). Abundant in macrophages and neutrophils.
  • Enzymes made in RER → Golgi adds mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) → M6P receptors send them to lysosomes.
  • Phagosome/pinocytotic vesicle + lysosome = secondary lysosome (heterolysosome) → undigested remains = residual body → lipofuscin in long-lived cells (neurons, muscle).
  • Autophagy: SER membrane wraps old organelles → autophagosome → fuses with lysosome. Increases in starvation.
  • Proteasomes: cylinders of protease rings with regulatory caps; destroy single ubiquitin-tagged proteins using ATP.
  • Peroxisomes: single membrane; oxidases make H2O2, catalase breaks it down; detox (liver, kidney), β-oxidation of long-chain fatty acids, bile acids, cholesterol. Proteins imported from the cytosol.

Lysosomes

  • Small membrane-bound organelles that break down cell waste and components: the cell’s digestive system.
  • About 40 enzymes, mainly acid hydrolases (proteases, nucleases, lipases, glycosidases, phosphatases) that digest proteins, nucleic acids, lipids and sugars.
  • Especially abundant in phagocytic cells: macrophages and neutrophils.
  • Usually spherical, 0.05–0.5 μm; dense and granular in the electron microscope. In macrophages they can be large enough to see by light microscopy with special stains.

Why the cell does not digest itself

  • The lysosomal membrane keeps the enzymes apart from the cytosol.
  • The enzymes work best at acidic pH ≈ 5.0. The cytosol is neutral, ≈ 7.2, so any leaked enzymes are inactive.

Making lysosomes: mannose-6-phosphate

  1. Lysosomal enzymes are made in the rough ER.
  2. They travel to the Golgi, where a marker, mannose-6-phosphate (M6P), is added.
  3. M6P receptors in the Golgi recognise the enzymes and send them into lysosomes rather than the secretory pathway.

Digestion of endocytosed material

  1. Material enters by endocytosis in a phagosome or pinocytotic vesicle.
  2. It fuses with a lysosome → secondary lysosome (heterolysosome); contents are mixed with enzymes and acidified.
  3. Digested nutrients pass into the cytosol.
  4. Undigested material stays in a residual body.
  5. In long-lived cells (neurons, heart and skeletal muscle) residual bodies accumulate as the brown pigment lipofuscin (“age pigment”).

Autophagy

  • “Self-eating”: lysosomes remove damaged or excess organelles.
  • A membrane from the smooth ER surrounds the material → an autophagosome → fuses with a lysosome for digestion.
  • Increases under stress such as starvation; the products are recycled.

Proteasomes

  • Small, abundant protein complexes (no membrane) that destroy damaged, misfolded or unneeded proteins.
  • Lysosomes digest whole organelles and endocytosed material; proteasomes destroy individual proteins.
  • Structure: a cylinder of stacked rings containing proteases, with a regulatory particle at each end that recognises ubiquitin.
  1. An unwanted protein is tagged with a chain of ubiquitin molecules.
  2. The cap recognises it; the protein is unfolded using ATP and fed into the cylinder.
  3. It is cut into peptides inside; ubiquitin is recycled.
  4. Peptides are broken down further, or used by the immune system (antigen presentation).

Peroxisomes

  • Spherical organelles with a single membrane.
  • Contain enzymes that make and destroy hydrogen peroxide (H2O2):
    • oxidases remove hydrogen atoms from substrates and pass them to O2 → H2O2;
    • peroxidases such as catalase break H2O2 down (to water and O2) so it cannot damage the cell.
  • Detoxify harmful substances and some drugs, especially in liver and kidney cells.
  • Lipid metabolism: β-oxidation of long-chain fatty acids; help make bile acids and cholesterol.
  • Formed by budding from the ER or by division of existing peroxisomes.
  • Their proteins are made on free ribosomes in the cytoplasm and carry targeting sequences for import.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Lysosomal enzymes are inactive at pH 7.2; that protects the cell.
  • M6P is added in the Golgi, not the ER.
  • Lipofuscin = residual bodies in long-lived cells.
  • The autophagosome membrane comes from the SER.
  • Proteasomes need ATP and ubiquitin; ubiquitin is recycled.
  • Peroxisomes both make (oxidases) and destroy (catalase) H2O2.

Key terms

Acid hydrolase
Lysosomal enzyme active at acid pH.
M6P
Mannose-6-phosphate: lysosome address tag.
Heterolysosome
Lysosome digesting material from outside.
Residual body
Lysosome with undigested remains.
Lipofuscin
Brown “age pigment” of residual bodies.
Autophagy
Lysosomal digestion of the cell’s own parts.
Ubiquitin
Small protein tag marking proteins for proteasomes.
Catalase
Enzyme splitting H2O2.
