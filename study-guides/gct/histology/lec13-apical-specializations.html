Specialisations of the Apical Surface

Microvilli, stereocilia, cilia and flagella: what they are made of, how big they are, where they are found and what they do.

Labobo topic: Histo Lec 13: Apical Surface Specializations

In 60 seconds

  • Apical projections either increase surface area (absorption) or move things along the surface.
  • Microvilli: ~1 μm long, 0.1 μm wide; core of bundled actin anchored in the terminal web; covered by enzyme-rich glycocalyx; form the brush border (small intestine).
  • Stereocilia: rare, longer microvilli-like (actin), may branch; male reproductive tract (absorption) and inner ear (sensing motion). Not true cilia.
  • Motile cilia: 5–10 μm long, 0.2 μm wide; microtubules in a 9 + 2 axoneme anchored to a basal body; beat using dynein + ATP.
  • Primary cilium: one non-motile sensory cilium on most cells (light, odours, fluid flow). Sperm flagellum has the same axoneme.

Learning objectives

  • describe the structure, size, location and function of microvilli, stereocilia and cilia;
  • explain the 9 + 2 axoneme and how cilia beat;
  • distinguish motile cilia, primary cilia and flagella.

Why apical specialisations?

Many columnar and cuboidal epithelial cells have structures projecting from their apical end. They either:

  • increase the apical surface area for better absorption (microvilli, stereocilia), or
  • move substances along the epithelial surface (motile cilia).

Microvilli

  • Many cells have temporary, variable projections driven by actin. Absorptive epithelial cells have numerous, uniform microvilli that hugely increase surface area.
  • In the small intestine, densely packed microvilli form the brush border (striated border), visible by light microscopy as a fuzzy apical band. (Also in kidney proximal tubules.)
  • Each is about 1 μm long and 0.1 μm wide.
  • Covered by a glycocalyx containing enzymes that finish digesting macromolecules.
  • Inside: a bundle of actin filaments stabilised by actin-binding proteins and anchored in the terminal web (a network of actin and other filaments under the apical membrane).
  • Though stable, they show small movements driven by myosin, helping absorption through membrane channels and receptors.

Stereocilia

  • Rare apical projections on:
    • absorptive cells of the male reproductive tract (epididymis, ductus deferens);
    • sensory hair cells of the inner ear.
  • Like microvilli: increase surface area for absorption; contain actin linked to the terminal web.
  • Unlike microvilli: much longer, less motile, and may branch at their tips.
  • In the inner ear, they detect motion (sound and head movement).

Cilia and flagella

  • Long, motile apical structures, larger than microvilli, built of microtubules (not actin).
  • Size: 5–10 μm long and 0.2 μm wide.
  • Core = the axoneme: 9 + 2 arrangement (9 peripheral microtubule doublets around 2 central single microtubules).
  • Each cilium is anchored to a basal body in the apical cytoplasm (a centriole-like structure).
  • Kinesin and dynein carry building blocks up and down the axoneme.
  • Cilia beat in coordinated waves, powered by ATP and dynein arms that slide the doublets, moving fluid or particles (e.g. mucus in the airways, the egg in the oviduct).
  • Primary cilium: most cells have one non-motile cilium that senses the environment (light, odours, fluid flow).
  • Sperm flagella have the same axoneme and mechanism.

Watch out

Watch out: the name “stereocilia”

Despite the name, stereocilia are not cilia: they contain actin, not microtubules, and have no axoneme or basal body. They are long microvilli.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Microvilli and stereocilia = actin; cilia = microtubules.
  • Cilia beat with dynein, not myosin.
  • Brush border = microvilli.
  • Axoneme = 9 + 2; it grows from a basal body.
  • The primary cilium is non-motile and sensory.
