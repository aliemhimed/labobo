Stem Cells, Meiosis and Apoptosis

How tissues renew from stem cells and progenitors, how meiosis makes four unique haploid cells, and how apoptosis removes cells cleanly, unlike necrosis.

Labobo topic: Histo Lec 11: Stem Cells, Meiosis and Apoptosis

In 60 seconds

  • Stem cells: few, undifferentiated, divide rarely and asymmetrically (one stem cell + one committed cell); live in niches.
  • Most dividing cells in renewing tissues (blood, skin, gut) are progenitor (transit-amplifying) cells, which divide fast then differentiate. Stable tissues (connective tissue, smooth muscle, endothelium) divide slowly and episodically.
  • Meiosis: two divisions, only in germ cells. Meiosis I separates homologues (after synapsis and crossing over); meiosis II separates sister chromatids with no new DNA replication → 4 unique haploid cells.
  • Apoptosis: fast, regulated, no inflammation. Bcl-2 family → cytochrome c → caspases; endonucleases fragment DNA; shrinkage, pyknotic nucleus, blebbing, apoptotic bodies eaten by phagocytes.
  • Necrosis: swelling and bursting → contents leak → inflammation.

Learning objectives

  • define stem, progenitor and differentiated cells and describe tissue renewal;
  • describe the stages and outcome of meiosis and compare it with mitosis;
  • describe the mechanism, features and roles of apoptosis;
  • distinguish apoptosis from necrosis.

Stem cells and tissue renewal

  • Stem cells are a small population of undifferentiated cells in many tissues; they renew differentiated cells throughout life.
  • They divide infrequently and asymmetrically: each division gives one stem cell (self-renewal) + one cell committed to differentiation.
  • They live in special microenvironments, niches, that keep them undifferentiated. They are rare and hard to spot in routine histology.

Meiosis

  • Two consecutive divisions, only in germ cells, producing sperm and eggs.
  • Makes haploid cells with unique gene combinations, essential for sexual reproduction.
  • Synapsis: homologous chromosomes pair. Crossing over: reciprocal DNA exchange between them → new gene combinations.
  • Haploid gametes combine at fertilisation to form a diploid zygote.
  1. DNA replication (before meiosis I).
  2. Meiosis I: homologues pair, cross over, and separate. Each daughter still has chromosomes with two sister chromatids (reductional division).
  3. Meiosis II: no DNA replication; sister chromatids separate (like mitosis).
  4. Result: four haploid cells, each genetically unique.

Apoptosis

Programmed cell death: rapid, regulated removal of defective or unneeded cells without inflammation. Apoptotic cells shrink and are removed as membrane-bound apoptotic bodies by phagocytosis, so contents never leak out.

FeatureWhat happens
Mitochondrial stepBcl-2 family proteins open mitochondrial membranes → cytochrome c released → activates caspases (proteases) that degrade proteins
DNA fragmentationEndonucleases cut DNA into small fragments, detectable by special stains
ShrinkageCytoskeleton and chromatin break down → cell shrinks; small dense pyknotic nucleus
Membrane changesBlebbing; exposed phospholipids (phosphatidylserine) signal “eat me”
RemovalApoptotic bodies quickly engulfed by neighbours or white blood cells

Roles of apoptosis

  • Shapes organs in the embryo, e.g. separating fingers and toes.
  • Removes excess cells: oocytes in the ovary, nerve cells during CNS development.
  • Eliminates damaged or stressed cells, often triggered by p53.
  • It is quick and leaves no trace, keeping tissues healthy.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Most dividing cells in a renewing tissue are progenitors, not stem cells.
  • Stem cells divide slowly; progenitors divide fast.
  • There is no DNA replication before meiosis II.
  • Homologues separate in meiosis I; sister chromatids in meiosis II.
  • Apoptosis causes no inflammation; necrosis does.
  • Caspases are activated by cytochrome c.
