The Cell Cycle and Mitosis

The four phases of the cell cycle, how cyclins and CDKs drive it, the checkpoints and p53, the stages of mitosis, and how tissues differ in their ability to renew.

Labobo topic: Histo Lec 10: Cell Cycle and Mitosis

In 60 seconds

  • Cycle: G1 → S → G2 → M. Interphase = G1 + S + G2 (the long part).
  • G1: growth, RNA and protein synthesis; variable length. S: DNA replication, histone synthesis, centrosome duplication starts. G2: proteins for division. M: division.
  • Driven by cyclins + cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs), which phosphorylate targets; each cyclin is destroyed when its phase ends.
  • Checkpoints: G1/S (nutrients, readiness), S (replication accuracy), G2/M (DNA damage). Unrepairable damage → p53 → apoptosis. Mutant p53 → cancer.
  • Mitosis: prophase (condense, spindle, envelope breaks down) → metaphase (equator, kinetochores) → anaphase (sisters separate) → telophase + cytokinesis (new envelopes; actin contractile ring).
  • Fast renewal: gut, skin, uterus. No division: neurons, cardiac muscle. Renewal depends on stem cells.

Learning objectives

  • list the cell-cycle phases and what happens in each;
  • explain how cyclins and CDKs control the cycle;
  • describe the checkpoints and the role of p53;
  • describe each stage of mitosis and cytokinesis;
  • classify tissues by their ability to regenerate.

Phases of the cell cycle

The cell cycle is how cells grow, replicate their DNA and divide.

PhaseWhat happens
G1 (gap 1)Between mitosis and DNA replication. The cell grows, makes RNA and proteins, prepares to replicate. Most variable in length (cells that stop dividing leave here into G0).
S (synthesis)DNA replication and histone synthesis; centrosome duplication begins.
G2 (gap 2)Before mitosis; makes the proteins needed for division.
M (mitosis)The cell divides into two.

Interphase = G1 + S + G2, the long period between mitoses.

Cyclins and CDKs

  • Progress is controlled by cyclins and their partner enzymes, cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs).
  • A CDK only works when its cyclin binds it. Different cyclins activate specific CDKs in each phase.
  • Active CDKs phosphorylate target proteins, pushing the cycle forward.
  • When a phase is done, its cyclin is destroyed (by proteasomes), letting the next cyclin take over. This keeps the cycle moving in one direction.

Checkpoints and p53

CheckpointChecks
G1/SEnough nutrients; ready for DNA replication
S phaseAccuracy of DNA replication
G2/MDNA damage before mitosis
  1. DNA damage is detected → the cycle halts to allow repair.
  2. Damage cannot be fixed → tumour suppressor proteins such as p53 trigger apoptosis.
Clinical link: cancer

Unrepaired damage, or mutations in key genes (growth factors, receptors, p53), allow uncontrolled division, the hallmark of cancer. p53 is mutated in many cancers, so damaged cells are not removed and genetic defects pile up.

Mitosis

One parent cell → two identical daughter cells, each with a complete set of chromosomes. It follows S-phase replication and is visible by light microscopy.

StageEvents
1. ProphaseChromatin condenses into visible chromosomes (two sister chromatids joined at the centromere). Centrosomes move to opposite poles and form the mitotic spindle. The nuclear envelope breaks down.
2. MetaphaseChromosomes line up at the equator (metaphase plate), attached to spindle fibres by kinetochores.
3. AnaphaseSister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles as spindle fibres shorten and the poles move apart.
4. Telophase + cytokinesisChromosomes decondense; a new nuclear envelope forms around each set. A contractile ring of actin (with myosin) pinches the cytoplasm in two.
Mnemonic: PMAT

Prepare (condense), Middle (line up), Apart (separate), Two (two nuclei).

Cell turnover and regeneration

  • Most tissues have slow turnover: a little division balanced by a little cell death.
  • Rapid renewal: digestive tract lining, skin (epidermis), uterus (endometrium).
  • Cannot divide: neurons and cardiac muscle cells, so damage there is permanent.
  • A tissue’s ability to regenerate depends on its stem cells or cells able to undergo mitosis.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • DNA and histones are made in S phase.
  • G1 is the most variable phase.
  • Cyclins are destroyed after each phase; CDK levels stay fairly constant.
  • The nuclear envelope breaks down in prophase and re-forms in telophase.
  • Sister chromatids separate in anaphase.
  • The cytokinesis ring is actin, not microtubules.

Key terms

Interphase
G1 + S + G2.
Cyclin
Protein whose level rises and falls to activate CDKs.
CDK
Cyclin-dependent kinase.
Checkpoint
Control point that halts the cycle if conditions are wrong.
p53
Tumour suppressor that triggers repair or apoptosis.
Kinetochore
Protein complex on the centromere binding spindle fibres.
Cytokinesis
Division of the cytoplasm.
