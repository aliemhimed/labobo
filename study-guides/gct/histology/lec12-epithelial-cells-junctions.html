Epithelial Cells, Basement Membrane and Junctions

The four basic tissues, what makes epithelium special (polarity, no blood vessels), the basement membrane, and the junctions that seal, anchor and connect epithelial cells.

Labobo topic: Histo Lec 12: Epithelial Cells and Junctions

In 60 seconds

  • Four basic tissues: epithelial, connective, muscle, nervous. Organs = parenchyma (functional cells) + stroma (supporting connective tissue).
  • Epithelium: tightly packed cells in sheets; covers surfaces and lines cavities; protection, absorption, secretion. Avascular (fed by the lamina propria; papillae increase contact). Polarity: apical, lateral, basal.
  • Basement membrane = basal lamina (20–100 nm: type IV collagen, laminin, nidogen, perlecan) + reticular lamina (type III collagen, type VII anchoring fibrils).
  • Junctions: tight (claudin, occludin; seal) → adherens (cadherin–catenin–actin) → desmosome (desmoglein/desmocollin–intermediate filaments) → gap (connexins; communication).
  • Basal: hemidesmosomes (integrin–intermediate filaments–basal lamina) and focal adhesions (integrin–actin–ECM; dynamic).

Learning objectives

  • name the four basic tissues and define parenchyma and stroma;
  • list the general features of epithelia;
  • describe the layers and molecules of the basement membrane;
  • compare all intercellular and basal junctions by protein, filament and function.

  • Organs usually have parenchyma (the specialised functional cells) and stroma (supporting connective tissue).
  • Epithelia line all external and internal surfaces, acting as a barrier. Examples: protection (epidermis), absorption (intestinal lining), secretion (gland cells); some specialised epithelial cells contract (myoepithelial) or sense (taste, retina).

Features of epithelial cells

  • Shapes: tall columnar, cuboidal, flat squamous. Shape reflects function.
  • The nucleus follows the cell shape: elliptic (columnar), spherical (cuboidal), flattened (squamous). Nuclei are the best clue to cell shape and density under the microscope.
  • Classification uses the number of layers and the cell/nuclear shape.
  • No blood vessels (avascular): nutrients and O2 diffuse from the underlying connective tissue, the lamina propria. Where there is friction, the connective tissue forms papillae (finger-like projections) to increase contact.
  • Polarity:
    • basal pole: faces the connective tissue;
    • apical pole: faces an open space (lumen or outside);
    • lateral surfaces: face neighbouring cells, often folded to increase area.

Basement membrane

A thin extracellular sheet under all epithelia: a semipermeable filter and structural support.

LayerMade ofMade by
Basal laminaDense layer of fine fibrils, 20–100 nm: type IV collagen (2-D network), laminin, nidogen and perlecan (cross-link laminin to collagen; regulate filtration)Epithelial cells
Reticular laminaFibrous layer: type III collagen (reticular fibres) + type VII collagen anchoring fibrilsConnective tissue cells

Functions: filter, structural support, keeps cell polarity, cell signalling and migration, scaffold for repair. Similar layers (external laminae) surround muscle, nerve and fat cells.

Key idea: basement membrane vs basal lamina

The basement membrane (seen by light microscopy, PAS-positive) = basal lamina + reticular lamina. The basal lamina alone is only visible by electron microscopy.

Intercellular junctions

From apical to basal, epithelial cells are joined by:

Tight (occluding) junctions

  • A seal between neighbours, blocking the paracellular pathway (passage between cells).
  • Proteins: claudin and occludin.
  • Also stop membrane proteins drifting, so apical and basolateral domains stay distinct (a “fence”).

Adherens (anchoring) junctions

  • Zonula adherens: a belt around the cell just below the tight junction.
  • Strong adhesion via cadherins, linked through catenins to actin filaments.
  • Mechanical strength and integrity.

Desmosomes

  • Spot-like junctions (macula adherens).
  • Link intermediate filaments (cytokeratin) of neighbouring cells via the cadherins desmogleins and desmocollins.
  • Very strong; resist mechanical stress (abundant in skin).

Gap junctions

  • Communication: let ions and small signalling molecules pass from cell to cell.
  • Connexins (6) form a connexon with a hydrophilic pore; connexons of two cells line up.
  • Coordinate activity, e.g. rhythmic contraction of heart and smooth muscle.

Watch out

Watch out: laminin

The slide lists type IV collagen, nidogen and perlecan as basal lamina components and mentions laminin only in passing. Laminin is a major basal lamina component; include it.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Epithelium has no blood vessels (but does have nerves).
  • Basal lamina = type IV; reticular lamina = type III + VII.
  • Adherens → actin; desmosome → intermediate filaments.
  • Hemidesmosomes use integrins, not cadherins.
  • Gap junctions = connexins; tight junctions = claudins/occludin.

Key terms

Parenchyma / stroma
Functional cells / supporting tissue of an organ.
Lamina propria
Connective tissue under an epithelium.
Papillae
Connective tissue projections into epithelium.
Polarity
Different apical, lateral and basal surfaces.
Basal lamina
Type IV collagen–laminin sheet under epithelium.
Paracellular
Between cells.
Cadherin / integrin
Cell–cell / cell–matrix adhesion protein.
Connexon
Six connexins forming a gap junction channel.
