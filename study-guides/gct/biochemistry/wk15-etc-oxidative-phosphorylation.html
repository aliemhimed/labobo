Electron Transport Chain and Oxidative Phosphorylation

How the mitochondrion turns NADH and FADH₂ into ATP: the four complexes, coenzyme Q and cytochrome c, the proton gradient, ATP synthase, uncouplers, the poisons that block the chain (cyanide, CO, rotenone) and Leigh syndrome.

Labobo topic: Biochem Wk 15: Cellular Respiration

In 60 seconds

  • The inner membrane is impermeable to H⁺, ATP, ADP and pyruvate; the outer membrane is freely permeable.
  • Order: Complex I → (II) → CoQ → Complex III → cytochrome c → Complex IV → O₂.
  • Protons pumped: I = 4, II = 0, III = 4, IV = 2. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor, becoming water.
  • ATP synthase (Complex V): F0 = proton channel in the membrane; F1 = catalytic head in the matrix (α₃β₃, only β makes ATP). About 4 H⁺ per ATP.
  • Statins lower CoQ synthesis too → possible muscle pain and weakness.
  • Inhibitors: rotenone, barbiturates (I), antimycin A (III), CO (IV, Fe²⁺), cyanide (IV, Fe³⁺). Cyanide antidotes: thiosulfate, hydroxocobalamin, nitrites.
  • Uncoupling proteins let H⁺ back in without making ATP → heat.

Inner membraneImpermeable to most small ions, including H⁺, and to ATP, ADP and pyruvate; needs specific carriers. Holds the ETC and ATP synthase
MatrixContains NAD⁺ and FAD (electron acceptors) and ADP and Pi (to make ATP)

Because the inner membrane does not leak H⁺, pumped protons stay outside and store energy as a gradient.

Oxidative phosphorylation in three parts

The final stage of cellular respiration, where cells make most of their ATP, using oxygen.

  1. Electron transport chain: electrons from NADH and FADH₂ pass through a series of proteins; the energy released pumps H⁺ from the matrix to the intermembrane space → a proton gradient.
  2. Chemiosmosis: protons flow back into the matrix through ATP synthase, and this flow drives ADP + Pi → ATP.
  3. Oxygen: the final electron acceptor; it combines with electrons and protons to form water. Without O₂ the whole process stops.

As electrons move along the chain, each carrier has a higher reduction potential (greater pull on electrons), so energy is released step by step: some as heat, most used to pump protons.

Memory hook: proton count “4-0-4-2”

NADH enters at I and passes I, III, IV: 4 + 4 + 2 = 10 H⁺. FADH₂ enters at II and passes III, IV: 4 + 2 = 6 H⁺. That is why FADH₂ makes less ATP.

Coenzyme Q and statins

  • CoQ (ubiquinone) is a quinone with a long hydrophobic tail, so it moves within the membrane.
  • It is made from an intermediate of cholesterol synthesis.
  • It links the flavoprotein dehydrogenases (Complexes I, II and others) to the cytochromes (Complex III).
  1. HMG-CoA reductase turns HMG-CoA into mevalonate.
  2. Mevalonate is used to make cholesterol and isoprenoids (FPP, GGPP), which are needed for CoQ.
  3. Statins block HMG-CoA reductase → less cholesterol, but also fewer isoprenoids → CoQ may fall.
  4. Less CoQ may impair energy production in muscle → one explanation for statin muscle pain, weakness and myopathy.

ATP synthase (Complex V)

ATP synthase uses the proton gradient to make ATP. It is a rotary motor with two parts:

F0F1
LocationEmbedded in the inner membrane; hydrophobicFaces the matrix; hydrophilic
JobProton channel: protonated and deprotonated as H⁺ flows from intermembrane space to matrix, which turns the ringCatalytic: shape changes make ATP from ADP + Pi
Subunitsa, b, c: a ring of 9–12 c subunits, one a and two bα₃β₃ hexamer + one each of γ, δ, ε

About 4 H⁺ flow back for every 1 ATP made (including the H⁺ used to bring Pi and ADP in).

Cyanide poisoning

  • Cyanide binds irreversibly to cytochrome a/a3, so electrons can’t reach O₂. Cells can’t use oxygen even though blood carries plenty: changes like tissue hypoxia.
  • Plasma half-life: 20–60 minutes.

Uncouplers

  • Normally electron transport and ATP synthesis are coupled: protons only re-enter through ATP synthase.
  • Uncoupling proteins (UCPs) in the inner membrane make channels that let H⁺ re-enter the matrix without making ATP; the energy is released as heat.
  • From the textbook: UCP1 (thermogenin) in brown fat keeps newborns warm. Chemical uncouplers such as 2,4-dinitrophenol and high-dose aspirin carry H⁺ across the membrane, causing fever and wasted energy.
Inhibitor vs uncoupler

An inhibitor stops electron flow, so O₂ use falls. An uncoupler lets electrons flow freely, so O₂ use rises, but ATP synthesis falls and heat is made.

Leigh syndrome

  • Subacute necrotizing encephalomyelopathy: a severe mitochondrial disease of infants and young children.
  • Caused by defects in oxidative phosphorylation, most often Complex I, Complex IV or ATP synthase.
  • Less ATP → cells switch to anaerobic metabolism → lactic acidosis.
  • High-energy tissues suffer most: brain, heart, lungs.

Watch out

Watch out: ATP per NADH

This lecture’s proton numbers (10 H⁺ per NADH, 6 per FADH₂, 4 H⁺ per ATP) give 2.5 ATP per NADH and 1.5 per FADH₂. Week 14 used the older values 3 and 2. Know both; if a question uses the TCA-cycle total of 12 ATP, it is using 3 and 2.

Watch out: “outside the cell”

Slide 16 says the a subunit passes protons between the c-ring and “outside the cell”. For mitochondria it is the intermembrane space (the slide text comes from a general description of ATP synthase).

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • Complex II pumps no protons.
  • Complex IV contains copper as well as heme.
  • Only the β subunits of F1 make ATP.
  • Protons are pumped out of the matrix into the intermembrane space, and flow back into the matrix through ATP synthase.
  • CoQ is made from the cholesterol pathway, so statins can lower it.
  • Nitrites help in cyanide poisoning by making methemoglobin.

Key terms

Oxidative phosphorylation
ATP synthesis driven by the proton gradient from electron transport.
Chemiosmosis
Protons flowing back through ATP synthase to make ATP.
Proton gradient
Higher H⁺ in the intermembrane space than in the matrix.
FMN
Flavin mononucleotide, first electron acceptor in Complex I.
Iron–sulfur cluster
Fe-S centre that carries electrons.
Coenzyme Q (ubiquinone)
Mobile lipid carrier from Complexes I/II to III.
Cytochrome
Heme protein whose Fe cycles between Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺.
Cytochrome c oxidase
Complex IV; reduces O₂ to water.
ATP synthase
Complex V; F0 channel + F1 catalytic head.
Uncoupler
Lets H⁺ back into the matrix without making ATP.
Thiocyanate
Less toxic, excretable product of cyanide detoxification.
Leigh syndrome
Infant mitochondrial disease with lactic acidosis.
