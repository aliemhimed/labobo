Nucleotide Metabolism, Part 1: Purines

How the body builds purine nucleotides from scratch, recycles them, turns them into deoxyribonucleotides for DNA, and breaks them down to uric acid, with the diseases of each step: Lesch–Nyhan, ADA deficiency, gout and tumour lysis syndrome.

Labobo topic: Biochem Wk 12: Purine Nucleotide Metabolism

In 60 seconds

  • Purines (A, G) have two rings; pyrimidines (C, T, U) have one. T = U + a methyl group.
  • Purine ring atoms come from aspartate, glycine, glutamine, CO₂ and N¹⁰-formyl-THF; most de novo synthesis is in the liver.
  • PRPP synthetase makes PRPP; GPAT catalyses the committed step. The pathway builds IMP, then AMP (needs GTP) and GMP (needs ATP).
  • Ribonucleotide reductase makes deoxyribonucleotides from NDPs; thioredoxin is reduced again by NADPH.
  • Salvage: HGPRT (hypoxanthine, guanine) and APRT (adenine). No HGPRT = Lesch–Nyhan.
  • No ADA → dATP builds up → blocks ribonucleotide reductase → SCID.
  • Gout: needle-shaped, negatively birefringent urate crystals; 90% underexcretion. Allopurinol/febuxostat block xanthine oxidase.

Bases, nucleosides and nucleotides

Purines and pyrimidines are nitrogen-containing ring structures (heterocycles). They are parts of the nucleotides in DNA and RNA, which store the cell’s information.

Nucleotides and their derivatives also act as energy sources (ATP), signal molecules (cAMP), regulators and carriers (NAD⁺, FAD, coenzyme A, UDP-glucose).

PurinesPyrimidines
RingsTwo (double ring)One
In DNAAdenine (A), guanine (G)Cytosine (C), thymine (T)
In RNAAdenine (A), guanine (G)Cytosine (C), uracil (U)

Thymine differs from uracil by one methyl group.

Nucleoside vs nucleotide

  • Nucleoside = base + sugar, joined by an N-glycosidic bond. With ribose → ribonucleoside; with 2-deoxyribose → deoxyribonucleoside.
  • Ribonucleosides of A, G, C, U: adenosine, guanosine, cytidine, uridine.
  • Nucleotide = nucleoside + one or more phosphates.

De novo purine synthesis

“De novo” = from scratch. The purine ring is built atom by atom on the ribose-phosphate, so the pathway makes nucleotides directly.

  • Ring atoms come from: aspartate, glycine, glutamine (amino acids), CO₂, and N¹⁰-formyltetrahydrofolate (N¹⁰-formyl-THF).
  • Mostly in the liver; bases and nucleosides then travel to other tissues in red blood cells. The brain also makes a lot.
  1. PRPP synthetase makes PRPP (5-phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate) from ATP + ribose 5-phosphate. Its gene is X-linked; it is activated by inorganic phosphate and inhibited by purine nucleotides.
  2. Committed step: the amide group of glutamine replaces the pyrophosphate on carbon 1 of PRPP, catalysed by glutamine:PRPP amidotransferase (GPAT).
  3. Further steps (slide diagram) add glycine, formyl groups from THF, CO₂, aspartate and another glutamine, closing both rings → inosine monophosphate (IMP), whose base is hypoxanthine.

The crossover

Making A uses GTP; making G uses ATP. This keeps the two in balance: plenty of one helps make the other.

Phosphorylation to NDP and NTP

  • NMP → NDP: specific nucleoside monophosphate kinases (e.g. adenylate kinase for AMP).
  • NDP → NTP: nucleoside diphosphate kinase, which works on a wide range of NDPs.

Deoxyribonucleotides

  • DNA needs 2′-deoxyribonucleotides. They are made from ribonucleoside diphosphates (ADP, GDP, CDP, UDP) by ribonucleotide reductase, during the S phase of the cell cycle.
  • The enzyme takes electrons from thioredoxin, which becomes oxidised.
  • Thioredoxin is reduced again by thioredoxin reductase (a selenoprotein), using NADPH + H⁺.

Salvage and degradation

Purines from breakdown of the cell’s own nucleic acids (and a little from food) can be recycled into new nucleotides: the salvage pathway. It costs far less energy than de novo synthesis, so it matters most in tissues like the brain.

The enzymes on the slide
EnzymeReaction
HGPRT (hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase)Hypoxanthine + PRPP → IMP; guanine + PRPP → GMP
APRT (adenine phosphoribosyltransferase)Adenine + PRPP → AMP
Adenosine kinaseAdenosine → AMP (adenosine is the only nucleoside phosphorylated directly)
ADA (adenosine deaminase)Adenosine → inosine (degradation)
PNP (purine nucleoside phosphorylase)Inosine → hypoxanthine; guanosine → guanine
XO (xanthine oxidase)Hypoxanthine → xanthine → uric acid

About 10% of the bases enter the excretion pathway: they are oxidised to xanthine and then to uric acid, which leaves in the urine.

  1. AMP → adenosine → (ADA) inosine → (PNP) hypoxanthine
  2. GMP → guanosine → (PNP) guanine → (guanase) xanthine
  3. Hypoxanthine → (XO) xanthine → (XO) uric acid → urine

Lesch–Nyhan syndrome

An inherited (from the textbook: X-linked recessive) defect of HGPRT, so purines cannot be salvaged.

  1. Hypoxanthine and guanine are not turned back into IMP and GMP → they become uric acid.
  2. PRPP is not used up, and low AMP, IMP and GMP remove the normal brakes on PRPP synthetase and GPAT.
  3. De novo purine synthesis rises → even more uric acid.
Clinical features
Usually no symptoms for the first 6 months
Orange, sand-like sodium urate crystals in the infant’s diapers
Developmental delay and cognitive impairment
Gouty arthritis, urate nephropathy, renal failure
Aggression and self-injury (biting lips and fingers)

Adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency

  • Rare, autosomal recessive, from mutations in the ADA gene. One of the major causes of severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID).
  1. No ADA → adenosine and deoxyadenosine are not broken down.
  2. Deoxyadenosine is phosphorylated → dATP builds up.
  3. dATP inhibits ribonucleotide reductase → no deoxyribonucleotides → DNA synthesis fails.
  4. Rapidly dividing lymphocytes cannot develop → T and B cells are missing.

Clinical features: failure to thrive despite good feeding; recurrent infections; chronic diarrhea; recurrent thrush. The absence of a thymic shadow on a chest X-ray (thymus missing or small) is a hallmark of SCID.

Gout and hyperuricemia

Gout is an acute inflammatory monoarthritis caused by monosodium urate crystals precipitating in a joint.

Risk factors and causes

  • Risk factors: male sex, hypertension, obesity, diabetes, dyslipidemia, alcohol. The strongest is hyperuricemia.
Cause of hyperuricemiaShareExamples
Underexcretion of uric acid90%Mostly idiopathic; worse with renal failure; some drugs (e.g. thiazide diuretics)
Overproduction of uric acid10%Lesch–Nyhan, PRPP excess, high cell turnover (tumour lysis syndrome), von Gierke disease

Clinical features

  • Asymmetric joints; joint swollen, red and painful.
  • Classic: painful MTP joint of the big toe (podagra).
  • Tophi (urate deposits), often on the external ear or Achilles tendon.
  • Attacks follow a large meal rich in purines (red meat, seafood), trauma, surgery, dehydration, diuresis, or alcohol (its metabolites compete with uric acid for secretion in the kidney).

Diagnosis

The gold standard is finding urate crystals in synovial fluid (needs arthrocentesis). The crystals are needle-shaped and negatively birefringent under polarised light: yellow when parallel to the light axis, blue when perpendicular.

In the clinic: tumour lysis syndrome (TLS)

A life-threatening emergency: rapid destruction of tumour cells releases K⁺, phosphate and uric acid into blood, causing acute kidney injury. Most common after starting chemotherapy for fast-growing, chemo-sensitive blood cancers: ALL, AML and high-grade non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

Watch out

Watch out: guanine to xanthine

Slide 14 says xanthine oxidase oxidises “hypoxanthine and guanine” to xanthine. In Lippincott, guanine is deaminated to xanthine by guanase; xanthine oxidase acts on hypoxanthine and then on xanthine (→ uric acid).

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • The committed step is GPAT, not PRPP synthetase (PRPP is used by other pathways too).
  • Ribonucleotide reductase works on diphosphates, not monophosphates.
  • Adenosine is the only nucleoside directly phosphorylated (adenosine kinase).
  • Most gout is from under-excretion, not overproduction.
  • Urate crystals are negatively birefringent.
  • ADA deficiency kills cells through dATP blocking ribonucleotide reductase.

Key terms

Purine / pyrimidine
Double-ring / single-ring nitrogenous base.
Nucleoside
Base + sugar (N-glycosidic bond).
Nucleotide
Nucleoside + phosphate(s).
De novo synthesis
Making nucleotides from small precursors.
PRPP
5-Phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate, the activated ribose.
IMP
Inosine monophosphate, the first purine nucleotide made; base hypoxanthine.
Ribonucleotide reductase
Makes deoxyribonucleotides from NDPs.
Salvage pathway
Recycling free bases into nucleotides with PRPP.
HGPRT
Salvages hypoxanthine and guanine; missing in Lesch–Nyhan.
Xanthine oxidase
Makes uric acid; blocked by allopurinol.
SCID
Severe combined immunodeficiency; ADA deficiency is a major cause.
Podagra
Gout of the big-toe MTP joint.
Tophus
Deposit of urate crystals in tissue.
Tumour lysis syndrome
Release of K⁺, phosphate and uric acid from dying tumour cells.
