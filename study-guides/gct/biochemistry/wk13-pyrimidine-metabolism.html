Nucleotide Metabolism, Part 2: Pyrimidines

How pyrimidines (U, C, T) are made and broken down, how dTMP is made with folate, what goes wrong in orotic aciduria and folate deficiency, and the cancer and immune drugs that block these steps.

Labobo topic: Biochem Wk 13: Pyrimidine Metabolism

In 60 seconds

  • We make about half a gram of pyrimidine nucleotides a day, mostly in the liver and dividing cells.
  • The ring is built first, then attached to PRPP (purines are built on PRPP).
  • Regulated step: CPS II (glutamine + CO₂ → carbamoyl phosphate), inhibited by UTP, activated by PRPP.
  • UMP synthase = orotate phosphoribosyltransferase + orotidylate decarboxylase; its defect is orotic aciduria, treated with uridine.
  • Thymidylate synthase makes dTMP from dUMP using N⁵,N¹⁰-methylene-THF, producing DHF; DHF reductase recycles it to THF.
  • Drugs: 5-FU (thymidylate synthase), methotrexate (DHF reductase), leflunomide (dihydroorotate dehydrogenase), mycophenolate, ribavirin (IMP dehydrogenase).
  • Pyrimidine breakdown gives β-alanine, β-aminoisobutyrate, CO₂ and NH₃, and no uric acid.

Overview

  • A pyrimidine nucleotide contains uracil (U), cytosine (C) or thymine (T) with ribose or deoxyribose (and phosphate).
  • We must make about 0.5 g of pyrimidine nucleotides per day.
  • Most synthesis happens in the liver and in dividing cells: intestinal epithelium, bone-marrow red cell precursors and tumour cells. This is why blocking it slows cancers.

Pyrimidine synthesis

The pyrimidine ring is made from glutamine, CO₂ and aspartate. Unlike purines, the ring is finished first and only then joined to ribose-phosphate (from PRPP).

  1. Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase II (CPS II): glutamine + CO₂ (+ 2 ATP) → carbamoyl phosphate. This is the regulated step: inhibited by UTP, activated by PRPP.
  2. Aspartate transcarbamoylase: adds aspartate → carbamoyl aspartate.
  3. Dihydroorotase: closes the ring → dihydroorotate.
  4. Dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (in the inner mitochondrial membrane): oxidises → orotate.
  5. Orotate phosphoribosyltransferase: adds ribose-phosphate from PRPP → OMP.
  6. Orotidylate decarboxylase: removes CO₂ → UMP.
  7. (Later) CTP synthetase: UTP → CTP.

Steps 5 and 6 are carried out by one bifunctional enzyme, UMP synthase. From the textbook: steps 1–3 are also one protein (CAD) in the cytosol.

CPS I vs CPS II

CPS II: cytosol, uses glutamine, makes pyrimidines. CPS I: mitochondria, uses ammonia, feeds the urea cycle, activated by N-acetylglutamate.

UTP and CTP

  • UMP → UDP by a nucleoside monophosphate kinase; UDP → UTP by nucleoside diphosphate kinase (phosphate from ATP).
  • CTP is made from UTP by CTP synthetase, driven by ATP hydrolysis (glutamine gives the amino group).
MoleculeUsed for
UTP → UDP-glucoseGlycogen synthesis; lactose synthesis; UDP-glucuronate, used in detoxification, including conjugation of bilirubin in the liver
CTPSynthesis of phospholipids: phosphatidylinositol, phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine, phosphatidylserine

Deoxyribonucleotides and dTMP

  • As for purines, ribonucleotide reductase turns NDPs into dNDPs. Thioredoxin reductases, which recharge it, contain selenocysteine in their active site.
  • dUMP can be formed by several routes (e.g. from dUDP or dCMP).
  1. Thymidylate synthase uses N⁵,N¹⁰-methylene-THF to methylate and reduce dUMP → dTMP.
  2. The folate is left as dihydrofolate (DHF).
  3. DHF reductase reduces DHF back to THF (using NADPH).
  4. THF is converted again to N⁵,N¹⁰-methylene-THF (folate = vitamin B9), ready for the next round.

This cycle is where 5-fluorouracil (at thymidylate synthase) and methotrexate (at DHF reductase) act.

Folate deficiency

  • Without enough folate (vitamin B9), too little THF is made.
  • Low THF blocks both purine and pyrimidine synthesis (THF gives carbons to purine rings and the methyl of thymine).
  • DNA synthesis slows and cell growth fails, hitting fast-dividing cells first. From the textbook: in the bone marrow this gives megaloblastic anemia; in pregnancy, folate deficiency raises the risk of neural tube defects.

Orotic aciduria

The slide is mostly a diagram; details from Lippincott:

  • A rare, autosomal recessive defect of UMP synthase (orotate phosphoribosyltransferase and/or orotidylate decarboxylase).
  • Orotic acid cannot become UMP → orotic acid builds up and appears in the urine (orotic acid crystals).
  • Too few pyrimidines → megaloblastic anemia that does not improve with vitamin B12 or folate; poor growth.
  • No hyperammonemia (this separates it from the urea-cycle defect ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency, which also raises orotic acid).
  • Treatment: uridine supplements. Uridine is salvaged to UMP, bypassing the block; UTP made from it also switches off CPS II, so less orotic acid is made.

Pyrimidine degradation

  • Mainly in the liver; the rings are opened into water-soluble products that are excreted or reused.
FromEnd products
Uracil and cytosineβ-alanine + CO₂ + NH₃
Thymineβ-aminoisobutyrate + CO₂ + NH₃

Unlike purines, pyrimidine breakdown makes no uric acid, so it never causes crystals.

In the clinic

These drugs hit every fast-dividing cell, not only tumours. That is why their side effects appear in the bone marrow (low blood counts), gut lining (mouth ulcers, diarrhea) and hair follicles.

Exam traps

Exam trap
  • CPS II is activated by PRPP and inhibited by UTP (not by CTP).
  • Thymidylate synthase turns its folate into DHF, which is why DHF reductase is needed.
  • Megaloblastic anemia that ignores B12 and folate points to orotic aciduria.
  • Leucovorin enhances 5-FU (but rescues normal cells from methotrexate).
  • Pyrimidine breakdown does not make uric acid.

Key terms

CPS II
Cytosolic carbamoyl phosphate synthetase; regulated step of pyrimidine synthesis.
Orotate
The first complete pyrimidine ring, before ribose is added.
UMP synthase
Bifunctional enzyme making UMP from orotate.
CTP synthetase
Makes CTP from UTP.
Thymidylate synthase
Makes dTMP from dUMP with methylene-THF.
DHF reductase
Regenerates THF from DHF; blocked by methotrexate.
THF
Tetrahydrofolate, active form of vitamin B9.
Orotic aciduria
UMP synthase defect: orotic acid in urine, megaloblastic anemia.
Megaloblastic anemia
Large, immature red cells from blocked DNA synthesis.
Prodrug
Inactive drug converted into the active form in the body (capecitabine → 5-FU).
Leucovorin
Folinic acid; enhances 5-FU.
