Thermodynamics II: Entropy, the Second Law and Gibbs Energy

Reversible and irreversible processes, heat engines and their efficiency, entropy as heat loss and as disorder, the second law, Gibbs free energy and spontaneity, and how these apply to ATP coupling, photosynthesis and protein or DNA denaturation.

Labobo topic: Biophys Lec 3: Thermodynamics II

In 60 seconds

  • In a reversible process every state is an equilibrium state; it is an idealisation. Natural processes are irreversible.
  • Heat engine: W = Qh − Qc; efficiency e = 1 − Qc/Qh; ideal (Carnot) e = 1 − Tc/Th, always < 1.
  • Entropy (Clausius): ΔS = Q/T (J/K). Statistically S = k ln W: spontaneous change goes from less to more probable (more disordered) arrangements.
  • Second law: heat flows hot → cold; some energy is always lost as heat; the entropy of the universe increases in natural processes; ΔS ≥ Q/T.
  • Gibbs energy G = H − TS; at constant T and p, ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; spontaneous when ΔG < 0.
  • ATP hydrolysis ΔG ≈ −7 kcal/mol drives glutamine synthesis (+3.4); photosynthesis ΔG = +686 kcal/mol, powered by light; helix–coil denaturation is spontaneous only at high T.

Review: first law and enthalpy

ΔU = Q − W. Isobaric: W = pΔV. Isothermal: ΔU = 0, Q = W. Isochoric: W = 0, ΔU = Q. Adiabatic: Q = 0, ΔU = −W. Enthalpy H = U + pV; at constant pressure ΔH = Q; ΔH < 0 exothermic, ΔH > 0 endothermic. (See Lecture 2.)

Reversibility and heat engines

A reversible process passes only through equilibrium states and could be run backwards; it is an idealisation. Most natural processes are irreversible.

A heat engine takes heat Qh from a hot reservoir, does work W and rejects Qc to a cold reservoir. Over a cycle ΔU = 0, so

W = Qh − Qc and e = W/Qh = 1 − Qc/Qh

The maximum (Carnot) efficiency is e = 1 − Tc/Th, always less than 1. A heat pump runs the cycle in reverse, using work to move heat from cold to hot.

Entropy

Rudolf Clausius (mid-1800s), puzzled by engines with only about 1% efficiency, quantified the heat that could not be turned back into work. He named it entropy (Greek tropē, transformation):

ΔS = Q/T (J/K), Q being heat irreversibly lost to the surroundings at absolute temperature T. Why he chose "S" is unknown (possibly for Sadi Carnot).

Entropy as disorder

Left alone, disordered arrangements are far more probable than ordered ones (fallen leaves are random, not a face). The more coins you toss, the less likely all heads becomes. Spontaneous change goes from less probable to more probable arrangements. Boltzmann: S = k ln W, with k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K and W proportional to the number of ways (probability) of a configuration.

The second law

  • Heat flows spontaneously from higher to lower temperature.
  • Converting energy into work always loses some energy as heat.
  • The entropy of an isolated system increases or stays constant; for an open system plus its surroundings the total entropy increases or stays constant.
  • Systems with their surroundings go from more ordered to less ordered states; the entropy of the universe increases in all natural processes.
  • In general ΔS ≥ Q/T.

The second law is a statement of what is most probable rather than what must be.

Watch out

Living things build order (lower their own entropy) without breaking the second law, because they are open systems that export more entropy (heat, waste) to the surroundings. The slide says "closed system"; strictly, the never-decreasing rule applies to an isolated system or to system + surroundings.

Gibbs free energy

About 10 years after Clausius, J. Willard Gibbs defined the energy available to do useful work:

G = H − TS = U + pV − TS

At constant temperature and pressure, ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. Because TΔS ≥ Q = ΔH, ΔG ≤ 0 for spontaneous processes.

Biological applications

ATP

  • Main cellular energy source; one glucose gives about 36 ATP, with over 50% efficiency (an internal combustion engine reaches 10–20%).
  • Cellular ATP is 1–10 mM; an adult turns over more than 70 kg of ATP per day.
  • Coupling: glutamine synthesis (ΔG = +3.4 kcal/mol) proceeds when coupled to ATP hydrolysis (ΔG = −7 kcal/mol), net ΔG ≈ −3.6 kcal/mol.

Photosynthesis

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂, ΔG = +686 kcal/mol, driven by light. Light reactions split water (protons → ATP, electrons → NADPH); dark reactions use ATP and NADPH to fix CO₂. About 100 reactions, 8 photons per carbon; photon absorption efficiency ~20%, overall efficiency ~5%.

Heat denaturation

For a helix → coil transition, ΔGcoil = ΔHcoil − TΔScoil. Breaking bonds costs energy (ΔH > 0) and the coil is more disordered (ΔS > 0), so ΔG becomes negative, and denaturation spontaneous, only at high temperature.

Key terms
