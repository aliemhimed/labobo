Heat, temperature and the zeroth law

Thermodynamics ("heat + force") studies heat flow and its conversion to work. Heat flows from a hotter to a colder body and can be turned into work. Temperature measures how hot or cold an object is.

Zeroth law: two bodies in thermal contact reach thermal equilibrium (equal temperature). A thermometer works because it comes to the same temperature as the object. T (K) = t (°C) + 273.15.

Heat is special: the laws of thermodynamics came from studying heat engines; heat is always available to measure; it reflects the kinetic energy of molecules; and it is the least organised form of energy, the least efficient for extracting useful work.

The joule is the energy to lift a 1 N weight by 1 m. The calorie raises 1 g of water from 14.5 °C to 15.5 °C at 1 atm. 1 cal = 4.184 J.

Kinetic theory of gases

Gas molecules move with a distribution of speeds that shifts to higher values as temperature rises. The average kinetic energy of one molecule is

Eav = 3/2 kT (k = Boltzmann constant, T absolute temperature)

and the internal energy of N molecules (ν moles) of an ideal monatomic gas is U = 3/2 NkT = 3/2 νRT, where R = k·NA. Temperature is therefore a measure of the mean molecular kinetic energy.

The first law and special processes

The first law is conservation of energy: the change in internal energy equals heat added minus work done by the system.

ΔU = Q − W, where Q > 0 when heat enters the system and W is work done by the system (W = pΔV for expansion).

ProcessConstantResult
IsobaricpW = pΔV; ΔU = Q − W
IsothermalTΔU = 0; Q = W
Isochoric (isovolumetric)VW = 0; ΔU = Q
AdiabaticQ = 0ΔU = −W
Watch out: sign convention

One slide says "W > 0 when work is done on the system", but the process equations use ΔU = Q − W, which only works if W is work done by the system. Use ΔU = Q − W with W = work by the system. Example: 500 J of heat in, 200 J of work out → ΔU = +300 J.

Enthalpy

Enthalpy is a state variable used to describe bond energies and heats of reaction:

H = U + pV, so ΔH = ΔU + pΔV + VΔp. Since ΔU = Q − pΔV, ΔH = Q + VΔp, and at constant pressure ΔH = Q.

Why not just use Q? Because H (like p, V and U) is a state variable and Q is not; under isobaric conditions the enthalpy change shows the heat flow of a process.

For A + B → C + D: ΔH = (HC + HD) − (HA + HB).

  • ΔH < 0: exothermic (heat released).
  • ΔH > 0: endothermic (heat absorbed).

Differential scanning microcalorimetry measures heat absorbed as a lipid bilayer is heated, revealing its phase transition.

Key terms

Thermal equilibrium: equal temperatures · Internal energy (U): total molecular energy · Heat (Q): energy transferred because of a temperature difference · Work (W): here pΔV · Heat capacity / specific heat: heat per degree / per degree per kg · Latent heat: heat of phase change · Isobaric / isothermal / isochoric / adiabatic: constant p / T / V / no heat · Enthalpy (H): U + pV · Exothermic / endothermic: releases / absorbs heat · State variable: depends only on the state, not the path.
