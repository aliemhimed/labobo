Fatty Acid and Triacylglycerol Synthesis

How excess carbohydrate becomes fat: getting acetyl-CoA into the cytosol, the committed step at acetyl-CoA carboxylase and its regulation, the seven cycles of fatty acid synthase, NADPH supply, elongation, desaturation, essential fatty acids and the assembly of triacylglycerol.

Labobo topic: Biochem Lec 4: Fatty Acid Synthesis

In 60 seconds

  • Lipogenesis: cytosol of liver, adipose tissue and lactating mammary gland; needs ATP, NADPH and fatty acid synthase; product palmitate (16:0). Insulin turns it on.
  • Acetyl-CoA leaves mitochondria as citrate; ATP citrate lyase releases it in the cytosol.
  • ACC (biotin, ATP, HCO₃⁻): acetyl-CoA → malonyl-CoA, the rate-limiting, committed step. Citrate activates (polymerisation), palmitoyl-CoA inhibits; phosphorylation (glucagon, epinephrine, AMPK) inhibits, insulin activates. Long-term: ChREBP, SREBP-1c, LXR.
  • FAS: dimer of identical chains, 7 activities + ACP (phosphopantetheine from B5). Condense (lose CO₂) → reduce → dehydrate → reduce; 7 cycles; thioesterase releases palmitate.
  • 14 NADPH per palmitate, from the pentose phosphate pathway and malic enzyme.
  • Elongation in the ER (on CoA); desaturation in the ER, mostly at Δ9, via cytochrome b₅. Linoleic and α-linolenic acids are essential.
  • TAG: glycerol 3-P → lysophosphatidic acid → phosphatidic acid → DAG → TAG (DGAT). Adipose lacks glycerol kinase, so it uses DHAP.

Functions and structure of fatty acids

Fatty acids give energy by β-oxidation (liver, muscle; hepatic oxidation also feeds ketogenesis), are stored as TAG in white adipose tissue, build phospholipids and glycolipids, anchor proteins to membranes and are precursors of prostaglandins.

  • A long hydrocarbon chain with a terminal carboxylate.
  • Saturated (no double bonds), monounsaturated (one), polyunsaturated (two or more); in humans most are saturated or monounsaturated.
  • Usually an even number of carbons (16, 18, 20). Very-long-chain (>22 C) matter in the brain.
  • Numbering starts at the carboxyl carbon (C1); next carbons are α, β, γ; the terminal methyl is ω.

Lipogenesis and the citrate shuttle

De novo synthesis turns acetyl-CoA into fatty acids, mainly in the liver and adipose tissue and in the lactating mammary gland. It rises with excess calories, especially carbohydrate (protein can contribute), happens in the cytosol and is stimulated by insulin in the fed state.

  1. In the fed state, high ATP and NADH inhibit isocitrate dehydrogenase, so citrate builds up in the matrix.
  2. Citrate leaves on the citrate carrier; ATP citrate lyase splits it into oxaloacetate + acetyl-CoA.
  3. Cytosolic acetyl-CoA feeds fatty acid synthesis, cholesterol synthesis and histone acetylation.
  4. OAA → malate (malate dehydrogenase) → pyruvate by malic enzyme, which makes NADPH; pyruvate returns to the matrix and PDH makes acetyl-CoA again.

Acetyl-CoA carboxylase and its regulation

ACC carboxylates acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA, using CO₂ from bicarbonate and ATP. Its coenzyme biotin (B7) is bound to a lysine; biotin is carboxylated first and passes the carboxyl to acetyl-CoA. This is the rate-limiting, first committed step.

In the clinic: metformin

Metformin activates AMPK, which phosphorylates and inhibits ACC and lowers ACC and FAS expression (via SREBP-1c), lowering plasma TAG. It lowers glucose by AMPK-driven GLUT4 uptake in muscle.

Long-term regulation

High carbohydrate and insulin increase transcription of ACC and FAS through ChREBP and SREBP-1c; LXR stimulates SREBP-1 transcription. Low-calorie or high-fat diets and many dietary PUFAs reduce lipogenesis.

Fructose drives lipogenesis

Fructose is quickly turned into DHAP (→ glycerol 3-phosphate, the TAG backbone) and acetyl-CoA, and it activates SREBP-1c and ChREBP.

Fatty acid synthase

Human FAS has two identical chains, each with seven catalytic activities and an acyl carrier protein (ACP), arranged head to tail. ACP carries a phosphopantetheine arm from coenzyme A (vitamin B5) with a free –SH.

  1. Acetyl group → cysteine –SH of one subunit; malonyl group → ACP –SH of the other.
  2. Condensation: C2 + C3 → C4 β-ketoacyl-ACP, releasing CO₂.
  3. Reduction (NADPH) → 3-hydroxyacyl-ACP.
  4. Dehydration → enoyl-ACP.
  5. Reduction (NADPH) → saturated acyl-ACP.
  6. Repeat: seven cycles in all give palmitoyl-ACP; thioesterase releases palmitate.
In the clinic: FASN and cancer

Many cancers overexpress FASN and keep making fatty acids for new membranes even when dietary fat is available; FASN is being studied as a drug target.

NADPH sources

One palmitate needs 14 NADPH (two per cycle × 7).

  • Pentose phosphate pathway: a major supplier.
  • Malic enzyme: malate → pyruvate + NADPH, linked to the citrate shuttle.

Elongation and desaturation

Elongation

  • Mainly in the ER by fatty acid elongase: two carbons from malonyl-CoA are added to the carboxyl end; the chain is on CoA, not ACP.
  • The brain can make VLCFA (>22 C) for myelin.
  • Mitochondria can elongate too, using acetyl-CoA with NADH and NADPH.

Desaturation (ER)

Most double bonds are put in between C9 and C10.

  1. NADH reduces the FAD of cytochrome b₅ reductase.
  2. It passes electrons one at a time to two cytochrome b₅ (Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺).
  3. Fatty acyl-CoA desaturase takes these 2 electrons plus 2 from the chain and reduces O₂ to 2 H₂O, forming the double bond.

Essential fatty acids

Humans cannot put double bonds near the ω end, so ω-3 and ω-6 fatty acids must come from food: linoleic acid (18:2 Δ9,12) and α-linolenic acid (18:3 Δ9,12,15). They can be elongated and desaturated; linoleic acid gives arachidonic acid, the eicosanoid precursor.

In the clinic: EFA deficiency

A dry, scaly dermatitis: the stratum corneum lipid barrier is defective, water is lost and skin flakes.

Triacylglycerol synthesis

Fatty acids are stored and transported as TAG. The three fatty acids usually differ: C1 is usually saturated, C2 unsaturated, C3 either. Fatty acids are first activated to fatty acyl-CoA by fatty acyl-CoA synthetases (thiokinases).

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • ACC needs biotin (B7); ACP needs pantothenate (B5).
  • Phosphorylation inhibits ACC.
  • Acetyl-CoA leaves the mitochondrion as citrate, not as acetylcarnitine.
  • The first condensation gives C4, so only seven cycles are needed, not eight.
  • Most desaturation needs NADH and O₂, not NADPH.
  • Fructose bypasses PFK-1 and feeds lipogenesis strongly.

Key terms

Lipogenesis: synthesis of TAG from glucose · Citrate carrier: moves citrate out of mitochondria · ATP citrate lyase: citrate → OAA + acetyl-CoA · Acetyl-CoA carboxylase: biotin enzyme making malonyl-CoA · Malonyl-CoA: C3 donor for synthesis; inhibits CPT-I · AMPK: energy sensor that inhibits ACC · SREBP-1c: insulin-driven lipogenic transcription factor · ChREBP: carbohydrate-responsive transcription factor · LXR: nuclear receptor inducing SREBP-1 · Fatty acid synthase: multifunctional dimer making palmitate · ACP: acyl carrier protein · Phosphopantetheine: B5-derived –SH arm · Thioesterase: releases palmitate · Malic enzyme: malate → pyruvate + NADPH · Elongase: ER enzyme adding 2 C · Desaturase: forms C=C using O₂ · Cytochrome b₅: electron carrier for desaturation · Essential fatty acid: must come from diet · Phosphatidic acid: shared TAG intermediate · DGAT: last enzyme of TAG synthesis.
