Fat Mobilization, Fatty Acid Oxidation and Ketone Bodies

How stored fat is released in fasting, how fatty acids enter mitochondria and are oxidised, the special routes for odd-chain, unsaturated, very-long-chain and branched fatty acids, the inherited disorders of each step, and how the liver makes ketone bodies for the rest of the body.

Labobo topic: Biochem Lec 5: Fat Mobilization, Fatty Acid Oxidation and Ketone Bodies

In 60 seconds

  • Lipolysis: ATGL (TAG → DAG), HSL (DAG → MAG), MGL (MAG → glycerol). PKA phosphorylates HSL and perilipin (freeing CGI-58 to activate ATGL). In humans epinephrine is the main trigger.
  • Activation on the outer mitochondrial membrane costs 2 ATP. Carnitine shuttle: CPT-I → translocase → CPT-II. Malonyl-CoA inhibits CPT-I.
  • β-oxidation: oxidation (FAD) → hydration → oxidation (NAD⁺) → thiolysis. Palmitate → 8 acetyl-CoA, 7 NADH, 7 FADH₂ → 106 ATP net.
  • Odd chains → propionyl-CoA → (biotin) → methylmalonyl-CoA → (B12 mutase) → succinyl-CoA: glucogenic.
  • Defects: carnitine deficiency, CPT-I (no cardiomyopathy), myopathic CPT-II (rhabdomyolysis), MCAD (commonest, C8 acylcarnitine, hypoketotic hypoglycaemia).
  • Peroxisomes: VLCFA (ABCD1, acyl-CoA oxidase makes H₂O₂), phytanic acid α-oxidation (Refsum), Zellweger.
  • Ketogenesis in liver mitochondria; rate-limiting mitochondrial HMG-CoA synthase. Liver lacks thiophorase, so it cannot use ketones.

Fat mobilisation

LipaseReaction
Adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL)TAG → DAG + FFA
Hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL)DAG → MAG + FFA
Monoacylglycerol lipase (MGL)MAG → glycerol + FFA

Signal

  1. Epinephrine (or glucagon) → G protein → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA.
  2. PKA phosphorylates HSL and perilipin; perilipin releases CGI-58, which activates ATGL.

In humans, catecholamines (β-receptors) are the main stimulus; glucagon has little direct effect on human adipocytes.

Fate of the products

  • FFAs travel on albumin to muscle and other tissues. RBCs cannot use them (no mitochondria); the brain uses very little.
  • Adipocytes lack glycerol kinase, so glycerol goes to the liver → glycerol 3-P → DHAP → glycolysis or gluconeogenesis, or TAG synthesis.

Activation and the carnitine shuttle

Long-chain FA enter cells via FAT, FABP and FATP and are activated by long-chain fatty acyl-CoA synthetase (thiokinase) on the outer mitochondrial membrane: FA + ATP → acyl-AMP + PPi; AMP is swapped for CoA; PPi is hydrolysed. Cost = 2 ATP.

  1. CPT-I: acyl-CoA + carnitine → acylcarnitine.
  2. Carnitine–acylcarnitine translocase carries it across the inner membrane.
  3. CPT-II rebuilds acyl-CoA in the matrix; carnitine returns.

Carnitine comes from the diet or is made from lysine (or methionine), starting in muscle and finishing in liver, with SAM and vitamin C. It is taken up by OCTN2 in heart, muscle and kidney.

Malonyl-CoA (from ACC2 in muscle, both ACC forms in liver) inhibits CPT-I, so new fat is not burned. Medium-chain FA (human milk) enter without CPT-I and are not inhibited.

For long chains, steps 2–4 are done by the mitochondrial trifunctional protein on the inner membrane.

Palmitate yield

7 spirals → 8 acetyl-CoA, 7 NADH, 7 FADH₂. 7 × 2.5 + 7 × 1.5 + 8 × 10 = 17.5 + 10.5 + 80 = 108, minus 2 for activation = 106 ATP. (The older calculation, with 3 and 2 ATP per NADH and FADH₂, gives a higher figure.)

Fatty acid oxidation gives about 80% of the heart’s energy and is vital in fasting, exercise and the newborn period.

Odd-chain fatty acids

The last thiolysis leaves propionyl-CoA, which also comes from bile acid synthesis and from valine, isoleucine, methionine and threonine.

  1. Propionyl-CoA carboxylase (ATP, biotin) → D-methylmalonyl-CoA.
  2. Racemase → L-methylmalonyl-CoA.
  3. Methylmalonyl-CoA mutase (deoxyadenosylcobalamin, B12) → succinyl-CoA → TCA → OAA → glucose.
In the clinic: methylmalonic acidaemia

Mutase or B12-metabolism defect: neuropathy, developmental delay, hypotonia, metabolic acidosis and recurrent crises. Restrict the propionyl-CoA sources (valine, odd-chain FA, methionine, isoleucine, threonine).

Fatty acids and gluconeogenesis

Acetyl-CoA from even-chain FA is not glucogenic: PDH is irreversible, and in the TCA cycle two CO₂ are lost for each acetyl-CoA, so there is no net OAA. But FA oxidation supports gluconeogenesis by supplying ATP/GTP and NADH, and its acetyl-CoA activates pyruvate carboxylase.

Carnitine, CPT and MCAD defects

All give hypoketotic hypoglycaemia in fasting: little acetyl-CoA for ketones, and gluconeogenesis lacks energy and pyruvate carboxylase activation.

ω-Oxidation and unsaturated fatty acids

ω-Oxidation works in the ER of liver and kidney when β-oxidation is blocked or medium-chain load is high. It starts at the ω (methyl) carbon: hydroxylation → aldehyde → carboxylic acid, giving a dicarboxylic acid.

Double bondExampleExtra enzymes
Odd positionOleic acid 18:1 Δ9Enoyl-CoA isomerase (cis-Δ³ → trans-Δ²)
Even positionLinoleic acid 18:2 Δ9,12Isomerase + NADPH-dependent 2,4-dienoyl-CoA reductase

Unsaturated FA yield less energy: fewer FADH₂ (and sometimes NADH) are made.

Peroxisomal oxidation

Very-long-chain fatty acids (≥22 C)

  1. Activated to acyl-CoA; imported by ABCD1 (adrenoleukodystrophy protein).
  2. Acyl-CoA oxidase (FAD) passes electrons directly to O₂ → H₂O₂, no ATP; catalase removes H₂O₂.
  3. Hydration, dehydrogenation (NADH), thiolysis, repeated until C8–C10, then sent to mitochondria via carnitine.

Phytanic acid: α-oxidation

A methyl group on the β-carbon blocks acyl-CoA dehydrogenase. Peroxisomes: phytanoyl-CoA ligase → phytanoyl-CoA α-hydroxylase (PhyH) → lyase removes C-1 as CO₂ → pristanal → pristanic acid → peroxisomal β-oxidation.

In the clinic: Refsum and Zellweger

Refsum disease (autosomal recessive, PhyH deficiency): retinitis pigmentosa, peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar ataxia; also anosmia, deafness, ichthyosis, short 4th toe, conduction defects. Restrict phytanic acid, avoid rapid weight loss; plasmapheresis in crises. Zellweger syndrome is a peroxisome biogenesis disorder: proteins are not targeted to peroxisomes, so VLCFA build up (Lippincott). An ABCD1 defect gives X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy.

Ketone bodies

Made when insulin:glucagon is low (fasting, uncontrolled type 1 diabetes, long exercise). Lipolysis floods the liver with FA; high acetyl-CoA inhibits PDH and activates pyruvate carboxylase; high NADH turns OAA into malate. With little OAA, acetyl-CoA goes to ketones (ketogenic amino acids such as leucine and lysine add more).

  1. Thiolase: 2 acetyl-CoA → acetoacetyl-CoA.
  2. Mitochondrial HMG-CoA synthase (rate-limiting) + acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA.
  3. HMG-CoA lyase → acetoacetate + acetyl-CoA.
  4. Acetoacetate → β-hydroxybutyrate (NADH) or spontaneously → acetone (volatile).

Extrahepatic tissues (muscle, heart, brain in starvation) turn ketones back to acetyl-CoA. The liver lacks thiophorase (succinyl-CoA:3-ketoacid CoA transferase), so it exports all ketones. In prolonged starvation the brain gets >50% of its energy from ketones, which inhibit PDH and glycolysis in the brain and spare muscle protein.

In the clinic: diabetic and alcoholic ketoacidosis

Ketone bodies are acids (pKa about 4). In uncontrolled type 1 diabetes, ketonaemia and ketonuria plus urinary glucose loss cause dehydration, concentrating the H⁺ → diabetic ketoacidosis, with fruity acetone breath. Ketoacidosis also occurs in prolonged fasting and heavy ethanol use (Lippincott p. 556).

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • FA oxidation defects cause hypoketotic hypoglycaemia; DKA is hyperketotic.
  • The liver makes ketones but cannot use them (no thiophorase); RBCs cannot use them either.
  • Even-chain FA give no net glucose; odd-chain FA and glycerol can.
  • In humans epinephrine, not glucagon, is the main lipolytic hormone.
  • Medium-chain FA do not need CPT-I, so malonyl-CoA does not block them.
  • Acetone is not metabolised; it is breathed out.

Key terms

ATGL / HSL / MGL: the three lipases of lipolysis · Perilipin: lipid droplet coat protein · CGI-58: ATGL activator · Thiokinase: fatty acyl-CoA synthetase · Carnitine shuttle: CPT-I, translocase, CPT-II · OCTN2: carnitine transporter · ETF: carries FADH₂ electrons to CoQ · Trifunctional protein: does steps 2–4 for long chains · Propionyl-CoA: end product of odd-chain oxidation · Methylmalonic acidaemia: mutase or B12 defect · MCAD: medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase · Reye-like episode: encephalopathy with fatty liver · ω-Oxidation: ER route forming dicarboxylic acids · Enoyl-CoA isomerase: handles odd-position double bonds · ABCD1: peroxisomal VLCFA importer · Phytanic acid: branched FA needing α-oxidation · Refsum disease: PhyH deficiency · Zellweger syndrome: peroxisome biogenesis disorder · HMG-CoA synthase: rate-limiting ketogenic enzyme · Thiophorase: reactivates acetoacetate; absent in liver.
