Cholesterol and Lipoprotein Metabolism

How cholesterol is built from acetyl-CoA and how its synthesis is controlled, how lipoproteins carry lipids between gut, liver and tissues, how LDL and HDL handle cholesterol, and the inherited hyperlipidaemias, abetalipoproteinaemia and the drugs that treat them.

Labobo topic: Biochem Lec 6: Cholesterol and Lipoprotein Metabolism

In 60 seconds

  • Cholesterol: 27 C, OH on C3, one double bond C5–C6. Absorbed by NPC1L1 (ezetimibe); plant sterols pumped back by ABCG5/G8 (sitosterolemia).
  • Synthesis (mainly liver): 3 acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA (cytosolic HMG-CoA synthase) → mevalonate (HMG-CoA reductase, rate-limiting, SER) → IPP (C5) → GPP (C10) → FPP (C15) → squalene (C30) → lanosterol → cholesterol. SLOS = DHCR7.
  • Control: low sterol → SREBP-2/SCAP to Golgi → ↑ reductase + LDL receptors; high sterol → INSIG retention and reductase degradation; AMPK phosphorylation inactivates, insulin activates.
  • Chylomicrons (apoB-48, ~90% TAG) → LPL (apoC-II) → remnants (apoE). VLDL (apoB-100) → IDL → LDL (hepatic lipase). LDL carries ~70% of plasma cholesterol.
  • LDL receptor binds apoB-100/apoE; PCSK9 sends receptors to degradation. HDL: ABCA1 loads apoA-I, LCAT esterifies, CETP swaps CE for TAG, SR-BI unloads CE in liver.
  • Type I (LPL/apoC-II), IIa/IIb (LDLR/apoB-100), III (apoE2/E2), IV (VLDL overproduction), V (chylomicrons + VLDL). Abetalipoproteinaemia = MTTP.

Cholesterol structure and sources

Cholesterol is an amphipathic sterol that controls membrane fluidity and is the precursor of steroid hormones, bile acids and vitamin D. High plasma (especially LDL) cholesterol raises atherosclerosis risk.

  • 386 Da, 27 carbons: 17 in rings A–D, 2 angular methyls (AB and CD junctions), 8 in the side chain.
  • OH on C3 (ring A); one double bond, C5–C6 (ring B).
  • Comes from synthesis and diet; intestinal uptake by NPC1L1 (blocked by ezetimibe).
  • Plant sterols (β-sitosterol) are absorbed ~5% (vs ~40% for cholesterol) and pumped back by ABCG5/ABCG8; defects cause sitosterolemia. Plant sterols also lower cholesterol absorption.

Cholesterol synthesis

Mainly in the liver (also intestine, adrenal cortex, gonads), from acetyl-CoA (β-oxidation, PDH, ketogenic amino acids: leucine only ketogenic, isoleucine both) with NADPH from the pentose phosphate pathway.

In the clinic
  • Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase and may lower CoQ10, proposed to contribute to myalgia (rarely rhabdomyolysis); evidence for CoQ10 supplements is inconclusive.
  • Progeria (HGPS): LMNA mutation → farnesylated progerin stuck to the nuclear envelope; lonafarnib (farnesyltransferase inhibitor) is approved.
  • Smith–Lemli–Opitz: autosomal recessive DHCR7 deficiency; low cholesterol, high 7-DHC; microcephaly, ptosis, micrognathia, 2–3 toe syndactyly, genital and heart defects.

Lipoproteins and apolipoproteins

Spherical lipid–protein particles: chylomicrons, VLDL, LDL, HDL. Density rises with protein:lipid ratio; HDL is the smallest and densest. Apolipoproteins give structure, bind receptors and regulate enzymes.

Chylomicrons

Made by enterocytes to carry dietary lipids and fat-soluble vitamins; about 90% TAG. ApoB-48 is made in the RER and glycosylated in ER and Golgi.

  • LPL (activated by apoC-II) in capillaries of muscle, heart and adipose hydrolyses TAG; remnants are taken up by the liver via apoE.
  • Fed state: insulin raises adipose LPL (storage). Fasting: muscle LPL is favoured. The heart keeps high LPL throughout.

VLDL, IDL and LDL

  1. Liver secretes VLDL with apoB-100; it gains apoC-II and apoE from HDL.
  2. LPL removes TAG → IDL; apoC-II returns to HDL; IDL keeps apoB-100 and apoE.
  3. IDL is either taken up by the liver (apoE) or loses more TAG by hepatic lipase → LDL (apoB-100 only).

CETP swaps TAG from VLDL for cholesteryl esters from HDL. Lipoprotein metabolism forms an exogenous and an endogenous cycle, both centred on the liver.

The LDL receptor and PCSK9

LDL carries about 70% of plasma cholesterol. The LDL receptor, on all cells, binds apoB-100 and apoE lipoproteins; the number of receptors sets the LDL level.

  1. LDL binds the receptor and is endocytosed.
  2. In the endosome they separate: the receptor recycles, LDL goes to lysosomes and releases cholesterol.
  3. High cell cholesterol inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, lowers LDL receptor expression (SREBP-2) and is stored by ACAT as CE.

PCSK9, a liver serine protease in plasma, binds the receptor and, after internalisation, sends it to degradation instead of recycling. PCSK9 inhibitors: alirocumab, evolocumab.

HDL and reverse cholesterol transport

  • Nascent HDL: disc-shaped, mainly phospholipid with apoA, C and E; made by adding lipid to apoA-I from liver and intestine. HDL is a reservoir of exchangeable apolipoproteins.
  • ABCA1 uses ATP to move cholesterol and phospholipid from cells to apoA-I.
  • LCAT (liver-made, activated by apoA-I) moves a fatty acid from lecithin to cholesterol → CE + lysolecithin; CE moves to the core.
  • Discoidal HDL → HDL3 → CE-rich HDL2; hepatic lipase shrinks HDL2 back to HDL3.
  • Delivery to liver: indirect (CETP to apoB lipoproteins) or direct (SR-BI selective CE uptake; HDL is released and reused).

HDL protects by reverse cholesterol transport to the liver for bile. Higher HDL goes with lower risk, but HDL alone does not set risk. Exercise and estrogen raise HDL.

Lipoprotein(a) and atherosclerosis

Lp(a) is LDL with apo(a) linked to apoB-100. Apo(a) resembles plasminogen but cannot become plasmin, so it competes with plasminogen and may inhibit fibrinolysis. High Lp(a) is a largely genetic, independent risk factor little changed by lifestyle; niacin lowers it but is not routinely recommended.

In the clinic: foam cells

In familial hypercholesterolaemia, pooled LDL is oxidised. Macrophage scavenger receptors (SR-A) take up oxidised LDL and, unlike the LDL receptor, are not down-regulated by cell cholesterol, so CE accumulates and macrophages become foam cells in plaques (Lippincott p. 647).

Abetalipoproteinaemia and the hyperlipidaemias

Abetalipoproteinaemia: MTTP mutation, no chylomicrons or VLDL; very low apoB-48, apoB-100, cholesterol and TAG. From infancy: steatorrhoea, failure to thrive, acanthocytes, neuropathy, ataxia; vitamin A and E loss → retinal degeneration and night blindness.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • Cytosolic HMG-CoA synthase is for cholesterol; mitochondrial is for ketones.
  • Phosphorylated HMG-CoA reductase is inactive.
  • ApoB-48 comes from RNA editing, not a separate gene.
  • Type I gives huge TAG but no premature atherosclerosis.
  • LDL receptor recycles; PCSK9 stops that.
  • GPP has 10 carbons (a favourite past-paper question).

Key terms

HMG-CoA reductase: rate-limiting enzyme, statin target · Mevalonate: its product · IPP: C5 isoprene unit · Farnesyl-PP: C15 intermediate and prenyl donor · Squalene: C30 linear precursor · Lanosterol: first sterol · SREBP-2: sterol-regulated transcription factor · SCAP / INSIG: sterol-sensing escort / ER retention proteins · NPC1L1: intestinal cholesterol importer · ABCG5/G8: plant sterol exporters · ApoB-48: edited intestinal apoB · LPL: capillary TAG lipase · Hepatic lipase: converts IDL → LDL · CETP: swaps CE and TAG · LDL receptor: binds apoB-100/apoE · PCSK9: degrades LDL receptors · ABCA1: loads cholesterol onto apoA-I · LCAT: esterifies cholesterol on HDL · SR-BI: hepatic HDL receptor · Lp(a): LDL with apo(a) · MTTP: loads lipid onto apoB.
