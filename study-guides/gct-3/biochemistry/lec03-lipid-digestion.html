Digestion and Absorption of Dietary Lipids

How dietary fat is broken down from the tongue to the jejunum, how bile acids are made, recycled and regulated, how lipids are absorbed and rebuilt into chylomicrons, how tissues use them, and why cystic fibrosis causes fat malabsorption.

Labobo topic: Biochem Lec 3: Digestion of Lipids

In 60 seconds

  • More than 95% of dietary lipid is TAG. Lingual and gastric lipases act on short- and medium-chain fatty acids (milk fat); most digestion is in the small intestine.
  • CCK (I cells) → pancreatic enzymes and gallbladder contraction; secretin (S cells) → HCO₃⁻.
  • Bile acids (C24) are made from cholesterol; rate-limiting CYP7A1 (7α-hydroxylase). Conjugated with glycine/taurine → bile salts; bacteria make secondary bile salts.
  • >95% reabsorbed (enterohepatic circulation): BSEP (liver → bile), ASBT (ileum in), OSTα/β (to portal blood). Ileal FXR → FGF19 shuts down synthesis.
  • Pancreatic lipase cuts sn-1 and sn-3 → 2 FFA + 2-MAG; needs colipase (activated by trypsin). Orlistat blocks it.
  • Mixed micelles carry long-chain FA, 2-MAG, cholesterol and vitamins A, D, E, K. Short/medium FA go straight to the portal vein.
  • Enterocyte SER rebuilds TAG (MGAT, DGAT) and CE (ACAT) → chylomicrons (apoB-48) → lacteals → thoracic duct. LPL (apoC-II) releases FA in tissues.

Digestion in the stomach

Dietary lipid is over 95% triacylglycerol, plus cholesterol, cholesteryl esters, phospholipids and free fatty acids.

  • Lingual and gastric lipases (back of tongue, stomach) prefer TAGs with short- and medium-chain fatty acids (≤12 C), which milk fat carries mostly at the 3-position; so they matter most in infants.
  • Parietal cells: carbonic anhydrase makes H⁺ + HCO₃⁻; H⁺/K⁺-ATPase secretes H⁺; HCO₃⁻ leaves basolaterally in exchange for Cl⁻, and Cl⁻ exits through apical channels.

H₂ blockers (cimetidine) block histamine; PPIs (omeprazole) inhibit the H⁺/K⁺-ATPase itself.

Bile and its functions

Bile is made in the liver, stored and concentrated in the gallbladder and released mainly in response to CCK.

  • Emulsification: bile salts break fat globules into small droplets, giving lipase more surface.
  • Mixed micelles: carry fatty acids, 2-MAG, cholesterol and vitamins A, D, E, K to the brush border.
  • Excretion: bilirubin, excess cholesterol, drugs and toxins.

Bile salt-stimulated lipase (carboxyl ester lipase) in human milk helps infants, whose pancreatic and bile digestion is immature.

Bile acid synthesis and modification

Bile acids (24 C, 2–3 OH groups) are made in the liver from cholesterol by hydroxylating the ring and cleaving the side chain. The first, rate-limiting step adds a 7α-OH: cholesterol 7α-hydroxylase (CYP7A1), inhibited by rising bile salts.

PathwayShareStartProducts
Classical~90%CYP7A1 (C-7)With CYP8B1 (12α-OH) → cholic acid; without → chenodeoxycholic acid
Alternative (acidic)~10%CYP27A1, then CYP7B1Mainly chenodeoxycholic acid
  • Conjugation with glycine or taurine makes bile salts more water-soluble.
  • Gut bacteria deconjugate and remove the 7-OH → secondary bile salts, less soluble and less reabsorbed.
  • Bile acids activate FXR in the terminal ileum → FGF19 → suppresses hepatic synthesis.
  • Lithocholic acid is hydrophobic and hepatotoxic; detoxified mainly by sulfation (also glucuronidation, hydroxylation).

Enterohepatic circulation and gallstones

Over 95% of bile salts are reabsorbed. Lithocholic acid is poorly absorbed.

TransporterWhereJob

OSTα–OSTβIleal enterocyte, basolateralExport to portal blood
In the clinic: cholestyramine

Binds bile salts in the gut so they are lost in stool; CYP7A1 is no longer inhibited and the liver uses more cholesterol to make bile acids, lowering plasma cholesterol. It also binds thyroid hormones in the gut and can be added to antithyroid drugs in severe thyrotoxicosis.

In the clinic: cholesterol gallstones

Cholesterol must be secreted with phospholipid and bile salts; if there is more cholesterol than they can keep in solution, it precipitates. Treatment of choice is laparoscopic cholecystectomy; for non-surgical patients, oral chenodeoxycholic acid dissolves stones over months to years.

Pancreatic enzymes

Pancreatic juice is alkaline: its HCO₃⁻ neutralises gastric acid.

In the clinic: orlistat and pancreatitis

Orlistat, a lipstatin derivative, binds the active-site serine of gastric and pancreatic lipase, so fat passes undigested. In acute pancreatitis, injured acinar cells leak lipase and serum lipase rises.

Absorption and resynthesis

  • Glycerol and FA shorter than 14 C go straight into the portal vein (on albumin) to the liver, without re-esterification.
  • Long-chain FA, free cholesterol and 2-MAG form mixed micelles with bile salts and fat-soluble vitamins.
  • Uptake: FATP4 (fatty acids, intestine) and NPC1L1 (cholesterol), blocked by ezetimibe.

Chylomicron secretion and use

  • Chylomicrons enter lacteals → lymph → thoracic duct → left subclavian vein.
  • In blood, nascent chylomicrons gain apoC-II and apoE from HDL.
  • LPL on capillaries of muscle, heart and adipose tissue, activated by apoC-II, hydrolyses TAG. Muscle oxidises the FA; adipose re-esterifies and stores them; glycerol goes to the liver.
  • Remnants are taken up by the liver through apoE-dependent receptors.
Watch out: heparin and LPL

LPL is held on the endothelium by proteoglycans. Heparin binds LPL and dislodges it from the capillary wall, so LPL activity at the wall is lost.

Cystic fibrosis

Defect in CFTR on chromosome 7, most often deletion of Phe508. CFTR secretes Cl⁻ in lungs and gut (water follows, hydrating mucus) but reabsorbs Cl⁻ in sweat ducts, so CF sweat is salty.

SystemEffect
LungsThick mucus, Pseudomonas and S. aureus infections, bronchitis, clubbing
GIBlocked bile and pancreatic flow: chronic pancreatitis, CF diabetes, fat malabsorption, loss of A, D, E, K, steatorrhoea, meconium ileus
Reproduction~95% of men infertile from absent vas deferens (spermatogenesis preserved); thick cervical mucus in women
MetabolicSalt loss → RAAS → contraction alkalosis and hypokalaemia

Diagnosis: sweat chloride test (pilocarpine); newborn screening with immunoreactive trypsinogen. Treatment: supportive plus CFTR modulators — lumacaftor corrects folding, ivacaftor opens the channel.

Exam traps

Exam traps
  • CFTR reabsorbs Cl⁻ in sweat ducts but secretes it in lungs and gut.
  • Short- and medium-chain fatty acids skip chylomicrons and go to the portal vein.
  • Colipase is activated by trypsin, not bile salts.
  • Chylomicrons enter the blood via the left subclavian vein, not the portal vein.
  • Cholestyramine lowers cholesterol by removing CYP7A1 inhibition, not by blocking absorption.

Key terms

Emulsification: breaking fat into small droplets · Mixed micelle: bile salt aggregate carrying digested lipids · CYP7A1: rate-limiting enzyme of bile acid synthesis · Bile salt: glycine- or taurine-conjugated bile acid · Secondary bile acid: bacterially modified, lacks 7-OH · FXR: nuclear bile acid receptor · FGF19: ileal hormone that suppresses bile acid synthesis · Enterohepatic circulation: reabsorption and reuse of bile salts · ASBT: ileal bile salt transporter · BSEP: hepatic bile salt export pump · Colipase: anchors lipase to droplets · 2-MAG: 2-monoacylglycerol · Orlistat: lipase inhibitor · NPC1L1: cholesterol uptake transporter · MGAT/DGAT: TAG-rebuilding acyltransferases · ACAT: intracellular cholesterol esterifier · Chylomicron: dietary TAG lipoprotein with apoB-48 · Lacteal: intestinal lymph vessel · CFTR: cAMP-regulated Cl⁻ channel · Meconium ileus: newborn bowel obstruction by thick meconium.
