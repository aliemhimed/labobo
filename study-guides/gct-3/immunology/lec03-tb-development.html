Receptor Diversity and T and B Cell Development

How a few hundred gene segments make millions of antigen receptors by V(D)J recombination, the structure of the BCR and TCR, the stages of B-cell development in the marrow, and how thymocytes are positively and negatively selected to give a self-MHC-restricted, self-tolerant T-cell repertoire.

Labobo topic: Immuno Lec 3: Receptor Diversity and T and B Cell Development

In 60 seconds

  • Development = commitment, proliferation, ordered receptor gene rearrangement, selection, and differentiation into subsets.
  • V(D)J recombination: synapsis → cleavage by RAG1/RAG2 → hairpin opening by ARTEMIS, nucleotide addition by TdT → joining (DNA ligase IV). It happens before antigen is ever met.
  • D segments exist only in Ig heavy chain and TCR β and δ loci. Junctional diversity contributes most to diversity.
  • B cells: pro-B (CD19, CD10; heavy chain rearranges) → pre-B (μ + surrogate light chain = pre-BCR, first checkpoint) → immature B (IgM) → transitional → mature (IgM + IgD) in the spleen.
  • T cells: DN (CD4⁻CD8⁻) → DP (CD4⁺CD8⁺) → SP. Weak self-MHC binding = positive selection; no binding = death by neglect; strong binding = negative selection or Treg.
  • Class I recognition → CD8; class II → CD4. AIRE in mTECs shows tissue antigens; its loss causes APS-1.

BCR and TCR structure

Each clone has a receptor with a unique antigen-binding structure, and an individual has about 10⁷–10⁹ clones.

B-cell receptor (membrane Ig)T-cell receptor
ChainsFour: two identical heavy + two identical light chains, Y-shaped, joined by disulfide bondsTwo: α and β (or γ and δ)
RecognisesUnprocessed antigens of almost any chemistry (protein, polysaccharide, lipid)Only peptides bound to MHC
Secreted?Yes, as antibodyNever; always membrane-bound
SignallingIgα and IgβCD3

The antigen-binding site is at the N-terminal ends of the variable (V) regions; its unique shape is the idiotype. Within V regions, hypervariable loops called complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) contact antigen, while framework regions support them. Most T cells use the αβ TCR; γδ T cells in epithelia can recognise peptide and non-peptide antigens.

Gene loci and V(D)J recombination

Each Ig locus has, from 5′ to 3′: many V segments (about 300 bp each), D segments (heavy chain only), J segments (30–50 bp), then C region genes. TCR loci are similar, with D segments only in β and δ. The human β locus has about 50 V, 2 D and 12 J segments; the α locus about 45 V and 50 J.

  • Light chain (κ or λ) V domain = rearranged VJ.
  • Heavy chain V domain = rearranged VDJ.
StepWhat happens
1. SynapsisTwo distant coding segments are brought together by chromosomal looping.
2. CleavageThe lymphocyte-specific V(D)J recombinase (RAG1 + RAG2) makes double-stranded breaks. RAG genes are expressed only in developing B and T cells.
3. Hairpin opening and end processingARTEMIS (endonuclease) opens the coding-end hairpins; TdT adds nucleotides to the broken ends.
4. JoiningThe coding ends are ligated (DNA ligase IV).
Watch out

Rearrangement is independent of antigen; receptors exist before antigen is encountered, exactly as clonal selection predicts. It occurs only at antigen-receptor genes.

Where diversity comes from

  1. Combinatorial diversity: random combinations of V, D and J segments (and of heavy with light, or α with β, chains).
  2. Junctional diversity: addition (TdT, N nucleotides) and removal of nucleotides at the V–D, D–J or V–J joints. This makes the largest contribution.

B-cell development

Most B cells arise from adult bone-marrow progenitors that initially make no Ig. Development from a lymphoid progenitor takes about 2–3 days.

StageKey features
Pro-BEarliest committed cell; no Ig; expresses CD19 and CD10; RAG1/RAG2 first expressed; heavy chain rearranges first.
Pre-BMakes μ heavy chain; light chains not yet rearranged. μ + surrogate light chain + Igα/Igβ = pre-BCR. Its signals give survival and the biggest proliferative burst; RAG is briefly switched off. This is the first checkpoint.
Immature BFirst cell with surface IgM; does not respond to antigen by proliferating. High-avidity self recognition in the marrow → receptor editing or death (negative selection).
Transitional BIgM⁺ cells leaving marrow; self-reactive ones are removed in the spleen.
Mature naive BIgM + IgD; subsets: follicular, marginal-zone and B-1 cells.

T-cell development in the thymus

The thymus is the main site of T-cell maturation. It involutes with age, but memory T cells live a very long time (perhaps over 20 years), so the need for new T cells falls with age.

StageFeatures
Double-negative (DN)Recent arrivals; germline TCR genes; no TCR, CD3, CD4 or CD8. Pro-T cells begin rearranging; RAG first expressed.
Double-positive (DP)CD4⁺CD8⁺ in the cortex; the complete αβ TCR is assembled here.
Single-positive (SP)CD4⁺ or CD8⁺; move to the medulla and leave as naive T cells.

Positive and negative selection

TCR binding to self peptide–self MHCFate
NoneApoptosis: death by neglect
Weak (low avidity)Survive: positive selection → CD4 or CD8 single-positive
Strong (high avidity)Apoptosis: negative selection (deletion), or diversion to regulatory T cells

Recognising self class I MHC makes a cell CD8⁺CD4⁻; recognising class II makes it CD4⁺CD8⁻. Negative selection happens in DP cells in the cortex and new SP cells in the medulla.

AIRE (autoimmune regulator) is a transcription factor in medullary thymic epithelial cells that switches on many tissue-restricted self antigens, so T cells reactive to them can be deleted. Self-tolerance created in the primary organs is central tolerance; tolerance induced in mature cells in the periphery is peripheral tolerance.

In the clinic: APS-1

AIRE mutations let self-reactive T cells escape, causing autoimmune polyendocrine syndrome type 1: chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis, hypoparathyroidism and adrenal insufficiency. Lack of RAG or ARTEMIS stops V(D)J recombination and causes severe combined immunodeficiency.

Key terms

Idiotype: unique antigen-binding structure · CDR: hypervariable loop forming the binding site · Framework region: conserved support for CDRs · V(D)J recombination: joining of gene segments · RAG1/RAG2: recombinase · ARTEMIS: hairpin-opening endonuclease · TdT: adds N nucleotides · Junctional diversity: changes at joints · Pre-BCR: μ + surrogate light chain · Receptor editing: new light-chain rearrangement in self-reactive B cells · DN / DP / SP: thymocyte stages · Death by neglect: no self-MHC recognition · AIRE: drives tissue antigens in mTECs · Central tolerance: tolerance made in primary organs.
